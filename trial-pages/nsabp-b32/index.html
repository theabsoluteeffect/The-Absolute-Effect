← The Absolute Effect

Breast Cancer · Surgical · Early-stage · Clinically Node-Negative

NSABP B-32

Clinical question

In clinically node-negative breast cancer, can sentinel lymph-node surgery replace routine axillary lymph-node dissection without compromising survival or regional control?

Trial context
Population
5,611 women with clinically node-negative invasive breast cancer; 3,989 had pathologically negative sentinel nodes and formed the primary survival-analysis population.
Intervention
Sentinel lymph-node resection alone when the sentinel node was negative.
Comparator
Sentinel lymph-node resection followed by conventional axillary lymph-node dissection.
Sample size / primary endpoint
5,611 randomized; primary outcomes included overall survival, disease-free survival and regional control among SLN-negative patients.
Absolute Effect
8-year overall survival
91.8% vs 90.3% (+1.5 percentage points)

SLN surgery alone versus SLN surgery plus ALND among patients with pathologically negative SLNs.

8-year disease-free survival
82.4% vs 81.5% (+0.9 points)

There were 8 versus 14 regional-node recurrences as first events (P=.22).

Relative effect / statistics

OS HR 1.20 (95% CI 0.96–1.50; P=.12); DFS HR 1.05 (95% CI 0.90–1.22; P=.54). The study supported statistical equivalence of outcomes in the SLN-negative population.

Treatment burden / toxicity

The major treatment-burden endpoint was surgical morbidity. Blue-dye allergic reactions were the most common adverse event; removing fewer axillary nodes avoids the morbidity of ALND.

NNT/NNH: NNT: not applicable. This was a surgical de-escalation/equivalence question; a meaningful benefit NNT is not established from the small survival differences.

Quality of life / subgroups

The clinically important subgroup is patients with a negative SLN; the results do not justify omitting ALND when the SLN is positive. The trial had adequate scale for survival and regional-control assessment.

Limitations and maturity

The primary survival analysis was restricted to pathologically SLN-negative patients; practice in SLN-positive disease was addressed by later trials such as Z0011. Older systemic therapy and surgical practice also limit direct extrapolation.

Clinical interpretation

For clinically node-negative breast cancer with a negative SLN, completion ALND adds axillary surgery without a demonstrated survival or regional-control advantage.
