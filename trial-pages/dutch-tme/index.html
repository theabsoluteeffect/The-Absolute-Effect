When high-quality total mesorectal excision is performed, does adding short-course preoperative radiotherapy reduce local recurrence?

Population
1,417 patients with primary rectal adenocarcinoma within 15 cm of the anal verge, without distant metastases, randomized to TME alone or 5×5 Gy preoperative RT followed by TME.
Sample size
1,417 analyzed patients: 713 RT+TME and 704 TME alone.
Intervention vs comparator
TME alone versus short-course preoperative RT 25 Gy in 5 fractions followed by TME.
Primary endpoint
Primary clinical question: local recurrence after standardized TME; survival and recurrence patterns were secondary.

Absolute Effect

The principal randomized evidence established local-control benefit; the trial did not demonstrate a major overall-survival advantage attributable to RT. Later follow-up confirmed that improved local control did not automatically translate into OS improvement.

Absolute effect: Five-year local recurrence was 4.6% with RT+TME versus 11.0% with TME alone: absolute reduction 6.4 percentage points.

NNT: 16, based on 5-year local recurrence (absolute reduction 6.4 percentage points).

NNH: not quantifiable from the cited recurrence analysis because a single comparable fixed-time serious toxicity endpoint was not reported.

Follow-up: Median follow-up 7.0 years in the detailed recurrence-pattern analysis.

Clinical results and treatment burden

  • Intervention: TME alone versus short-course preoperative RT 25 Gy in 5 fractions followed by TME.
  • Principal toxicity: Preoperative RT adds treatment burden and can affect postoperative function, but the detailed recurrence publication did not provide a single prespecified grade 3–5 toxicity rate suitable for an NNH calculation.
  • Other efficacy/recurrence findings: RT reduced local recurrence across subsites; presacral recurrence remained important, particularly with advanced tumours and after abdominoperineal resection.
  • Quality of life: Long-term bowel and sexual function were relevant consequences of treatment, but the recurrence analysis was not primarily a QoL trial.

Eligibility and clinical context

1,417 patients with primary rectal adenocarcinoma within 15 cm of the anal verge, without distant metastases, randomized to TME alone or 5×5 Gy preoperative RT followed by TME.

Limitations and maturity: The trial predates modern high-resolution pelvic MRI, routine neoadjuvant systemic therapy and contemporary risk-adapted RT. Its value is foundational: it established the interaction between meticulous TME and preoperative pelvic RT.

Clinical interpretation

The Dutch TME programme established TME as the surgical foundation and showed that even excellent surgery leaves a clinically meaningful pelvic-relapse risk that preoperative RT can reduce.

Primary / authoritative source

Dutch TME Trial investigators; recurrence-pattern analysis, Eur J Surg Oncol. 2010.
