The Absolute Effect / Evidence / ELIOT

Breast Cancer · Radiation · Stage I · Intraoperative electron radiotherapy

ELIOT

In selected women with early breast cancer undergoing breast-conserving surgery, can single-dose electron intraoperative radiotherapy replace postoperative whole-breast irradiation?

Registry: NCT01849133 · One underlying randomized study; aliases and follow-up reports consolidated.

Design, population and eligibility

Design

Single-centre, open-label, randomized phase III equivalence trial.

Population

1,305 women; 654 assigned whole-breast irradiation and 651 ELIOT.

Eligibility

Women aged 48–75 years with unicentric tumour ≤25 mm and clinically negative axilla; final pathology may identify higher-risk features.

Maturity

Median follow-up 12.4 years; 15-year local recurrence and OS reported.

Treatment and comparator

Electron intraoperative radiotherapy (ELIOT), 21 Gy single fraction delivered to the tumour bed during surgery.

Whole-breast irradiation, 50 Gy in 25 fractions plus a 10 Gy tumour-bed boost.

Primary endpoint: Ipsilateral breast tumour recurrence (IBTR); OS secondary.

Other endpoints: Local recurrence at 5, 10 and 15 years; overall survival.

Key efficacy results

Fifteen-year IBTR was 12.6% with ELIOT versus 2.4% with whole-breast irradiation; HR 4.62 (95% CI 2.68–7.95), P<0.0001.

Five-year IBTR: 4.2% versus 0.5%; ten-year: 8.1% versus 1.1%.

OS did not differ: HR 1.03 (95% CI 0.77–1.36), P=0.85; 15-year OS 83.4% versus 82.4%.

Absolute effect

Fifteen-year IBTR was 12.6% with ELIOT and 2.4% with whole-breast irradiation: absolute excess 10.2 percentage points. Approximate NNH ≈10 at 15 years for one additional IBTR with ELIOT compared with whole-breast irradiation. This is a recurrence endpoint, not a toxicity NNH.

NNT: No benefit NNT; ELIOT had more local recurrences.

NNH: Approximately 10 at 15 years for one additional ipsilateral breast tumour recurrence with ELIOT versus whole-breast irradiation.

Toxicity, harms and treatment burden

The principal harm is inferior local control; long-term adverse-event data were not collected in this trial, so late toxicity comparisons cannot be reliably quantified.

Single-session treatment reduces postoperative RT visits but does not eliminate the need for final pathology review or possible additional external-beam RT in patients with adverse features.

No grade 3–5 toxicity comparison or treatment-discontinuation rate is available in the final long-term abstract; do not invent one.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

The single intraoperative treatment substantially reduces travel and treatment time, but the primary long-term publication does not provide a robust quantitative QoL comparison. Convenience should not obscure the 10.2-point 15-year local recurrence excess.

Subgroups, maturity and limitations

ELIOT is a single-centre trial with a specific electron technique and patient selection; external validity depends on expertise and pathology-based selection.

Local recurrence risk was markedly higher after ELIOT, particularly in patients with adverse pathological features in other analyses; risk-adapted selection is essential.

The trial did not collect long-term adverse-event data; absence of toxicity data is not evidence of absence of toxicity.

Clinical interpretation

ELIOT shortened treatment to one intraoperative fraction but produced substantially more ipsilateral breast recurrence over 15 years, without an OS difference. It should be considered only in carefully selected low-risk patients after explaining the local-control trade-off.

Current-practice context · 1 November 2026 (full guideline freshness review pending): Intraoperative RT is not interchangeable with standard whole-breast RT for unselected early breast cancer. Where considered, use strict selection, discuss final pathology and the possibility of additional postoperative irradiation.
