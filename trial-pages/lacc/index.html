Gynaecological Oncology · Cervical cancer · Early-stage surgery

LACC

In early-stage cervical cancer requiring radical hysterectomy, does minimally invasive surgery preserve oncological outcomes compared with open abdominal surgery?

Design, population and eligibility

International, multicentre, randomised phase 3 non-inferiority trial. A total of 631 patients with FIGO 2009 stage IA1 with lymphovascular-space invasion, IA2 or IB1 cervical cancer were randomised: 319 to minimally invasive radical hysterectomy and 312 to open abdominal radical hysterectomy. Eligible histologies included squamous-cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma and adenosquamous carcinoma.

The primary endpoint was disease-free survival (DFS) at 4.5 years. The trial failed to establish non-inferiority of minimally invasive surgery.

Treatment and comparator

Intervention: laparoscopic or robot-assisted radical hysterectomy with pelvic lymph-node assessment.

Comparator: open abdominal radical hysterectomy with pelvic lymph-node assessment.

Key efficacy outcomes

Minimally invasive
85.0%
Open abdominal
96.0%

In the final 4.5-year follow-up, DFS was 85.0% with minimally invasive surgery versus 96.0% with open surgery; HR 3.91 (95% CI 2.02–7.58), P<0.001. Four-and-a-half-year OS was 90.6% versus 96.2%; HR 2.71 (95% CI 1.32–5.59), P=0.007. The original primary report also showed inferior DFS with minimally invasive surgery.

Absolute effect and NNH

NNH 10 for oncological failure: the 4.5-year DFS difference is 96.0% − 85.0% = 11.0 percentage points. 1/0.11 = 9.09, rounded up to 10. This is an NNH for a DFS event (recurrence or death), not a perioperative toxicity NNH.

Perioperative harms and quality of life

A dedicated safety analysis included 536 patients: grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 91/536 (16.9%) and serious adverse events in 69/536 (12.8%) overall; the abstract does not provide a single arm-specific grade ≥3 composite rate. In the primary report, intraoperative and postoperative complication rates were not significantly different between groups.

The patient-reported outcome analysis found no significant between-group difference in FACT-Cx total quality-of-life scores at baseline, 6 weeks or 3 months. Surgical approach did not produce a measurable short-term global QoL advantage that offsets the oncological result.

Interpretation and limitations

The LACC trial demonstrated inferior DFS and OS with minimally invasive radical hysterectomy in the studied early-stage population. The result applies to radical hysterectomy; it should not be conflated with the separate SHAPE question of simple hysterectomy in carefully selected low-risk disease. The final follow-up is more mature than the original report, but subgroup analyses remain limited by event counts.

Current-practice context · reviewed 15 November 2026

The 2023 ESGO/ESTRO/ESP cervical cancer guideline describes open laparotomy as the standard approach for procedures involving radical parametrectomy. The PubMed-indexed NCCN Cervical Cancer Version 2.2026 guideline article record was checked, but the full guideline and reference list were inaccessible in this run. No NCCN category or reference number is claimed; a separate guideline-freshness task remains open.
