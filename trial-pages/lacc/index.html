Cervical Cancer · Surgical · Localized / Early-stage

LACC trial

Does minimally invasive radical hysterectomy preserve survival compared with open radical hysterectomy in early cervical cancer?

Population and treatment

Phase III, multicentre, randomized non-inferiority trial. 631 women with FIGO stage IA1 with lymphovascular-space invasion, IA2 or IB1 squamous-cell, adenocarcinoma or adenosquamous cervical cancer and ECOG performance status 0–1 were enrolled: 319 assigned to minimally invasive radical hysterectomy and 312 to open abdominal radical hysterectomy. The trial closed early after a safety imbalance.

Primary endpoint: disease-free survival at 4.5 years, defined as recurrence or death from cervical cancer. Comparator: open abdominal radical hysterectomy. Registry: NCT00614211.

Results

Original primary analysis: 4.5-year DFS was 86.0% with minimally invasive surgery versus 96.5% with open surgery; absolute difference −10.6 percentage points (95% CI −16.4 to −4.7). HR for recurrence or cervical-cancer death 3.74 (95% CI 1.63–8.58). Three-year OS was 93.8% versus 99.0% (HR for death 6.00, 95% CI 1.77–20.30).

Final OS analysis: at 4.5 years, DFS was 85.0% versus 96.0% (difference −11.1 points; HR 3.91, 95% CI 2.02–7.58); OS was 90.6% versus 96.2% (HR 2.71, 95% CI 1.32–5.59).

Toxicity, surgical morbidity and quality of life

This surgical trial did not use a systemic CTCAE grade 3–5 toxicity endpoint. The relevant treatment burden is perioperative morbidity and the oncologic consequences of surgical approach. The secondary quality-of-life analysis included 496/631 patients (244/312 open; 252/319 minimally invasive). FACT-Cx total scores were similar at 6 weeks (128.7 open vs 130.0 minimally invasive) and 3 months (132.0 vs 133.0); postoperative quality of life was similar between groups.

Quality-of-life publication: Frumovitz M, et al. Lancet Oncol. 2020;21:851–860. DOI 10.1016/S1470-2045(20)30081-4 · PMID 32502445.

Interpretation and limitations

Minimally invasive radical hysterectomy failed the non-inferiority objective and was associated with worse DFS and OS than open surgery in this randomized population. Open radical hysterectomy remains the oncologically supported approach for patients requiring radical hysterectomy; do not extrapolate SHAPE de-escalation results to this higher-risk population.

The study stopped before planned enrollment after a safety imbalance. The 2024 final analysis strengthens the adverse oncologic signal. Quality of life was similar despite the different cancer outcomes. Current guideline-version freshness remains a separate open task.
