Bladder cancer · Medical Oncology · Phase II

Disitamab vedotin plus tislelizumab

BCG-unresponsive high-risk NMIBC in patients seeking bladder preservation. This page separates the primary endpoint from secondary outcomes and distinguishes source-reported absolute rates from time-to-event estimates.

DesignOpen-label, single-arm phase II study
Population28 patients with BCG-unresponsive high-risk NMIBC treated with bladder-preserving systemic therapy.
Primary endpointComplete response at the protocol-defined early assessment.

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 28 patients with BCG-unresponsive high-risk NMIBC treated with bladder-preserving systemic therapy.

Intervention: Disitamab vedotin 2 mg/kg intravenously every 2 weeks plus tislelizumab 200 mg intravenously every 3 weeks on the protocol schedule.

Comparator: No randomized comparator.

Primary endpoint: Complete response at the protocol-defined early assessment.

Follow-up and maturity: Response durability assessed through 24 months; cystoscopic surveillance and salvage cystectomy remain essential.

Registry: NCT05495724. Randomization, analysis populations and endpoint definitions should be read from the linked primary report and protocol; no denominator is inferred where the abstract does not state one.

Key efficacy outcome

Complete response

Complete response (19/28)
67.9%

Complete response occurred in 19/28 patients (67.9%); durable response at 24 months was reported in approximately 83.5% of responders/defined response population as specified in the primary report.

Time-to-event medians and hazard ratios are reported as such; they are not converted into NNT. Fixed-time absolute effects are shown separately only when a comparable endpoint and horizon are available.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in approximately 28.6%; cytopenias, neuropathy, skin reactions and immune-related toxicity are relevant.

No NNT is calculated because this is a single-arm or non-comparative study without a randomized comparator.

Quality of life and treatment burden

No comparative QoL outcome is available.

Interpretation and limitations

The combination is a promising bladder-preserving investigational approach, but randomized comparison and longer-term cystectomy-free survival are needed.

Small single-arm study with selected BCG-unresponsive patients; response definitions and denominator for durability must be kept distinct. This does not replace standard cystectomy discussion.

Superiority, non-inferiority, surrogate response and mature survival are not interchangeable. The page preserves the primary endpoint hierarchy and does not infer a survival advantage from response or PFS alone.
