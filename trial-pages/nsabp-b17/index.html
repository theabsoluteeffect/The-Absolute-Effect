Breast Cancer · Radiation · DCIS · Breast-Conserving Surgery · 2026-09-18

NSABP B-17

After complete excision of localized DCIS with negative margins, does whole-breast radiotherapy reduce ipsilateral breast recurrence compared with lumpectomy alone?

Population
818 women with localized mammographically detected DCIS and histologically tumor-free margins.
Intervention
Lumpectomy followed by 50 Gy whole-breast irradiation.
Comparator
Lumpectomy alone.
Sample size / primary endpoint
818 randomized women; Event-free survival / ipsilateral breast tumour recurrence

Absolute Effect

Primary efficacy result
8-year invasive ipsilateral recurrence: 3.9% vs 13.4% (−9.5 percentage points)
Mean follow-up 90 months; mature 8-year analysis.

Overall survival was not materially different because deaths from breast cancer were uncommon; the principal benefit was local/ipsilateral control.

At 5 years, event-free survival was 84.4% with RT vs 73.8% without RT (absolute +10.6 points; P=.001).

NNT / NNH
NNT: 11, based on 5-year event-free survival.
NNH: not quantifiable; the trial's clinically central trade-off is additional treatment burden rather than a reliably quantifiable fixed-time serious-toxicity endpoint.

Treatment burden and toxicity

The major treatment burden was addition of whole-breast RT. No major excess in regional/distant events or breast-cancer mortality was demonstrated. Radiation reduced both invasive and non-invasive ipsilateral tumours.

Quality of life: No modern patient-reported QoL endpoint was central to the original trial.

Subgroups / clinically important modifiers

All examined clinical/mammographic subgroups benefited from RT in the 8-year report.

Limitations and maturity

Older RT technique and DCIS diagnostic era; systemic/endocrine selection was not the modern ER-directed pathway. The endpoint was recurrence, not overall survival.

Clinical interpretation

Radiotherapy after lumpectomy substantially reduces ipsilateral recurrence in DCIS, but the demonstrated absolute benefit is primarily local control rather than survival.

This is the foundational randomized evidence for adding RT after breast-conserving surgery for DCIS and provides the comparator against which later RT-omission studies should be interpreted.
