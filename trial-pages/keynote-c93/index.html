Clinical question: In first-line dMMR advanced or recurrent endometrial cancer, does pembrolizumab monotherapy improve outcomes compared with carboplatin-paclitaxel?

Published 14 November 2026 (online 26 October 2026). Interim analysis; data cutoff 24 April 2026. Median PFS was 38.2 versus 8.4 months (HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.42–0.79; p=0.0002). The interim OS boundary was not met.

Eligibility: Adults aged 18 years or older with inoperable stage III or IV, persistent, advanced or recurrent mismatch-repair-deficient endometrial cancer.

Intervention: Pembrolizumab 400 mg IV every 6 weeks for up to 18 cycles.

Comparator: Carboplatin AUC 5 or 6 plus paclitaxel 175 mg/m² IV on day 1 every 3 weeks for six cycles.

Stratification: Disease status, histology and previous neoadjuvant/adjuvant chemotherapy.

Primary endpoints: Progression-free survival by blinded independent central review (RECIST 1.1) and overall survival.

Approximate NNT to avoid one grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse event = ceil[100/(51−18)] = 4. Treatment-related adverse events led to discontinuation in 14/149 (9%) versus 21/144 (15%). Treatment-related deaths occurred in two versus one participant.

Common treatment-related events (pembrolizumab versus chemotherapy) included alopecia 2% versus 50%, anaemia 5% versus 49%, nausea 5% versus 28%, decreased neutrophil count 3% versus 28%, peripheral neuropathy 0% versus 26%, and fatigue 13% versus 26%.

Patient-reported outcomes and maturity

Quality of life was assessed in the study, but numerical QoL results are not provided in the accessible PubMed abstract; they are not inferred here. The abstract reports the interim data cutoff (24 April 2026) but not median follow-up. OS remains immature at this analysis.

Interpretation and limitations

First-line pembrolizumab monotherapy produced a substantial PFS improvement over carboplatin-paclitaxel in this dMMR population and fewer grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events. The interim OS analysis did not meet its prespecified statistical significance boundary.

Critical applicability limitation: The trial did not compare pembrolizumab monotherapy with the contemporary standard of care of immunotherapy plus chemotherapy. It therefore does not establish that pembrolizumab alone is superior to chemo-immunotherapy or that it should replace combination treatment in patients eligible for that approach.

Landmark NNTs are approximate because the Kaplan–Meier percentages are rounded. No OS NNT is calculated because the interim OS comparison was not statistically significant and fixed-time absolute OS estimates were not supplied in the abstract.
