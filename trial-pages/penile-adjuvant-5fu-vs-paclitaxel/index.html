Adjuvant Paclitaxel-Platinum vs 5-FU-Platinum in High-Risk Penile Cancer

Penile Cancer · Adjuvant High-Risk Node-Positive

Clinical question

In high-risk node-positive penile squamous cell carcinoma, does adjuvant paclitaxel-platinum provide comparable efficacy with lower toxicity than 5-FU-platinum?

Trial context

This randomized 2026 study enrolled men with node-positive penile squamous cell carcinoma with at least one high-risk feature. Treatment was followed by cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy.

Key result

At median follow-up of 69 months, median DFS was 12.4 versus 19.6 months and median OS was 21.3 versus 36.7 months for 5-FU-platinum versus paclitaxel-platinum. The study was stopped early and was underpowered for definitive efficacy comparisons. Grade ≥3 toxicity was 77.3% versus 40.9%, grade ≥3 hematologic toxicity 54.5% versus 4.5%, and hospitalization for toxicity 45.5% versus 9.1%.

Critical clinical interpretation

The study does not establish survival equivalence or superiority. Its strongest signal is treatment burden, with substantially less severe toxicity and better treatment completion in the paclitaxel-platinum arm.

Comparator limitation

The trial did not directly compare paclitaxel-platinum with neoadjuvant TIP. TIP is an influential regimen for high-risk nodal penile cancer in the neoadjuvant setting, but this randomized study addresses postoperative adjuvant treatment. Its results should therefore not be generalized to establish paclitaxel-platinum as the preferred regimen for all high-risk penile cancer.

Clinical takeaway

The most defensible conclusion is a strong tolerability signal favouring paclitaxel-platinum, with efficacy remaining inconclusive because of early termination and limited sample size.
