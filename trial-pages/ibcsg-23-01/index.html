The Absolute Effect / Evidence / Breast Cancer

Breast Cancer · Surgical · Stage I · Definitive / Local Treatment

IBCSG 23-01

Clinical question: In early breast cancer with sentinel-node micrometastases ≤2 mm, can completion axillary lymph-node dissection be omitted?

Registry / identifier: NCT00072293 · One underlying study; aliases and follow-up publications consolidated.

Phase III · randomized · non-inferiorityEvidence cut-off: 8 November 2026

Design, population and eligibility

Design

International, open-label randomized phase III non-inferiority trial.

Sample size

934 randomized: 469 to no completion ALND and 465 to ALND; three ineligible patients excluded after randomization.

Eligibility

Clinically node-negative early breast cancer with sentinel-node micrometastases ≤2 mm and no extracapsular extension; patients underwent breast-conserving surgery or mastectomy.

Follow-up / maturity

Median follow-up 9.7 years; 10-year DFS reported.

Treatment and comparator

Intervention: No completion axillary lymph-node dissection after sentinel-node biopsy.

Comparator: Completion axillary lymph-node dissection.

Primary endpoint: Disease-free survival; non-inferiority of omission.

Key efficacy outcomes

10-year DFS: 76.8% without ALND vs 74.9% with ALND HR 0.85 (95% CI 0.65–1.11); superiority p=0.24; non-inferiority p=0.0024.

Any-grade lymphoedema: 4% vs 13% Long-term surgical morbidity.

Sensory neuropathy: 13% vs 19%

Motor neuropathy: 3% vs 9%

The 10-year DFS difference was +1.9 percentage points for omission, but the superiority comparison was not significant. The prespecified non-inferiority criterion was met. This is evidence for de-escalation, not superiority.

NNT / NNH and absolute benefit–harm balance

No NNT for DFS: the trial established non-inferiority, not superiority. The observed absolute 9-point reduction in any-grade lymphoedema corresponds to approximately 12 patients managed without ALND to avoid one case over the reported long-term follow-up (crude reciprocal of the observed difference; not a prespecified NNT).

No comparative NNH is supported for the primary efficacy endpoint. The morbidity balance favoured omission: sensory and motor neuropathy were also less frequent without ALND.

Toxicity, grade 3–5 harms and treatment burden

Any-grade lymphoedema 4% versus 13%; sensory neuropathy 13% versus 19%; motor neuropathy 3% versus 9%. One serious postoperative infection/inflamed axilla requiring admission was attributed to ALND. Grade 3–5 event rates were not reported in the abstracted long-term summary.

Omission avoids a second axillary operation and its recovery burden. Systemic treatment and radiotherapy were delivered according to local standards; treatment allocation was not blinded.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

The trial's long-term report primarily quantifies surgical morbidity. A formal global QoL comparison is not available in the cited 10-year abstract.

Subgroups and applicability

The result applies to minimal sentinel-node tumour burden ≤2 mm. It should not be extrapolated to macrometastases, clinically node-positive disease or gross extranodal extension.

Critical appraisal and limitations

Low-volume sentinel-node disease; open-label design; DFS difference was not superior; the trial predates some current systemic and regional nodal treatment patterns. Event numbers were limited.

Clinical interpretation

For appropriately selected patients with sentinel-node micrometastases, omission of completion ALND preserves 10-year DFS within the prespecified non-inferiority framework and reduces arm morbidity. Do not generalize this result to macrometastatic disease.
