SWENOTECA clinical stage I NSGCT management programme

In clinical stage I non-seminomatous germ-cell tumour, how does risk-adapted one-cycle BEP alter relapse risk compared with surveillance?

Population745 patients with clinical stage I NSGCT managed prospectively in Sweden and Norway.Risk factorVascular tumour invasion was used to guide treatment recommendations.InterventionOne cycle BEP in patients recommended adjuvant treatment; vascular-invasion-negative patients could choose surveillance or BEP.Key endpointRelapse, with disease-specific survival as the ultimate safety endpoint.

Absolute Effect

Vascular-invasion positive
41.7% surveillance vs 3.2% after 1-cycle BEP

Absolute relapse reduction ≈38.5 percentage points.

Vascular-invasion negative
13.2% surveillance vs 1.3% after 1-cycle BEP

Absolute relapse reduction ≈11.9 percentage points.

Clinical interpretation

Risk-adapted treatment sharply reduces relapse after orchiectomy, particularly in patients with vascular invasion, while surveillance remains an option for selected lower-risk patients.

No patient died from progressive disease in the reported cohort. The clinical trade-off is avoiding chemotherapy in patients who may never relapse versus accepting relapse risk and intensive salvage treatment.

Limitations

This was a prospective management programme rather than a single randomized comparison of BEP versus surveillance across all risk groups. Treatment choice was risk-adapted, so absolute differences should not be interpreted as randomized causal effects.

NNT

Because this is not a randomized two-arm trial, a formal NNT is not appropriate. The absolute relapse differences are reported directly to preserve the study design.
