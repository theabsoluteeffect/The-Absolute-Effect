Does consolidation durvalumab improve outcomes after definitive concurrent chemoradiotherapy (cCRT) in limited-stage SCLC without progression?

Population and treatment

Patients with limited-stage SCLC (stage I–III) without disease progression after 4 cycles of platinum-based concurrent chemoradiotherapy; ECOG performance status 0–1. Patients were randomized 1:1:1 to durvalumab, durvalumab plus tremelimumab, or placebo. The reported primary comparison here is durvalumab monotherapy versus placebo; the combination arm remained blinded at the primary interim analysis.

Durvalumab: 1,500 mg IV every 4 weeks for up to 24 months, initiated within 42 days after cCRT. Comparator: placebo on the same schedule.

Dual primary endpoints: OS and PFS for durvalumab vs placebo.

Efficacy

Overall survival · 36-month landmark

36-month OS was 56.5% with durvalumab vs 47.6% with placebo (HR 0.73; 98.321% CI 0.54–0.98; P=.01).

Any-grade pneumonitis/radiation pneumonitis: 38.2% vs 30.2% (absolute excess 8.0 points; approximate NNH 13). Grade 3–4 pneumonitis/radiation pneumonitis: 3.1% vs 2.6% (absolute excess 0.5 points; approximate NNH 200).

Interpretation

Consolidation durvalumab after cCRT significantly improved OS and PFS compared with placebo in patients with limited-stage SCLC who had not progressed after cCRT. Grade 3–4 all-cause AE rates were similar, although discontinuation and pneumonitis were more frequent with durvalumab.

Do not combine the durvalumab-tremelimumab arm with the primary durvalumab-versus-placebo comparison. These data are from the first planned interim analysis.
