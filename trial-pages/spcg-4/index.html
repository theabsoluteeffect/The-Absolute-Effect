Prostate Cancer · Surgical · Localized disease

SPCG-4

In localized prostate cancer, does radical prostatectomy improve survival and metastatic outcomes compared with watchful waiting?

Population695 men randomized between 1989 and 1999 with clinically localized prostate cancer.InterventionRadical prostatectomy.ComparatorWatchful waiting, with treatment for progression when indicated.Follow-upVery long-term follow-up is essential because prostate-cancer mortality differences widened over time.

Absolute Effect

15-year prostate-cancer mortality
Absolute reduction 6.1 percentage points

The 15-year analysis estimated an NNT of approximately 17 to avert one prostate-cancer death.

23-year follow-up
11.0-point absolute reduction in prostate-cancer mortality

Prostate-cancer death occurred in 63/347 after surgery versus 99/348 with watchful waiting; relative risk 0.56.

Time dependence

The absolute benefit increased with longer follow-up. A later analysis found prostate-cancer mortality ARR rising from 1.7% at 5 years to 17.0% at 30 years, illustrating why a single short-term NNT can be misleading in a disease with a long natural history.

Clinical interpretation

SPCG-4 demonstrates that radical prostatectomy can reduce prostate-cancer mortality and metastasis over long follow-up, while the magnitude of benefit depends strongly on age, competing mortality and disease risk.

The trial population predates PSA-era contemporary imaging and active surveillance selection, so absolute event rates should not be directly applied to today's low-risk populations.

Treatment burden and limitations

Surgery carries urinary, sexual and perioperative morbidity. Watchful waiting is not equivalent to modern MRI-based active surveillance. The trial included a broad historical spectrum of localized disease.

NNT

NNT ≈17 at 15 years, and approximately 9 using the 11.0-point absolute reduction at 23 years. These are time-specific mortality NNTs and should not be mixed across follow-up horizons.
