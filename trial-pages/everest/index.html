EVEREST

SWOG S0931 · NCT01120249

Clinical question

After complete nephrectomy for high-risk renal cell carcinoma, does one year of adjuvant everolimus reduce recurrence compared with placebo?

The trial's prespecified one-sided significance threshold was p=0.044. The observed RFS difference therefore fell just short of the required threshold.

Absolute Effect
4 fewer recurrences per 100 patients at 5 years

The overall randomized result corresponds to an approximately 4 percentage-point improvement in 5-year recurrence-free survival. This is a modest absolute benefit, and it must be interpreted alongside treatment toxicity and discontinuation.

The subgroup signal does not overturn the neutral overall primary analysis. A subgroup result should not be interpreted as proof that the overall trial was positive.

Treatment burden
46% vs 11% grade ≥3 adverse events

Grade 3 or higher adverse events occurred in 343/740 patients receiving everolimus versus 79/723 receiving placebo — an absolute excess of approximately 35 percentage points.

Only 355 of 755 eligible patients assigned to everolimus completed the planned 54-week treatment; treatment discontinuation was a major limitation.

Overall survival

No statistically significant overall-survival benefit was demonstrated in the primary trial population. The later very-high-risk clear-cell analysis also found improved recurrence-free survival but no significant overall-survival benefit (HR 0.85, 95% CI 0.64–1.14; p=0.30).

Absolute Effect clinical verdict

EVEREST did not establish adjuvant everolimus as a routine postoperative standard for high-risk RCC.

  • The overall trial showed only a modest absolute improvement in 5-year recurrence-free survival and narrowly missed its prespecified significance threshold.
  • A larger recurrence-free survival effect was seen in the very-high-risk subgroup, but this should be interpreted in the context of the overall randomized result.
  • The potential benefit came with substantial treatment burden: grade ≥3 toxicity was approximately 35 percentage points higher with everolimus.
  • The clinically relevant question is therefore not simply whether everolimus can reduce recurrence, but whether the modest absolute benefit justifies the toxicity and discontinuation burden.

Primary evidence

Ryan CW et al. Lancet. 2023;402:1043–1051. Final phase III EVEREST results. The later clear-cell/very-high-risk analysis was published in European Urology in 2024.
