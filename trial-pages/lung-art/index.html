Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer · Radiation · Adjuvant / Postoperative

Lung ART

In patients with completely resected pN2 NSCLC, does postoperative mediastinal radiotherapy improve disease-free survival compared with no PORT?

IFCT-0503 · NCT00410683 · One underlying randomized study.

Design, population and eligibility

Design

Open-label, multicentre, phase III randomized superiority trial across 64 centres in five European countries.

Population

501 patients with completely resected NSCLC and pathologically or cytologically proven mediastinal N2 involvement; 252 assigned PORT and 249 observation.

Eligibility

Complete resection, mediastinal nodal exploration and N2 involvement; age ≥18 years, WHO performance status 0–2. Neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapy permitted.

Maturity

Median follow-up 4.8 years; primary endpoint assessed at 3 years.

Treatment and comparator

PORT to mediastinum, 54 Gy in 27 or 30 daily fractions; 3D conformal radiotherapy mandatory, IMRT permitted in experienced centres.

Control: no postoperative radiotherapy. Systemic chemotherapy was allowed and commonly delivered.

Primary endpoint: 3-year disease-free survival by intention to treat. Secondary outcomes included OS, patterns of relapse and toxicity.

Key efficacy results

Three-year DFS was 47% (95% CI 40–54) with PORT versus 44% (37–51) without PORT; HR 0.86 (95% CI 0.68–1.08), P=0.18. The primary endpoint was not statistically significant.

Median DFS was 30.5 versus 22.8 months. Three-year OS was 67% with PORT versus 69% without PORT; adjusted HR 0.97 (95% CI 0.73–1.28) in the primary report.

Mediastinal relapse was reduced (25% with PORT versus 46% without PORT), but this did not translate into a significant DFS or OS improvement.

Absolute effect

The observed three-year DFS difference was 3 percentage points, but the confidence interval crossed 1 and P=0.18. No statistically significant DFS superiority was established. The reduction in mediastinal relapse should be considered separately from DFS and OS.

NNT: Not assigned; primary superiority endpoint was not significant.

NNH: Not assigned as one fixed-time toxicity endpoint; late grade 3–4 cardiopulmonary toxicity was 11% versus 5%.

Toxicity and treatment burden

Early grade 3–4 adverse events: 12% with PORT versus 8% with observation. Common grade 3–4 events included pneumonitis (5% vs <1%), lymphopenia (4% vs 0%) and fatigue (3% vs <1%).

Late grade 3–4 cardiopulmonary toxicity: 11% versus 5%. Two patients died from pneumonitis partly related to RT and infection; one died from chemotherapy-related sepsis, all in the PORT group.

PORT adds 27–30 treatment fractions and cardiopulmonary exposure after major thoracic surgery.

Quality of life, subgroups and limitations

No robust numerical QoL benefit is established in the primary report. Treatment burden, pulmonary reserve, cardiac exposure and postoperative recovery are central considerations.

Most patients were staged with PET-CT and received neoadjuvant or adjuvant chemotherapy. The study evaluated predominantly 3D conformal PORT; it does not establish the efficacy or toxicity profile of modern highly conformal IMRT in a selected high-risk subgroup.

PORT reduced mediastinal relapse but did not significantly improve the primary DFS endpoint or OS. The study was not a test of whether PORT can be omitted in all biologically or anatomically high-risk patients.

Clinical interpretation

Lung ART does not support routine 3D conformal PORT as standard treatment after complete resection of pN2 NSCLC. Better mediastinal control did not produce a statistically significant DFS or OS advantage and was accompanied by more late cardiopulmonary toxicity.

Current-practice context · 1 November 2026 (full guideline freshness review pending): Consider contemporary systemic therapy, margin status, extent of nodal disease, adequacy of nodal dissection, cardiopulmonary reserve and multidisciplinary review. Do not extrapolate the negative trial to every individualized PORT indication or assume IMRT is equivalent to the tested 3D-CRT strategy.
