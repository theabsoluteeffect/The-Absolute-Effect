Non-small-cell lung cancer · Medical oncology · Phase III

ALINA

Adjuvant alectinib versus platinum-based chemotherapy after complete resection of ALK-positive NSCLC. Primary publication: 2024; HRQoL/safety analysis: 2026. Evidence review: 24 November 2026.

DesignGlobal, open-label, randomized phase III trial; 1:1 allocation.
Population257 patients with completely resected stage IB (tumour ≥4 cm), II or IIIA ALK-positive NSCLC; 130 alectinib and 127 chemotherapy.
Primary endpointDisease-free survival, tested hierarchically in stage II–IIIA and then the intention-to-treat population.
Data maturityPrimary DFS analysis median follow-up 27.8 months; OS immature. HRQoL/safety follow-up was 24.8 months with alectinib.

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility: Adults with completely resected, histologically confirmed stage IB (tumour ≥4 cm), II or IIIA NSCLC (AJCC/UICC seventh edition), documented ALK-positive disease, ECOG performance status 0–1, and no previous systemic anticancer therapy. Randomization occurred 4–12 weeks after complete resection.

Randomization: 257 patients were randomized 1:1 to alectinib (n=130) or chemotherapy (n=127). Randomization was stratified by disease stage and race (Asian versus non-Asian). The primary DFS hypothesis was tested hierarchically in stage II–IIIA and then the ITT population.

Intervention: Alectinib 600 mg orally twice daily for 24 months or until recurrence, unacceptable toxicity or withdrawal.

Comparator: Four 21-day cycles of platinum-based chemotherapy. Protocol options included cisplatin 75 mg/m² on day 1 with vinorelbine 25 mg/m² on days 1 and 8, gemcitabine 1,250 mg/m² on days 1 and 8, or pemetrexed 500 mg/m² on day 1; carboplatin AUC 5–6 was permitted for cisplatin intolerance.

Primary endpoint: DFS, defined as recurrence or a new primary NSCLC or death from any cause. Secondary endpoints included OS and safety; CNS-DFS was exploratory.

Stage II–IIIA DFS: At 2 years, 93.8% with alectinib versus 63.0% with chemotherapy; HR 0.24 (95% CI 0.13–0.45; P<0.001). Median DFS was not reached with alectinib versus 44.4 months with chemotherapy.

ITT DFS: At 2 years, 93.6% versus 63.7%; HR 0.24 (95% CI 0.13–0.43; P<0.001). OS was immature at the primary analysis.

CNS-DFS: HR 0.22 (95% CI 0.08–0.58) in the ITT population.

Absolute effect and NNT

Stage II–IIIA DFS at 24 months: Absolute benefit 30.8 percentage points (93.8%−63.0%). Calculation: ceil[1/0.308]=4.

NNT 4 at 24 months

One additional patient alive and disease-free at 2 years for every 4 treated with adjuvant alectinib rather than platinum chemotherapy (stage II–IIIA). Calculation: ceil[1/0.308]=4.

No OS NNT is assigned because OS was immature at the primary analysis.

Safety, treatment burden and quality of life

The 2026 HRQoL/safety analysis included 128 alectinib-treated and 120 chemotherapy-treated patients in the safety population. Median safety follow-up was 24.8 months with alectinib and 3.7 months with chemotherapy. Grade 3–4 AEs occurred in 38/128 (29.7%) versus 37/120 (30.8%); no grade 5 AEs or AE-related deaths were reported. Serious treatment-related AEs occurred in 2/128 (1.6%) versus 8/120 (6.7%). AEs led to treatment withdrawal in 7/128 (5.5%) versus 15/120 (12.5%).

The most frequent grade 3–4 events with alectinib were increased creatine phosphokinase (8/128, 6%), alanine aminotransferase (2/128, 2%) and bilirubin (2/128, 2%). With chemotherapy, the most frequent were decreased neutrophil count (12/120, 10%), neutropenia (10/120, 8%) and nausea (5/120, 4%).

SF-36v2 HRQoL improved by week 12 with alectinib in bodily pain, role physical, mental health, social functioning and vitality domains. Physical and mental component scores were maintained through week 96 (48.8 and 49.9, respectively; population norm 50). Comparisons after week 12 are limited because chemotherapy had ended.

Interpretation and limitations

ALINA demonstrated a large DFS advantage for 2 years of adjuvant alectinib over four cycles of platinum chemotherapy in completely resected ALK-positive stage IB (≥4 cm)–IIIA NSCLC. The benefit is biomarker-specific and should not be generalized to ALK-negative disease. OS remains immature. The open-label design and different exposure durations complicate long-term safety and HRQoL comparisons after chemotherapy ends.

Current-practice context · reviewed 24 November 2026

Adjuvant alectinib is an evidence-supported postoperative option for resected ALK-positive NSCLC. The trial does not address other molecular subgroups or unresectable disease. The current full NCCN NSCLC guideline/reference list was not accessible for exact mapping; no NCCN version, section, reference number or recommendation category is claimed.
