The Absolute Effect

GETUG-AFU 16

Prostate Cancer · Salvage Radiotherapy

Clinical question

Does adding short-term androgen deprivation to salvage radiotherapy improve progression-free outcomes after prostatectomy biochemical recurrence?

Population and intervention

Men with biochemical recurrence after radical prostatectomy were randomized to salvage radiotherapy alone or salvage radiotherapy plus 6 months of androgen deprivation.

Key result

At 5 years, progression-free survival was approximately 80% with combined treatment versus 62% with radiotherapy alone, with a hazard ratio of about 0.50.

Absolute Effect perspective

The approximately 18 percentage-point improvement in 5-year progression-free survival is clinically meaningful. The endpoint and time horizon should always accompany any NNT or absolute-effect calculation, and the benefit should be balanced against the adverse effects of androgen deprivation.

Clinical takeaway

GETUG-AFU 16 established that short-term androgen deprivation can improve progression-related outcomes when added to salvage radiotherapy in appropriately selected men with post-prostatectomy biochemical recurrence.
