Prostate Cancer · Radiation · Localized · Dose Escalation · 2026-09-18

MRC RT01

Does escalating conformal prostate RT from 64 Gy to 74 Gy improve biochemical control?

Population
843 men with T1b–T3a N0 M0 prostate cancer and PSA <50 ng/mL; all received 3–6 months of neoadjuvant ADT.
Intervention
74 Gy in 37 fractions.
Comparator
64 Gy in 32 fractions.
Sample size / primary endpoint
843 randomized men; Biochemical progression-free survival and overall survival

Absolute Effect

Primary efficacy result
10-year biochemical PFS: 55% vs 43% (+12 percentage points)
Median follow-up 10.0 years.

10-year OS was identical at 71% in both groups (HR 0.99, 95% CI 0.77–1.28; P=.96).

Biochemical progression was reduced: 43% of the control group versus 57% of the escalated group remained free of progression at 10 years.

NNT / NNH
NNT: 9, based on 10-year biochemical progression-free survival.
NNH: 50, based on the 5-year cumulative incidence of severe patient-reported bowel problems (8% vs 6%).

Treatment burden and toxicity

Dose escalation increased late bowel toxicity. Grade ≥2 late bowel toxicity was more frequent; rectal bleeding, diarrhoea and proctitis all increased with escalation.

Quality of life: Patient-reported bowel and sexual function worsened during treatment; overall late severe bowel toxicity remained at clinically acceptable absolute levels.

Subgroups / clinically important modifiers

The trial supports biochemical-control improvement across the enrolled localized-risk population, but no OS advantage was demonstrated.

Limitations and maturity

The control dose is obsolete by current standards, and modern IMRT/image guidance may reduce the toxicity penalty.

Clinical interpretation

Dose escalation from 64 to 74 Gy improves biochemical control but not overall survival and increases late bowel toxicity.

RT01 is the key randomized dose-escalation step showing that better biochemical control does not automatically translate into longer survival; toxicity must be included in the absolute-effect calculation.
