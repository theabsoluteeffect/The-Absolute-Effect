In fit patients with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma, does first-line FOLFIRINOX improve survival compared with gemcitabine alone?

Population
342 chemotherapy-naïve patients with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma, ECOG performance status 0–1 and adequate organ function; patients ≥76 years were excluded.
Sample size
342 randomized patients (171 per arm).
Intervention vs comparator
FOLFIRINOX every 2 weeks: oxaliplatin 85 mg/m², irinotecan 180 mg/m², leucovorin 400 mg/m² and 5-FU 400 mg/m² bolus followed by 2,400 mg/m² over 46 h, versus gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m² on the standard weekly schedule.
Primary endpoint
Primary endpoint: overall survival.

Absolute Effect

Median OS was 11.1 vs 6.8 months; HR 0.57 (95% CI 0.45–0.73), P<.001. One-year OS was 48.4% vs 20.6%.

Absolute effect: At 1 year, survival improved by 27.8 percentage points (48.4% vs 20.6%). Median PFS was 6.4 vs 3.3 months; HR 0.47, P<.001. Response rate was 31.6% vs 9.4%.

NNT: 4, based on 1-year overall survival (absolute improvement 27.8 percentage points).

NNH: 5, based on grade 3–4 neutropenia (45.7% vs 21.0%; absolute increase approximately 24.7 percentage points).

Follow-up: Median follow-up 26.6 months.

Clinical results and treatment burden

  • Intervention: FOLFIRINOX every 2 weeks: oxaliplatin 85 mg/m², irinotecan 180 mg/m², leucovorin 400 mg/m² and 5-FU 400 mg/m² bolus followed by 2,400 mg/m² over 46 h, versus gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m² on the standard weekly schedule.
  • Principal toxicity: FOLFIRINOX caused substantially more grade 3–4 toxicities, particularly neutropenia, febrile neutropenia and diarrhea. Febrile neutropenia occurred in 5.4%.
  • Other efficacy/recurrence findings: Quality-of-life deterioration at 6 months occurred in 31% vs 66%, favouring FOLFIRINOX despite greater treatment toxicity.
  • Quality of life: Time to definitive deterioration of QoL was significantly delayed with FOLFIRINOX (HR 0.47, 95% CI 0.30–0.70; P<.001).

Eligibility and clinical context

342 chemotherapy-naïve patients with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma, ECOG performance status 0–1 and adequate organ function; patients ≥76 years were excluded.

Limitations and maturity: The trial enrolled a selected fit population and therefore does not establish FOLFIRINOX as the default for frail, elderly or ECOG ≥2 patients. Subsequent modified regimens may have different toxicity.

Clinical interpretation

For fit patients with metastatic pancreatic cancer, FOLFIRINOX produced a large absolute survival benefit and delayed QoL deterioration, at the cost of materially greater chemotherapy toxicity.

Primary / authoritative source

Conroy T, et al. FOLFIRINOX versus Gemcitabine for Metastatic Pancreatic Cancer. N Engl J Med. 2011;364:1817–1825.
