Endometrial cancer · Randomized phase III · Adjuvant / postoperative

GOG-258 (NRG-258)

In patients with locally advanced endometrial carcinoma after surgery, does adding volume-directed radiotherapy and concurrent cisplatin to adjuvant chemotherapy improve relapse-free survival compared with chemotherapy alone?

813Randomized patients
Phase IIIOpen-label randomized trial
47 monthsMedian follow-up at primary RFS analysis
112 monthsMedian follow-up at final OS analysis

Population and eligibility

Patients with FIGO 2009 stage III–IVA endometrial carcinoma with less than 2 cm residual disease, or stage I–II serous/clear-cell endometrial carcinoma with positive peritoneal cytology. Carcinosarcoma and recurrent endometrial carcinoma were excluded.

Primary endpoint: relapse-free survival (RFS). Secondary endpoints: overall survival (OS), acute/chronic toxicity and quality of life.

Treatment

Experimental: chemoradiotherapy

Concurrent cisplatin 50 mg/m² IV on days 1 and 29 with volume-directed external-beam radiotherapy (45 Gy, with brachytherapy when indicated), followed by carboplatin (AUC 5–6) plus paclitaxel 175 mg/m² every 3 weeks for 4 cycles.

Comparator: chemotherapy

Carboplatin (AUC 6) plus paclitaxel 175 mg/m² every 3 weeks for 6 cycles.

Randomized: 407 to chemoradiotherapy and 406 to chemotherapy. Primary RFS analysis included 736 eligible patients; 707 received assigned treatment (346 and 361, respectively).

Efficacy

Relapse-free survival at 5 years · primary endpoint

Five-year Kaplan–Meier RFS was 59% with chemoradiotherapy and 58% with chemotherapy alone (HR 0.90; 90% CI 0.74–1.10). The trial did not demonstrate an RFS improvement.

Five-year site-specific recurrence

Radiotherapy reduced vaginal and pelvic/paraaortic nodal recurrence, while distant recurrence was more frequent in the chemoradiotherapy arm.

These NNT/NNH values are reciprocals of reported 5-year absolute differences in recurrence incidence, rounded up. They are descriptive landmark estimates, not time-to-event adjusted treatment-effect estimates; interpret cautiously in the presence of competing risks and the trial's broader RFS result. They should not be presented as evidence of an overall net benefit.

Overall survival · long-term analysis

At median follow-up of 112 months, median OS was not reached in either arm. OS HR 1.05 (95% CI 0.82–1.34; P=.72). Chemoradiotherapy did not improve OS.

Grade 3–5 adverse events occurred in 202/346 (58%) patients receiving chemoradiotherapy and 227/361 (63%) receiving chemotherapy alone. These aggregate rates do not support an excess-harm NNH for chemoradiotherapy; do not infer toxicity equivalence from this comparison.

In the long-term report, treatment completion was 75% in the chemoradiotherapy arm and 85% in the chemotherapy arm. The detailed toxicity profile, specific adverse-event categories, dose modifications and patient-reported QoL should be interpreted from the primary report and its supplements rather than inferred from aggregate grade 3–5 rates.

Interpretation

Adding radiotherapy improved vaginal and pelvic/paraaortic control but did not improve RFS or OS. Distant recurrence was more frequent in the chemoradiotherapy arm. The results illustrate a locoregional-control benefit without a demonstrated survival advantage; they do not establish chemoradiotherapy as superior to chemotherapy alone.

Applicability is limited to the enrolled postoperative population and eligibility criteria. Do not extrapolate directly to carcinosarcoma, recurrent disease, or patients with residual disease beyond the trial eligibility threshold.
