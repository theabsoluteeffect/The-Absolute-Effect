Small Cell Lung Cancer · Consolidation / Maintenance

DeLLphi-305

In extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer that has not progressed after first-line durvalumab plus platinum–etoposide, does adding tarlatamab to durvalumab maintenance improve survival?

DesignPhase III · open-label · randomized 1:1
Population563 patients · ES-SCLC without progression after induction
Primary endpointOverall survival (interim analysis)
RegistryNCT06211036

Study design, population and eligibility

DeLLphi-305 is an international, open-label, randomized phase III maintenance study. It enrolled 563 patients with extensive-stage small-cell lung cancer (ES-SCLC) whose disease had not progressed after first-line durvalumab plus platinum–etoposide induction. Patients were randomized 1:1 after induction (tarlatamab–durvalumab, n=282; durvalumab alone, n=281). The trial population was fit for maintenance treatment (ECOG performance status 0–1); patients with treated or asymptomatic brain metastases could participate.

Randomization was stratified by brain and/or liver metastases, response to induction chemo-immunotherapy, and platinum agent. The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS); key secondary endpoints included progression-free survival (PFS), objective response and duration of response. The reported OS analysis was a prespecified interim analysis, not a final mature OS analysis.

Treatment and comparator

  • Experimental: tarlatamab 10 mg IV every 2 weeks (with protocol step-up dosing) plus durvalumab 1,500 mg IV every 4 weeks.
  • Control: durvalumab 1,500 mg IV every 4 weeks.
  • Both groups had received first-line durvalumab with platinum–etoposide induction (typically four cycles; at least three cycles of durvalumab were required before randomization). Maintenance continued until progression or unacceptable toxicity.

The maintenance comparison is conditional on non-progression through induction; it should not be extrapolated to patients who progress during first-line chemo-immunotherapy.

Key efficacy outcomes

At median OS follow-up of 15.9 months in the combination arm and 16.1 months in the control arm, median OS was 21.1 versus 12.7 months (HR 0.42, 95% CI 0.32–0.54; P<0.001). Median PFS, with median follow-up 13.9 months, was 7.4 versus 1.9 months (HR 0.34, 95% CI 0.28–0.42; P<0.001).

Absolute effect and NNT/NNH

At 12 months, estimated OS was approximately 79% versus 53%, an absolute survival difference of 26 percentage points. The landmark NNT is 4 (ceil[100/26]=4) to have one additional patient alive at 12 months from maintenance randomization. This is an NNT derived from Kaplan–Meier survival estimates, not from median OS or the hazard ratio.

No NNH is calculated. Grade ≥3 adverse-event rates are cumulative over unequal treatment exposure and are not reported as a common fixed-time risk difference. The 12-month PFS landmark is displayed above; no separate PFS NNT is assigned in this page.

Toxicity, treatment burden and quality of life

Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 46.8% with tarlatamab–durvalumab and 40.6% with durvalumab. Cytokine-release syndrome (CRS) occurred in 49.6% versus 1.4%; grade 3 CRS was uncommon (0.4%). Immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome was reported in approximately 1.1%. Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events were 20.9% versus 10.8% in the reported analysis. CRS monitoring, step-up dosing and infusion logistics are clinically relevant treatment burdens.

Patient-reported quality-of-life results were not available in the primary interim publication. The accessible report does not provide a single fixed-horizon, exposure-adjusted harm endpoint suitable for NNH calculation.

Interpretation and limitations

  • The OS result is statistically strong and clinically large, but follow-up remains relatively short for a maintenance strategy and the OS analysis is interim.
  • Only patients without progression after first-line durvalumab–platinum–etoposide induction were randomized; the result does not answer how to treat induction-refractory disease.
  • The control was durvalumab maintenance. DeLLphi-305 is not a head-to-head comparison against other maintenance strategies such as lurbinectedin-containing approaches.
  • CRS, step-up dosing, neurologic monitoring and additional infusion visits need to be weighed against survival benefit.
  • QoL and longer-term safety remain important follow-up needs.

Clinical interpretation and current-practice context · reviewed 16 November 2026

DeLLphi-305 supports adding tarlatamab to durvalumab maintenance for selected ES-SCLC patients who have completed first-line chemo-immunotherapy without progression. The trial does not support substituting this regimen for induction therapy or applying it to patients who progressed during induction. There is no direct comparison with other maintenance strategies. NCCN full-guideline recommendation category and exact reference mapping were not verified in this run.
