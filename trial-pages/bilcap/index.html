Biliary Tract Cancer · Medical · Adjuvant / Postoperative

BILCAP

After macroscopically complete resection of biliary tract cancer, does adjuvant capecitabine improve survival compared with observation?

Trial design and population

Population / eligibility
447 patients after macroscopically complete resection of cholangiocarcinoma or muscle-invasive gallbladder cancer.
Intervention vs comparator
Capecitabine 1,250 mg/m² orally twice daily on days 1–14 every 21 days for 8 cycles vs observation.
Sample size
447
Primary endpoint
Overall survival

Key results

Long-term ITT median OS 49.6 months with capecitabine vs 36.1 months with observation (adjusted HR 0.84; 95% CI 0.67–1.06). Prespecified sensitivity analysis adjusted for minimization factors, nodal status, grade and sex: HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.59–0.94). The primary ITT analysis did not reach conventional statistical significance.

Safety and treatment burden

Among capecitabine-treated patients, 44% had at least one grade 3 toxicity; common grade 3 toxicities included hand-foot syndrome (20%), diarrhoea (8%) and fatigue (8%).

Follow-up, maturity and limitations

Interpret the negative primary ITT analysis alongside prespecified sensitivity/per-protocol analyses and long-term follow-up. Toxicity rates were collected asymmetrically in the original study.

Clinical interpretation

Capecitabine is widely used as adjuvant standard of care, supported by guideline recommendations and the sensitivity/per-protocol analyses; communicate that the original ITT primary endpoint was not statistically significant.
