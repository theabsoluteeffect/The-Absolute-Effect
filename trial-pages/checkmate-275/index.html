Bladder Cancer · Medical · Platinum-Refractory Metastatic · Immunotherapy · 2026-09-18

CheckMate 275

Can nivolumab produce clinically meaningful responses after platinum therapy in metastatic or unresectable urothelial carcinoma, and are responses restricted to PD-L1-positive tumours?

Population
270 treated patients with metastatic or surgically unresectable urothelial carcinoma progressing after at least one platinum-based regimen; 265 were evaluable for activity.
Intervention
Nivolumab 3 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks until progression/toxicity.
Comparator
Single-arm study; no randomized comparator.
Sample size / primary endpoint
270 treated patients; Confirmed objective response rate

Absolute Effect

Primary efficacy result
ORR 19.6% (95% CI 15.0–24.9%) overall
Median OS follow-up 7.0 months at the primary report.

OS was immature at the primary analysis; the study was not powered for a randomized OS comparison.

No comparative PFS effect can be calculated because this was a single-arm study.

NNT / NNH
NNT: not applicable; single-arm response data do not support a treatment-vs-control NNT.
NNH: not applicable; there is no randomized control risk from which to calculate an NNH.

Treatment burden and toxicity

Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 18%; three treatment-related deaths were attributed to pneumonitis, acute respiratory failure and cardiovascular failure.

Quality of life: The study was not designed primarily around comparative QoL; treatment discontinuation and immune-related toxicity were clinically important.

Subgroups / clinically important modifiers

ORR was 28.4% with PD-L1 ≥5%, 23.8% with PD-L1 ≥1%, and 16.1% with PD-L1 <1%, demonstrating responses irrespective of PD-L1 expression.

Limitations and maturity

Single-arm phase II design, short initial OS follow-up and absence of a randomized comparator limit causal absolute-effect inference.

Clinical interpretation

CheckMate 275 established that nivolumab can produce durable responses after platinum failure, including in PD-L1-low tumours, but its single-arm design prevents a conventional NNT/NNH treatment-effect calculation.

The trial is best understood as response-evidence that helped establish checkpoint blockade in platinum-refractory urothelial cancer; later randomized trials are required to quantify comparative survival benefit.
