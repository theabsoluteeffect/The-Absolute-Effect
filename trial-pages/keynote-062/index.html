Clinical question: In first-line her2-negative advanced gastric/gej adenocarcinoma, pd-l1 cps ≥1, does the investigational strategy improve clinically meaningful outcomes versus its control?

Registry: NCT02494583 · One underlying study; aliases and follow-up publications consolidated.

Phase III, randomized, open-label, three-arm trial; 763 patients. Evidence cut-off: 13 November 2026.

Design, population and eligibility

Design / sample size

Phase III, randomized, open-label, three-arm trial; 763 patients.

Eligibility

Previously untreated locally advanced unresectable or metastatic HER2-negative gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma with PD-L1 CPS ≥1.

Intervention

Pembrolizumab alone; pembrolizumab + cisplatin/fluoropyrimidine; or chemotherapy alone.

Pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks; chemotherapy was cisplatin plus 5-FU or capecitabine per protocol.

Comparator

As described in the study design; see primary publication.

Primary endpoint: OS and PFS with hierarchical testing; primary comparisons included pembrolizumab monotherapy and pembrolizumab-chemotherapy versus chemotherapy.

Key efficacy outcomes

CPS ≥1: pembrolizumab OS 10.6 vs 11.1 months, HR 0.91 (99.2% CI 0.69–1.18), meeting non-inferiority but not superiority. Pembrolizumab + chemotherapy OS 12.5 vs 11.1 months, HR 0.85 (95% CI 0.70–1.03); superiority not demonstrated. CPS ≥10 monotherapy OS 17.4 vs 10.8 months, HR 0.69, but this comparison was not formally tested after hierarchy failure.

Safety and treatment delivery

Grade 3–5 TRAEs: pembrolizumab 17%, pembrolizumab-chemotherapy 73%, chemotherapy 69%.

Clinical interpretation and limitations

A critical negative/discordant first-line study. Do not claim pembrolizumab-chemotherapy superiority or definitive CPS ≥10 monotherapy superiority from the exploratory/un-tested subgroup. Monotherapy non-inferiority is not superiority.

Interpret the reported hazard ratios in the context of the prespecified endpoint, statistical hierarchy, follow-up maturity, crossover/subsequent therapy and enrolled population. Median survival differences are not risk differences and must not be converted into NNT.

Absolute effect

No NNT/NNH is displayed unless an absolute event-risk difference at a clearly stated fixed timepoint can be calculated from reported trial data. Median survival differences and hazard ratios alone do not support an NNT. The visual above uses reported outcome data only.
