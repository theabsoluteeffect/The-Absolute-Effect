ImmunoPRISM

ImmunoPRISM reported deep responses and a PFS signal with fixed-duration teclistamab versus lenalidomide–dexamethasone in high-risk smouldering myeloma; the small control arm and safety run-in require careful interpretation.

Study identity and design

Open-label, randomized phase II trial. Sixty-four patients were enrolled; 59 had received at least one treatment cycle at the 26 May 2026 cutoff (45 teclistamab-treated, including six safety-run-in patients; 14 Rd). The randomized population comprised 39 teclistamab and 14 Rd participants.

High-risk smouldering myeloma defined by IMWG 20/2/20 criteria and/or additional high-risk clinical/cytogenetic features. Baseline imaging was performed to exclude myeloma-defining bone disease. Median age was 65 years; 64% met the 20/2/20 high-risk category.

Intervention and comparator

Experimental: Subcutaneous teclistamab with step-up, then weekly and every-other-week dosing. The safety run-in schema was 720 μg/kg weekly in cycle 1, 1.5 mg/kg weekly for two cycles, 3 mg/kg every 2 weeks for four cycles, then 3 mg/kg monthly; after amendment, maximum 12 cycles or 15 months. The exact randomized-cohort step-up details are in the study supplement.

Comparator: Lenalidomide 25 mg days 1–21 plus dexamethasone 40 mg days 1, 8 and 15 for cycles 1–12; lenalidomide 15 mg days 1–21 for cycles 13–24. Dexamethasone could be reduced or stopped for intolerance.

Key efficacy outcome

Primary endpoint — randomized population: CR or better in 29/39 (74%) with teclistamab versus 0/14 with Rd at median follow-up 24.5 months. The accessible primary text does not report a p value for this primary comparison. In an expanded all-teclistamab-treated analysis including the six safety-run-in patients, CR or better was 35/45 (77.8%).

Secondary PFS: median PFS not reached versus 20.3 months; HR 0.15 (95% CI 0.04–0.59), p=0.007. The all-treated Kaplan–Meier 2-year PFS estimates were 92% (95% CI 84–100) versus 49% (95% CI 26–91); results were reported as consistent in the randomized-only analysis. MRD negativity at 10⁻⁵ was 37/45 (82.2%) versus 0/14.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade 3/4 neutropenia occurred in 16/45 (35.6%) versus 4/14 (28.6%); grade 3 infections in 9/45 (20%) versus 3/14 (21.4%); no grade 4 infections were observed. CRS occurred in 32/45 (71.1%) with teclistamab (29 grade 1, 3 grade 2; no grade ≥3 CRS); no ICANS was reported. No deaths occurred in either arm.

Quality of life: No patient-reported quality-of-life outcome was reported in the primary manuscript.

Treatment burden / discontinuation: At cutoff, 8/45 teclistamab-treated patients remained on treatment, 30 completed protocol-defined cycles and 7 discontinued early (progression 2, adverse events 3, patient/physician decision 2). In the Rd arm, 6/14 discontinued (CRAB progression 3, adverse events 2, decision 1). Step-up dosing was initially inpatient; selected patients later completed it outpatient.

Absolute effect and NNT / NNH

NNT is not calculated. CR was assessed over variable follow-up, and the published 2-year PFS rates are from an all-treated analysis that includes a nonrandomized safety run-in; no comparable randomized-only fixed-time risk difference is reported.

Interpretation and limitations

The trial shows unusually deep responses and a strong PFS signal in HR-SMM, but it is small, open-label and has a 14-patient control arm. Daratumumab was approved for HR-SMM in 2025, so Rd is a previous rather than necessarily current standard comparator. These data are hypothesis-generating and do not establish teclistamab as routine early-intervention therapy.

Limitations: Small phase II study; six-patient safety run-in included in expanded efficacy/safety analyses; protocol duration changed; no OS events and no QoL data; primary CR p value not reported in the accessible text; comparator may be superseded.
