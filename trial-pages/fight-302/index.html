Biliary Tract Cancer · Medical · Advanced / Metastatic · First-line FGFR2-rearranged advanced cholangiocarcinoma

FIGHT-302

In untreated advanced FGFR2-rearranged cholangiocarcinoma, does pemigatinib improve progression-free survival over gemcitabine-cisplatin?

Registry: NCT03656536 · Evidence reviewed 9 November 2026 · Aliases and follow-up publications consolidated.

Phase III randomized global trial; n=167; early closure after standard-of-care change.Primary-source evidence summary

Study design, population and eligibility

Design and sample

Phase III, open-label, global randomized active-controlled trial; 167 patients randomized 1:1 (pemigatinib n=83; gemcitabine/cisplatin n=84) across 79 sites. 4,563 were prescreened and 196 screened. The study closed early after a change in first-line standard of care.

Population

Adults with histologically/cytologically confirmed unresectable, locally advanced or metastatic cholangiocarcinoma with documented FGFR2 rearrangement. 98% had intrahepatic primary tumours and 78% had distant metastases; ECOG 0–1. One urgent cycle of gemcitabine/cisplatin before randomization was permitted.

Randomization/stratification: 1:1; stratified by prior receipt of one GemCis cycle, geographic region and tumour burden (locally advanced vs metastatic). Safety population was 83 vs 73 because 11 randomized chemotherapy patients withdrew before receiving treatment.

Treatment and comparator

Intervention: pemigatinib 13.5 mg orally once daily continuously in a 21-day cycle; uptitration to 18 mg after cycle 1 was permitted if phosphate was <5.5 mg/dL. This is continuous dosing, not the 2-weeks-on/1-week-off schedule used in FIGHT-202.

Comparator: gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m² IV plus cisplatin 25 mg/m² IV on days 1 and 8 every 3 weeks for up to 8 cycles. Crossover to second-line pemigatinib was allowed after centrally confirmed progression on chemotherapy; 42/73 treated chemotherapy patients (58%) crossed over.

Endpoints and follow-up

Primary endpoint: progression-free survival by independent central review (ICR) per RECIST v1.1. Secondary endpoints: objective response rate, duration of response, disease-control rate and overall survival. Health-related quality of life was a secondary endpoint; the primary paper states that those results will be published separately.

Radiologic assessments were performed every 9 weeks; survival follow-up every 12 weeks. The trial ran from 3 June 2019 to 15 July 2025.

Key efficacy and clinical outcomes

Median PFS: 8.3 vs 6.8 months; HR 0.58 (95% CI 0.39–0.87), nominal P=0.0078. PFS estimates were 39% vs 17% at 12 months and 21% vs 9% at 24 months.

ORR: 39/83 (47.0%; 95% CI 35.9–58.3) vs 13/84 (15.5%; 95% CI 8.5–25.0); odds ratio 5.57 (95% CI 2.54–12.24), P<0.0001. DOR: 14.2 vs 6.3 months; HR 0.40 (95% CI 0.16–1.01), P=0.0526. DCR: 74/83 (89.2%) vs 57/84 (67.9%); OR 4.10 (95% CI 1.76–9.54), P=0.0007.

OS: median 24.4 vs 25.0 months; the publication describes OS as comparable. Interpretation is confounded by crossover and extensive post-progression FGFR-inhibitor use. Median PFS and OS are time-to-event medians, not fixed-time event risks.

Toxicity and treatment burden

Safety population: pemigatinib 83; gemcitabine/cisplatin 73. Grade ≥3 TEAEs occurred in 65/83 (78.3%) vs 49/73 (67.1%); serious TEAEs in 23/83 (27.7%) vs 17/73 (23.3%). TEAEs leading to treatment discontinuation occurred in 5/83 (6.0%) vs 8/73 (11.0%); dose interruption in 70/83 (84.3%) vs 47/73 (64.4%).

Common pemigatinib TEAEs included hyperphosphatemia 68/83 (81.9%), alopecia 56.6%, palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia 47.0% and stomatitis 45.8%. Three fatal TEAEs (3.6%) occurred in the pemigatinib group; none was considered treatment-related. Grade ≥3 events were mainly palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia, stomatitis and nail disorder with pemigatinib, and hematologic toxicity with GemCis.

Absolute effect and NNT/NNH

At 12 months, PFS was 39% vs 17%: absolute benefit 22 percentage points; approximate landmark NNT 5 (ceil[100/22]) for one additional patient progression-free at 12 months. At 24 months, PFS was 21% vs 9%: absolute benefit 12 points; approximate landmark NNT 9 (ceil[100/12]). These are based on the published Kaplan–Meier landmark estimates, not on median PFS or the hazard ratio.

No NNT is calculated from median PFS/OS or ORR because ORR is best response over variable follow-up rather than a fixed-time risk. No NNH is calculated from aggregate TEAEs because the reported comparison is a cumulative treatment-period safety measure.

Critical interpretation and limitations

Small sample and early closure; OS confounded by crossover/subsequent FGFR inhibitors. Distinct from FIGHT-202.

This page is an evidence summary, not a substitute for multidisciplinary assessment or patient-specific decision-making.

Current-practice context

Review date: 15 November 2026. FIGHT-302 compared first-line pemigatinib with gemcitabine/cisplatin, the comparator standard when the study began. It does not compare pemigatinib with contemporary gemcitabine/cisplatin plus immune-checkpoint inhibition; its PFS result should not be interpreted as superiority over current chemoimmunotherapy. The trial supports activity of FGFR2-directed therapy in this molecular subgroup, with OS limited by early closure and post-progression crossover/FGFR inhibitor use. Current NCCN/ESMO version and sequencing recommendations remain under separate review; no guideline version is claimed here.
