The Absolute Effect

NSABP B-27

Breast Cancer · Neoadjuvant Therapy

Clinical question

Does adding docetaxel to neoadjuvant doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide improve response and disease outcomes in operable breast cancer?

Trial context

NSABP B-27 was a large randomized neoadjuvant breast cancer trial evaluating the addition of docetaxel after doxorubicin plus cyclophosphamide. The study is foundational for understanding how improving tumour response before surgery does not necessarily produce an overall survival benefit across an unselected population.

Key findings

Adding docetaxel substantially increased clinical and pathologic response, including pathologic complete response. However, the improvement in response did not translate into a uniform overall survival advantage for the entire randomized population.

Absolute Effect perspective

The important distinction is between treatment response and patient-important outcomes. Pathologic complete response is prognostically meaningful, but it is not interchangeable with event-free or overall survival. The absolute benefit of neoadjuvant escalation should therefore be interpreted in the context of tumour subtype, baseline risk and subsequent treatment.

Clinical takeaway

NSABP B-27 supports neoadjuvant chemotherapy as an effective way to increase tumour response and facilitate surgical downstaging. It does not support the assumption that greater pathologic response automatically means a survival benefit for every patient.
