Breast Cancer · Neoadjuvant Therapy

NSABP B-27

Adding docetaxel to neoadjuvant doxorubicin plus cyclophosphamide

Clinical question

Does adding docetaxel to neoadjuvant doxorubicin and cyclophosphamide improve response and disease outcomes in operable breast cancer?

Trial at a glance

Design
Large randomized neoadjuvant trial
Population
Operable breast cancer
Intervention
Docetaxel added after doxorubicin + cyclophosphamide
Key outcomes
Clinical response, pathologic complete response, survival

Key findings

Adding docetaxel substantially increased clinical and pathologic response, including pathologic complete response. However, the improvement in response did not translate into a uniform overall survival advantage for the entire randomized population.

Absolute Effect perspective
More tumour response does not automatically mean longer survival

Pathologic complete response is prognostically meaningful, but it is not interchangeable with event-free or overall survival. The absolute benefit of neoadjuvant escalation should be interpreted in the context of tumour subtype, baseline risk and subsequent treatment.

Clinical takeaway

NSABP B-27 supports neoadjuvant chemotherapy as an effective way to increase tumour response and facilitate surgical downstaging. It does not support assuming that greater pathologic response automatically means a survival benefit for every patient.
