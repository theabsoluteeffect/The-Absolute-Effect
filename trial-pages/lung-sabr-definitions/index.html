Early-stage lung SABR: definitions, dose and OAR constraints

Protocol-focused reference for ROSEL, STARS, CHISEL, SPACE, LUSTRE, HILUS, RTOG 0813 and SUNSET. Dose constraints below are protocol/trial-specific; they are not interchangeable planning recommendations.

Terminology warning: There is no single universal definition of central or ultracentral lung lesions. Always preserve the definition used by the individual study. “Peripheral” often means outside a 2-cm zone around the proximal bronchial tree (PBT), but studies used different anatomic landmarks and target-volume rules.

Study details and primary sources

ROSEL

Randomized surgery versus SABR trial in stage IA NSCLC; closed early with very small accrual. Only tumours at least 2 cm from hilar structures were eligible. SABR schedules were 54 Gy/3 fractions or 60 Gy/5 fractions depending on centre protocol.

Primary pooled analysis: Chang et al., Lancet Oncology.

STARS

Randomized study of CyberKnife SABR versus surgical resection. The pooled STARS/ROSEL report describes 54 Gy/3 fractions for peripheral lesions and 50 Gy/4 fractions for central lesions in STARS. Do not conflate this with the later single-arm STARS trial, which used a different protocol.

CHISEL / TROG 09.02

Peripheral stage I NSCLC; SABR improved local control over standard radiotherapy. SABR was 54 Gy/3 fractions, or 48 Gy/4 fractions for tumours less than 2 cm from chest wall. This chest-wall adaptation is not a centrality definition.

SPACE

Randomized phase II study comparing SBRT with conventional radiotherapy in medically inoperable stage I NSCLC. SBRT schedule: 66 Gy/3 fractions; comparator: 70 Gy/35 fractions. The study did not establish a universal central/ultracentral definition.

LUSTRE

Phase III randomized trial. Central was defined as within 1 cm of mediastinum and/or within 2 cm of PBT. SBRT was 48 Gy/4 fractions for peripheral and 60 Gy/8 for central disease versus 60 Gy/15 fractions. Three-year local control was 87.6% versus 81.2% (HR 0.61; p=.15); the difference was not statistically significant. One grade-5 hemoptysis occurred in an ultracentral SBRT case.

HILUS

Prospective phase II study of ultracentral tumours ≤1 cm from PBT; 56 Gy/8 fractions prescribed to the 67% isodose. Two-year local control was 83%; 22/65 patients had grade 3–5 toxicity, including 10 treatment-related deaths. This trial is a toxicity caution, not a default prescription template.

RTOG 0813

Phase I/II dose-escalation for central NSCLC using 50–60 Gy/5 fractions. Published summaries report PBT, esophagus, great vessels and heart/pericardium maximum doses up to 105% of prescription, spinal cord Dmax 30 Gy and brachial plexus Dmax 32 Gy. Check the protocol version for all volume constraints and contour definitions.

SUNSET

Prospective phase I/II study in ultracentral NSCLC; 60 Gy/8 fractions with hotspot limited to 120% within PTV; endobronchial invasion excluded. Three-year local control was 89.6%. Grade 3–5 adverse events occurred in 2/30 patients (6.7%), including one grade-5 pneumonia in a patient with imaging consistent with background interstitial lung disease.

How to read the trial visualisations

Each panel displays the endpoint and time point reported by that study. Bars are descriptive proportions, not reconstructed Kaplan–Meier curves. Do not compare percentages across trials without accounting for eligibility, tumour location, follow-up, endpoint definitions, prescription isodose and competing mortality.

Important: A 3-year local-control estimate, a crude failure proportion at data cut-off, and a dose-limiting-toxicity probability are different statistical quantities. The labels above deliberately retain those distinctions.

How to use this page safely

  • Centrality is determined from the trial’s stated landmark (GTV, PTV, PBT, mediastinum, esophagus or vessels); do not substitute a modern definition retrospectively.
  • OAR dose metrics are not interchangeable: Dmax, D0.03cc, D10cc and Vx describe different quantities. Confirm whether constraints refer to point dose, small-volume dose, or organ volume.
  • Prescription dose alone does not establish a safe plan. Use the full protocol, current institutional constraints, contouring definitions, dose calculation and image-guidance requirements.
  • Where this page says “protocol-specific; verify”, exact thresholds were not reproduced here rather than guessed. It is a literature index, not a substitute for the source protocol.
