In HER2-negative breast cancer with residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant anthracycline/taxane chemotherapy, does adjuvant capecitabine improve disease-free and overall survival?

Population
910 patients with HER2-negative residual invasive breast cancer after neoadjuvant chemotherapy.
Intervention / comparator
Capecitabine for 6–8 cycles versus standard postoperative therapy without capecitabine.
Primary/major efficacy result
5-year DFS: 74.1% vs 67.6%; absolute improvement 6.5 points; NNT 16.
Overall survival
5-year OS: 89.2% vs 83.6%; absolute improvement 5.6 points; NNT 18.

Absolute Effect

5-year DFS: 74.1% vs 67.6%; absolute improvement 6.5 points; NNT 16.

Clinical interpretation: The clearest benefit was in triple-negative disease: 5-year DFS 69.8% vs 56.1% and OS 78.8% vs 70.3%. Benefit in hormone-receptor-positive disease was smaller and imprecise.

Toxicity / treatment burden: Hand-foot syndrome occurred in 73.4% with capecitabine. A single defensible NNH for clinically central toxicity is not established from the reported fixed-time data.

Important limitations

Open-label design and treatment evolution after progression can affect absolute survival comparisons. Absolute effects are reported only where a reliable common timepoint or directly reported rate permits calculation; otherwise NNT/NNH is not forced from medians or hazard ratios.

Primary source

Masuda N, et al. N Engl J Med. 2017;376:2147-2159.
