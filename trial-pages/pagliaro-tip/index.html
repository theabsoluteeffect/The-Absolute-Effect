The Absolute Effect / Penile Cancer / Medical

Pagliaro TIP

Clinical question

In men with bulky regional penile squamous cell carcinoma without distant metastases, can neoadjuvant paclitaxel, ifosfamide and cisplatin produce enough tumour response to enable consolidative surgery?

Population / design

30 men with penile SCC and bulky regional nodal disease (cN2–cN3); prospective single-arm phase II study. This is not a randomized comparison.

Treatment

Neoadjuvant TIP: paclitaxel, ifosfamide and cisplatin for four planned cycles, followed by surgical consolidation in eligible responders.

Absolute effect

Objective response occurred in 15/30 (50%); 22/30 (73.3%) underwent surgery and 3/30 (10%) achieved a pathologic complete response. Median time to progression was 8.1 months and median OS 17.1 months.

NNT is not defensible: there was no randomized comparator and therefore no causal absolute treatment effect can be calculated.

Harm / burden

Reported grade 3–4 toxicities included anemia, neutropenia, febrile neutropenia and peripheral neuropathy, each occurring in fewer than 5% in the published phase II report.

Interpretation

Pagliaro TIP is important benchmark evidence for multimodal management of bulky regional penile cancer, but its single-arm design means response and survival cannot be attributed causally to TIP.

Primary source

JCO 2010 phase II study.
