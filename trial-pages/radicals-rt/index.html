Prostate Cancer · Radiation · Post-prostatectomy

RADICALS-RT

After radical prostatectomy, does routine adjuvant radiotherapy improve outcomes compared with observation and early salvage radiotherapy triggered by PSA recurrence?

PopulationMen with ≥1 risk factor for biochemical progression: pT3/4, Gleason 7–10, positive margins or preoperative PSA ≥10 ng/mL.Randomized1,396 men: 697 adjuvant RT and 699 observation with salvage RT.Salvage triggerPSA ≥0.1 ng/mL or three consecutive rises.Primary conceptCompare immediate RT with a strategy that reserves RT for biochemical progression.

Absolute Effect

5-year biochemical progression-free survival
85% adjuvant vs 88% salvage

HR 1.10 (95% CI 0.81–1.49), P=.56. There was no evidence that routine adjuvant RT improved this endpoint.

10-year freedom from distant metastases
93% vs 90% (+3 points)

Long-term follow-up reported HR 0.68 (95% CI 0.43–1.07), P=.095, with no overall-survival improvement.

Toxicity / treatment burden

Urinary incontinence was worse with adjuvant RT at 1 year. Grade 3–4 urethral stricture within 2 years occurred in 6% vs 4%. Long-term patient-reported urinary and faecal incontinence remained worse in the adjuvant group.

Only a minority of men in the salvage-policy arm ultimately required RT, meaning the strategy avoided immediate RT for many patients.

Clinical interpretation

RADICALS-RT supports an observation policy with early salvage RT rather than routine immediate postoperative RT for many men with adverse pathological features.

The trial does not show that salvage RT is unnecessary; it shows that exposing every patient to immediate RT did not improve the principal disease-control outcomes sufficiently to offset the additional morbidity.

NNT / NNH

No efficacy NNT is calculated because the 5-year biochemical endpoint numerically favoured the salvage policy and the 10-year metastasis endpoint was not statistically definitive. A harm metric can be expressed for grade 3–4 urethral stricture: approximately 50 men would need adjuvant rather than salvage-policy treatment to generate one additional stricture based on 6% vs 4%, but this should be interpreted cautiously.
