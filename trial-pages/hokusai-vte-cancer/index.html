Design, population and treatment

Design: Open-label, randomized, non-inferiority phase III trial.

Eligibility and population: 1,050 patients randomized; modified intention-to-treat efficacy population 1,046 patients with active cancer and acute symptomatic or incidental VTE.

Intervention: Edoxaban 60 mg orally once daily after at least 5 days of parenteral anticoagulation; dose reduced to 30 mg for low body weight, creatinine clearance 30–50 mL/min or selected P-glycoprotein inhibitors.

Comparator: Dalteparin 200 IU/kg subcutaneously daily for month 1, then 150 IU/kg daily for months 2–12.

Randomization and endpoint: Composite of recurrent VTE or major bleeding over 6–12 months; recurrent VTE and major bleeding were separately assessed. The full randomization stratification variables and protocol amendments remain subject to protocol-level reconciliation before release.

Key efficacy outcome

The composite outcome occurred in 12.8% versus 13.5% (HR 0.97, 95% CI 0.70–1.36; non-inferiority p=0.006). Recurrent VTE was 7.9% versus 11.3%; superiority was not established for the composite.

Data maturity: Treatment duration 6–12 months; primary outcome assessed through the treatment period. Primary versus secondary endpoints are kept distinct; no survival estimate is inferred from a hazard ratio or median alone.

Safety and absolute effect

Major bleeding occurred in 6.9% versus 4.0%, an absolute increase of 2.9 percentage points (95% CI for difference approximately 0.1–5.6). Point-estimate NNH 35 (ceil[1/0.029]); excess bleeding was concentrated in gastrointestinal cancer.

Delivery and discontinuation: Oral once-daily edoxaban follows an initial parenteral anticoagulant lead-in; dose reduction criteria and GI mucosal bleeding risk are clinically important.

Quality of life and patient experience

No QoL result was verified in the primary report.

Where a separate patient-reported outcome publication exists, it is treated as a secondary analysis attached to this underlying study and not as a separate trial page. Unextracted domains remain explicitly open rather than inferred.

Absolute effect and NNT / NNH

NNH 356–12 months of treatment

Treat approximately 35 patients with the higher-risk strategy rather than the comparator to cause one additional major bleeding event over the stated period.

Absolute harm 2.9 percentage points; NNH = ceil(1 / 0.029) = 35. NNH point estimate; excess major bleeding was concentrated in GI cancer.

Only same-endpoint, same-time-horizon absolute differences are eligible for NNT/NNH. Median survival and hazard ratios alone are not used. Major bleeding (safety endpoint): 2.9 percentage-point absolute harm at 6–12 months of treatment; NNH 35.

Interpretation and limitations

Edoxaban was non-inferior to dalteparin for the composite endpoint but increased major bleeding, particularly in GI cancer. The bleeding signal is central to agent selection in luminal GI malignancy.

Limitations: Non-inferiority design; recurrent VTE reduction should not be interpreted as proven superiority. The NNH is a point estimate for major bleeding over the trial treatment period; confidence limits are wide.
