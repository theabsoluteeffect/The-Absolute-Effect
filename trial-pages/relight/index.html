Gastric / gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma · First-line · Reviewed 17 November 2026

RELIGHT

Does retlirafusp alfa plus CAPOX improve survival compared with CAPOX alone in previously untreated, unresectable HER2-negative gastric or gastroesophageal junction adenocarcinoma?

DesignPhase III · randomized · double-blind
Population731 randomized; HER2-negative advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma
InterventionRetlirafusp alfa 30 mg/kg IV q3w + CAPOX
ComparatorPlacebo + CAPOX
Primary endpointOverall survival in PD-L1 CPS ≥5 and ITT
MaturityPrimary JCO report; 13.6-month follow-up in linked PRO/safety reports

Design, population and treatment

Multicentre, randomized, double-blind phase III study. Part 1 established the recommended SHR-1701 dose; part 2 randomized 731 patients 1:1 to retlirafusp alfa (SHR-1701) plus CAPOX or placebo plus CAPOX. Eligible patients had previously untreated, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic HER2-negative gastric or gastroesophageal-junction adenocarcinoma. Randomization was stratified by PD-L1 CPS (≥5 vs <5), ECOG performance status and peritoneal metastasis.

The primary JCO publication is the principal efficacy source. The ESMO 2024 LBA60 report provides the protocol dose and early efficacy; the 2025 ASCO GI abstract provides additional safety detail; the 2025 ESMO 2108P abstract reports exploratory patient-reported outcomes.

Treatment and comparator

Experimental: Retlirafusp alfa 30 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks plus CAPOX. CAPOX comprised oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² IV on day 1 and capecitabine 1,000 mg/m² orally twice daily on days 1–14 of each 21-day cycle; chemotherapy was given for up to 6 cycles, with retlirafusp continued per protocol.

Comparator: Matching placebo plus the same CAPOX schedule. The protocol dose is reported in the primary ESMO LBA60 conference abstract.

Endpoints and follow-up

Primary endpoint: overall survival in the prespecified PD-L1 CPS ≥5 population and the intention-to-treat population. PFS, objective response and duration of response were secondary efficacy outcomes. The ESMO 2024 data cut (20 May 2024) had median follow-up 8.5 months; the linked 2025 ASCO GI safety/PRO report described a Kaplan–Meier median follow-up of 13.6 months. The 2026 JCO publication reports the later survival analysis.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events were 62.6% with retlirafusp plus CAPOX and 59.0% with placebo plus CAPOX in the 2025 ASCO GI safety analysis. Serious treatment-related adverse events were 34.9% versus 24.0%; treatment-related deaths were 1.9% versus 1.1%. The ASCO GI report also found lower rates of grade ≥3 thrombocytopenia (19.0% vs 28.1%), neutropenia (12.1% vs 16.4%) and leukopenia (3.6% vs 5.2%).

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

An exploratory ESMO 2025 PRO analysis included 720 patients (QLQ-C30 n=360 per arm; QLQ-STO22 n=359/361), with compliance above 95%. Time to deterioration favoured retlirafusp for global health status/QoL (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.61–0.95), nausea/vomiting (HR 0.79, 0.64–0.98), pain (HR 0.71, 0.56–0.90), gastric pain (HR 0.59, 0.46–0.76) and reflux (HR 0.76, 0.61–0.95). At week 12, abdominal pain and hair-loss scores showed small nominal improvements; other subscales were similar. These were exploratory patient-reported outcomes.

Absolute effect and NNT/NNH

No NNT/NNH is calculated. The primary report supplies median OS/PFS and hazard ratios, not a comparable fixed-time absolute event difference used here. Median survival and hazard ratios are not converted into NNT.

Interpretation and limitations

RELIGHT provides phase III evidence of an OS and PFS advantage for retlirafusp alfa plus CAPOX in HER2-negative advanced G/GEJ adenocarcinoma. The control was CAPOX alone. The protocol and trial design predated widespread adoption of first-line PD-1 inhibitor plus chemotherapy in many jurisdictions; therefore, the study does not establish superiority over contemporary chemo-immunotherapy combinations. The CPS ≥5 and ITT populations should be reported separately, and the exploratory PRO findings should not be interpreted as proof of a broad QoL benefit.
