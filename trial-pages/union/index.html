Rectal cancer · Multimodality Oncology · Phase III

UNION

Multimodality neoadjuvant treatment for locally advanced rectal cancer. This page separates the primary endpoint from secondary outcomes and distinguishes source-reported absolute rates from time-to-event estimates.

DesignRandomized phase III multimodality trial
Population231 patients with locally advanced rectal cancer randomized between short-course radiotherapy plus immunochemotherapy and long-course chemoradiotherapy-based treatment.
Primary endpointPathological complete response; event-free survival was a key longer-term endpoint.
Data maturityThree-year EFS reported; longer-term OS and late toxicity follow-up continue.

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 231 patients with locally advanced rectal cancer randomized between short-course radiotherapy plus immunochemotherapy and long-course chemoradiotherapy-based treatment.

Intervention: Short-course radiotherapy 25 Gy in 5 fractions followed by camrelizumab 200 mg every 3 weeks plus CAPOX (oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² day 1 and capecitabine 1000 mg/m² twice daily days 1–14), then surgery and protocol adjuvant therapy.

Comparator: Long-course chemoradiotherapy approximately 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions with capecitabine, followed by CAPOX and surgery according to protocol.

Primary endpoint: Pathological complete response; event-free survival was a key longer-term endpoint.

Follow-up and maturity: Three-year EFS reported; longer-term OS and late toxicity follow-up continue.

Registry: NCT04928807. Randomization, analysis populations and endpoint definitions should be read from the linked primary report and protocol; no denominator is inferred where the abstract does not state one.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade ≥3 adverse events were approximately 37% versus 29%; hematologic, gastrointestinal, radiation and immune-related toxicities require integrated review.

NNT 5Pathological complete response · At surgery

39.8% versus 15.3%; absolute benefit 24.5 percentage points. No viable tumour in the resection specimen. Calculation: ceil(100 / (39.8 − 15.3)) = ceil(4.08) = 5.

Quality of life and treatment burden

No definitive QoL advantage was reported in the primary abstract.

Interpretation and limitations

UNION supports a short-course RT plus camrelizumab/CAPOX strategy in the studied locally advanced rectal cancer population. It should not be interpreted as proof that immunotherapy alone accounts for the benefit.

Complex multimodality comparison changes radiotherapy fractionation, immunotherapy and systemic treatment together; the contribution of each component cannot be isolated.

Superiority, non-inferiority, surrogate response and mature survival are not interchangeable. The page preserves the primary endpoint hierarchy and does not infer a survival advantage from response or PFS alone.
