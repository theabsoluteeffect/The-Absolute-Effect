Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 231 patients with locally advanced rectal cancer randomized between short-course RT followed by camrelizumab/CAPOX and standard long-course chemoradiotherapy-based care.

Intervention: Short-course radiotherapy 25 Gy in 5 fractions, followed by two cycles of camrelizumab 200 mg day 1 plus CAPOX (oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² day 1; capecitabine 1000 mg/m² twice daily days 1–14), then surgery and protocol adjuvant CAPOX plus camrelizumab.

Comparator: Long-course chemoradiotherapy 50.4 Gy in 28 fractions with capecitabine 825 mg/m² twice daily on radiotherapy days, followed by surgery and protocol adjuvant CAPOX.

Primary endpoint: Pathological complete response; event-free survival was a prespecified interim endpoint.

Follow-up and maturity: Prespecified interim analysis with three-year EFS; longer-term OS and late toxicity follow-up continue.

Registry: NCT04928807. Arm denominators and endpoint definitions are preserved as reported; no missing denominator is inferred.

pCR 39.8% versus 15.3% (absolute difference 24.5 points; NNT 5). Three-year EFS 91.3% versus 77.4%; HR 0.33 (95% CI 0.15–0.71), P=0.0029, crossing the prespecified interim boundary. Absolute EFS difference 13.9 points; NNT 8.

Safety and absolute effect

Common grade 3–4 events included lymphopenia (20% versus 28%), neutropenia (12% versus 11%), diarrhoea (8% versus 3%) and nausea (8% versus 2%). One treatment-related death from radiation enteritis was reported in the control strategy.

Quality of life and treatment burden

No definitive QoL advantage was reported in the interim analysis.

Interpretation and limitations

UNION supports the studied short-course RT/camrelizumab/CAPOX strategy in locally advanced rectal cancer. The staged page's previous adjuvant schedule and overall grade ≥3 toxicity rates were not source-faithful and have been corrected.

The experimental strategy changes RT fractionation, immunotherapy and systemic treatment together; the contribution of each component cannot be isolated. EFS is an interim analysis and should not be called mature OS.
