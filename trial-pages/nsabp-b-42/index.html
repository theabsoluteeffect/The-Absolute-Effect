The Absolute Effect / Evidence / NSABP B-42

Breast Cancer · Medical · Stage I–IIIA · Extended adjuvant endocrine therapy

NSABP B-42

In postmenopausal women with hormone-receptor-positive early breast cancer who remain disease-free after five years of endocrine therapy, does extended letrozole improve disease-free survival compared with placebo?

Trial registry: NCT00382070 · One underlying study; follow-up publications consolidated.

Design, population and eligibility

Design

Double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III randomized trial.

Population

3,966 postmenopausal women; stage I–IIIA hormone-receptor-positive breast cancer.

Eligibility

Disease-free after five years of AI-based endocrine therapy or tamoxifen for no more than three years followed by an AI; prior surgery and indicated local treatment completed.

Maturity

Ten-year update; median follow-up approximately 10.3 years.

Treatment and comparator

Letrozole 2.5 mg orally once daily for five additional years.

Placebo once daily for five years.

Primary endpoint: DFS: recurrence, second primary malignancy or death as defined by protocol.

Other endpoints: OS, breast cancer-free interval, distant recurrence, osteoporotic fractures and arterial thrombotic events.

Key efficacy results

Ten-year DFS was 75.9% with letrozole versus 72.6% with placebo; HR 0.85 (95% CI 0.74–0.96), P=0.01.

Ten-year OS was not improved: HR 0.97 (95% CI 0.82–1.15), P=0.74.

Ten-year absolute reductions in breast cancer-free interval events and distant recurrence were reported; no significant increase in osteoporotic fractures or arterial thrombotic events was observed in the 10-year update.

Absolute effect

Absolute ten-year DFS improvement was 3.3 percentage points. Approximate NNT = 30 over ten years (1/0.033), endpoint DFS. There was no OS benefit.

NNT: Approximately 30 over ten years to prevent one DFS event, using the reported fixed-time DFS estimates.

NNH: Not quantifiable from the reported fracture/arterial-event comparison because no significant excess was demonstrated and a single toxicity endpoint NNH is not justified.

Toxicity, harms and treatment burden

The long-term update did not identify a significant increase in osteoporotic fractures or arterial thrombotic events; this does not remove the need to assess bone health and vascular risk.

The trial included treatment burden of five additional years of daily endocrine therapy, including arthralgia, vasomotor symptoms and adherence burden.

No statistically significant OS improvement was seen. Do not imply a survival benefit from the DFS result.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

Quality-of-life and symptom burden should be weighed against a modest absolute DFS gain. The ten-year update did not establish a broad QoL advantage; numerical QoL effects should be taken from the dedicated patient-reported outcome publication if included.

Subgroups, maturity and limitations

Benefit in DFS was observed across several prespecified strata; absolute benefit varies with baseline recurrence risk.

The population had already tolerated and completed five years of endocrine therapy, limiting generalizability to patients with substantial prior toxicity or poor adherence.

B-42 is distinct from MA.17 and MA.17R; do not combine their denominators or treatment histories.

Clinical interpretation

NSABP B-42 shows a modest but statistically significant ten-year DFS benefit from extended letrozole, without an OS advantage. The 3.3-point absolute DFS gain (NNT approximately 30 at ten years) should be weighed against an additional five years of treatment and individual toxicity risk.

Current-practice context · 1 November 2026 (full guideline freshness review pending): Extended AI therapy is not automatic for every patient. Consider nodal status, tumour size/grade, prior treatment, late-recurrence risk, bone density, competing mortality and patient preference.
