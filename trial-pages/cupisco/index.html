Cancer of unknown primary · Precision / medical oncology · Phase II · 436 randomized

CUPISCO

In unfavourable non-squamous cancer of unknown primary, does comprehensive genomic profiling followed by molecularly guided treatment improve progression-free survival compared with continuing platinum-based chemotherapy after initial disease control?

Design, population and treatment

Open-label, randomized, active-controlled, phase II trial conducted at 159 sites in 34 countries. Patients had previously untreated unfavourable non-squamous CUP, ECOG performance status 0–1, central confirmation of CUP, and disease control after three cycles of investigator-selected platinum-based induction chemotherapy.

Of 636 enrolled patients, 438 achieved disease control and 436 were randomized 3:1: molecularly guided therapy (n=326) or continued chemotherapy (n=110). The safety-evaluable populations in the extended follow-up were 312 and 102, respectively.

Induction treatment and randomized strategies

Induction: three cycles of investigator-choice platinum chemotherapy. Common regimens were carboplatin–paclitaxel, cisplatin–gemcitabine, or carboplatin–gemcitabine; dose and schedule followed protocol-permitted standard regimens and were not uniform across sites.

Molecularly guided arm: comprehensive genomic profiling was reviewed by a molecular tumour board. Patients with actionable genomic alterations or molecular signatures received matched targeted or immunotherapy. Most patients without an actionable alteration received atezolizumab with continuation chemotherapy. The strategy was heterogeneous by design; there is no single uniform dose or schedule for the randomized intervention.

Control: continuation of the same investigator-selected platinum-based chemotherapy for at least three further cycles.

Key efficacy outcome

Primary endpoint: investigator-assessed progression-free survival from randomization. At the primary analysis, median PFS was 6.1 versus 4.4 months (HR 0.72; 95% CI 0.56–0.92; p=0.0079). The extended follow-up reported median follow-up of 37 months and a sustained PFS effect (HR 0.75; 95% CI 0.59–0.95; p=0.0177); medians remained 6.1 versus 4.4 months.

At extended follow-up, median overall survival was 15.2 versus 12.8 months (HR 0.79; 95% CI 0.61–1.02; p=0.0689); this was not statistically significant. The primary paper reported a higher objective response rate with molecularly guided therapy (18% vs 8%; absolute difference 9.6 percentage points, 95% CI 2.4–16.8).

Safety and absolute effect

In the extended safety population (312 vs 102), grade 3–5 adverse events occurred in 205/312 (65.7%) versus 44/102 (43.1%). Because treatment exposure and follow-up differed, the primary safety analysis also reported exposure-adjusted rates: grade 3–5 events 188.9 versus 318.6 per 100 patient-years at risk; treatment-related grade 3–5 events 55.3 versus 51.5 per 100 patient-years.

Serious adverse events occurred in 119/312 (38.1%) versus 15/102 (14.7%); serious treatment-related events in 55/312 (17.6%) versus 7/102 (6.9%). Adverse events led to treatment withdrawal in 84/312 (26.9%) versus 17/102 (16.7%). These figures should not be interpreted as fixed-time NNH estimates.

Quality of life

The extended report did not demonstrate a clear overall time-to-deterioration advantage in global quality of life. Some exploratory molecularly defined subgroups showed differences in self-rated health or general wellbeing; these are not definitive treatment-selection biomarkers.

Current-practice context · reviewed 27 November 2026

Molecularly guided therapy after disease control with platinum-based induction improved PFS in unfavorable non-squamous CUP. This does not justify replacing induction chemotherapy with immediate targeted therapy for all CUP patients; comprehensive molecular profiling and a matched actionable alteration are required. The 2026 follow-up is supportive but does not remove phase II and subgroup limitations. NCCN CUP full guideline/reference list was not accessible.

Interpretation and limitations

CUPISCO supports comprehensive genomic profiling early in the work-up of selected patients with unfavourable non-squamous CUP and disease control after initial platinum chemotherapy. It does not establish benefit for all CUP presentations, favourable or squamous CUP, or patients who progress during induction. The trial also does not prove that atezolizumab plus chemotherapy benefits the large subgroup without an actionable genomic alteration.

Limitations

  • Open-label phase II design with investigator-assessed PFS.
  • Three-to-one randomization and a heterogeneous molecularly guided treatment strategy.
  • Only patients achieving disease control after three induction cycles were randomized; results do not apply to early progressors.
  • Overall-survival difference was not statistically significant at extended follow-up.
  • Two-thirds of the molecularly guided arm had no actionable genomic alteration and received atezolizumab plus chemotherapy; the trial was not powered to isolate the contribution of this regimen.
  • Quality-of-life findings were not a definitive primary endpoint.
