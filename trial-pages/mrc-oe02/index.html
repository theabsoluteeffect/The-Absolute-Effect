In operable esophageal cancer, does two-cycle neoadjuvant cisplatin/5-FU improve survival compared with surgery alone? [MRC OE02]

In operable esophageal cancer, does two-cycle neoadjuvant cisplatin/5-FU improve survival compared with surgery alone?

Population
802 patients with treatment-naïve, resectable esophageal cancer; approximately two thirds had adenocarcinoma and one third squamous carcinoma.
Sample size
802 randomized patients: 400 chemotherapy+surgery and 402 surgery alone.
Intervention vs comparator
Two 3-weekly cycles of cisplatin plus continuous-infusion 5-FU before surgery versus immediate surgery.
Primary endpoint
Primary endpoint: overall survival; resection and disease-free outcomes were important secondary endpoints.

Absolute Effect

Median OS was 16.8 vs 13.3 months; long-term HR 0.84 (95% CI 0.72–0.98), P=.03. Five-year OS was 23.0% vs 17.1%.

Absolute effect: Five-year OS improved by 5.9 percentage points (23.0% vs 17.1%). R0 resection increased from 54% to 60%.

NNT: 17, based on 5-year overall survival (absolute improvement 5.9 percentage points).

NNH: not quantifiable from the primary report because a single fixed-time serious toxicity endpoint was not reported in a form suitable for a defensible NNH.

Follow-up: Long-term follow-up confirmed persistent survival benefit.

Clinical results and treatment burden

  • Intervention: Two 3-weekly cycles of cisplatin plus continuous-infusion 5-FU before surgery versus immediate surgery.
  • Principal toxicity: The major treatment burden was preoperative cisplatin/5-FU toxicity and the possibility of treatment-related morbidity delaying surgery; operative outcomes did not erase the survival benefit.
  • Other efficacy/recurrence findings: The survival effect was consistent across adenocarcinoma and squamous histologies in the long-term analysis, although subsequent trials showed that chemoradiotherapy and perioperative strategies may be preferable in particular anatomical/histological settings.
  • Quality of life: QoL was not the principal endpoint; the trial's patient-centred value lies mainly in survival and successful R0 resection.

Eligibility and clinical context

802 patients with treatment-naïve, resectable esophageal cancer; approximately two thirds had adenocarcinoma and one third squamous carcinoma.

Limitations and maturity: Later randomized trials have produced heterogeneous results for neoadjuvant chemotherapy, particularly in squamous disease. Modern practice also incorporates PET/CT, MRI, molecular staging and chemoradiotherapy/perioperative FLOT depending on tumour location and histology.

Clinical interpretation

OE02 established that a short course of preoperative cisplatin/5-FU can improve long-term survival and R0 resection compared with surgery alone in operable esophageal cancer.

Primary / authoritative source

Medical Research Council Oesophageal Cancer Working Party. Lancet. 2002;359:1727–1733; long-term analysis J Clin Oncol. 2009.
