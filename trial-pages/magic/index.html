Gastric and Gastroesophageal Junction Cancer · Medical

MAGIC

In resectable gastric, gastroesophageal-junction or lower oesophageal adenocarcinoma, does perioperative ECF improve survival over surgery alone?

Population
503 patients with resectable adenocarcinoma of the stomach, lower oesophagus or gastroesophageal junction.
Intervention vs comparator
Three preoperative and three postoperative cycles of epirubicin, cisplatin and continuous-infusion fluorouracil (ECF) versus surgery alone.
Sample size
503
Primary endpoint
Overall survival

Reported outcome visualization

Key results

Five-year OS was 36% with perioperative ECF versus 23% with surgery alone; HR for death 0.75 (95% CI 0.60–0.93; P=.009). PFS improved (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.53–0.81; P<.001). R0 resection was 79% versus 70%.

Absolute Effect

Absolute 5-year OS gain 13 percentage points; NNT ≈8 for one additional survivor at 5 years, calculated from 1/0.13. This is a historical comparison against surgery alone, not modern FLOT.

NNT: ≈8, based on 5-year overall survival.

NNH: Not quantifiable from the verified fixed-time toxicity data used in this summary; see toxicity and treatment burden below.

Time-to-event interpretation: Hazard ratios and median survival are relative/time-to-event measures; they are not converted into NNT unless a fixed-time absolute event estimate is available.

Toxicity and treatment burden

Postoperative complications were similar (about 46% vs 45%) and 30-day postoperative mortality was similar. Only about 42% completed all six planned cycles; perioperative chemotherapy adds hematologic, gastrointestinal, renal and cardiac burden.

Follow-up, limitations and maturity

Follow-up: Median follow-up approximately 4 years in the primary report.

ECF is no longer the preferred contemporary perioperative regimen in many settings; limited D2 lymphadenectomy and low completion of postoperative treatment constrain transportability. FLOT4 later changed practice.

Clinical interpretation

Perioperative chemotherapy established a survival advantage over surgery alone, but MAGIC is foundational historical evidence rather than the current preferred regimen.

Primary publication and sources

Cunningham D, et al. N Engl J Med. 2006;355:11–20.

Current-practice context · reviewed 15 October 2026

MAGIC established perioperative chemotherapy over surgery alone, but ECF is not the preferred modern regimen for most fit patients. FLOT4 established perioperative FLOT as a more effective option than ECF/ECX in fit resectable gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma; by 2026, MATTERHORN has further changed the perioperative evidence landscape through durvalumab plus FLOT. The original MAGIC absolute effect should remain clearly labelled as a historical surgery-alone comparison.
