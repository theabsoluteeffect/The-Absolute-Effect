The Absolute Effect / Evidence / FASTRACK II
Renal Cell Carcinoma · Radiation Oncology · Localized Primary RCC

FASTRACK II

Clinical question

Can stereotactic ablative body radiotherapy provide effective definitive local control for primary RCC in patients unsuitable for surgery?

Trial context
Population
70 patients with biopsy-confirmed primary RCC who were medically inoperable, high surgical risk or declined surgery
Intervention
Definitive SABR: 26 Gy single fraction for ≤4 cm tumours or 42 Gy in 3 fractions for >4–10 cm tumours
Key endpoint
12-month local control
Time horizon / follow-up
12 months
Absolute Effect

What changes for patients?

FASTRACK II achieved 100% local control at 12 months in 70 treated patients, exceeding the prespecified 90% target. Median follow-up in the primary publication was 43 months. Grade 3 toxicity occurred in fewer than 10% of patients and there were no grade 4–5 events. This was a single-arm phase II study, so no comparative NNT can be assigned.

NNT: Not applicable; single-arm phase II

Treatment burden / harm

Grade 3 treatment-related toxicity

NNH: Not applicable; single-arm study

Relative effect

Local control: 100% at 12 months

Primary endpoint met; prespecified threshold was 90%

Clinical interpretation

Interpret the absolute difference together with the endpoint, time horizon, relative effect and limitations of the study design.
