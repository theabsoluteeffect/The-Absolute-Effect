The Absolute Effect / Evidence / Breast Cancer

Breast Cancer · Surgical · Stage I–II · sentinel-node positive, cT1–2 · Definitive / Curative

AMAROS (EORTC 10981-22023)

Clinical question: In clinically node-negative T1–2 breast cancer with a positive sentinel node, can axillary radiotherapy replace completion axillary lymph-node dissection?

Registry: NCT00014612 · One underlying study; aliases and follow-up publications consolidated.

Phase III, multicentre, open-label randomized non-inferiority trialEvidence cut-off: 3 November 2026

Design, population and eligibility

Design

Phase III, multicentre, open-label randomized non-inferiority trial

Sample size

4,806 patients underwent sentinel-node biopsy; 1,425 with a positive sentinel node were randomized: ALND 744 and axillary RT 681.

Eligibility

cT1–2, clinically node-negative invasive breast cancer with a positive sentinel lymph node. The evidence applies to sentinel-node-positive patients in the trial's early-stage setting, not bulky clinically node-positive disease.

Follow-up / maturity

Follow-up and endpoint maturity are stated with the reported efficacy results; further long-term updates are noted where applicable.

Treatment and comparator

Completion ALND versus axillary radiotherapy, 50 Gy in 25 fractions to axillary levels I–III and medial supraclavicular fossa. Adjuvant breast/chest-wall treatment and systemic therapy followed standard practice.

Primary and secondary endpoints

Primary endpoint: 5-year axillary recurrence, with a non-inferiority framework. Long-term outcomes include 10-year axillary recurrence, DFS, OS, morbidity and QoL.

Key efficacy results

At 10 years, cumulative axillary recurrence was 0.93% (95% CI 0.18–1.68; 7 events) after ALND and 1.82% (95% CI 0.74–2.94; 11 events) after axillary RT; HR 1.71 (95% CI 0.67–4.39). OS did not differ (HR 1.17; 95% CI 0.89–1.52) and DFS did not differ (HR 1.19; 95% CI 0.97–1.46).

At 5 years, clinical lymphedema was 24.5% after ALND versus 11.9% after axillary RT (P<0.001). QoL scales did not differ through 5 years.

Absolute effect

At 5 years, clinical lymphedema was 12.6 percentage points lower with axillary RT (11.9% vs 24.5%); approximate NNT 8 to prevent one additional case of clinical lymphedema at 5 years by choosing RT rather than ALND. The 10-year axillary recurrence difference was 0.89 percentage points higher with RT, but the confidence interval was wide and no NNT is assigned.

Trial-specific toxicity and treatment burden

Clinical lymphedema at 5 years: 24.5% ALND versus 11.9% axillary RT. Arm circumference increase >10% at 5 years: 13% versus 5%. Shoulder range of motion did not differ significantly. This is a morbidity trade-off: RT reduces surgical arm morbidity but carries regional radiation exposure and potential late effects. Do not conflate clinical lymphedema with the circumference-based measure.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

Selected QoL scales (arm symptoms, pain and body image) did not show statistically significant clinically relevant differences through 5 years, despite less clinical lymphedema with RT.

Subgroups and applicability

The trial included breast-conserving surgery and mastectomy patients; it was not designed to establish management for patients with heavy nodal burden or gross residual disease. Contemporary practice must account for tumour burden and systemic therapy.

Current-practice context · reviewed 3 November 2026

Axillary RT is an established alternative to ALND for appropriately selected sentinel-node-positive cT1–2 patients when further axillary treatment is indicated. This study is distinct from trials testing omission of axillary surgery altogether.

Limitations and critical interpretation

The primary non-inferiority question was limited by very low recurrence rates; the 10-year HR CI is wide. The trial was not a direct comparison of RT versus no further axillary treatment. Modern systemic therapy and surgical de-escalation have changed the population treated.

Bottom line: AMAROS demonstrates excellent long-term axillary control with either strategy and materially less clinical lymphedema after axillary RT. It supports replacing ALND with RT in selected sentinel-node-positive patients, not universal axillary treatment omission.
