Prostate Cancer · Radiation · Post-prostatectomy

EORTC 22911

After radical prostatectomy with pathological high-risk features, does immediate postoperative radiotherapy improve biochemical control compared with observation?

PopulationpN0M0 men with positive surgical margins, extracapsular extension and/or seminal-vesicle invasion after prostatectomy.InterventionImmediate prostate-bed radiotherapy, 60 Gy.ComparatorWait-and-see policy with treatment at biochemical progression.Sample1,005 randomized men.

Absolute Effect

Biochemical progression-free survival
5-year: 74.0% vs 52.6% (+21.4 points)

The initial Lancet report showed a large improvement in biochemical progression-free survival with immediate RT.

Long-term disease control
Benefit persisted for biochemical control

Long-term follow-up confirmed improvement in progression-free outcomes, although the magnitude of benefit was smaller in some prognostic subgroups.

Clinical interpretation

EORTC 22911 is foundational evidence that postoperative prostate-bed RT can improve biochemical control in men with pathological risk factors after prostatectomy.

It should be interpreted alongside modern early-salvage trials, because many men in the observation arm of older trials were treated later than current PSA-triggered salvage strategies.

Treatment burden and limitations

Radiotherapy adds urinary, bowel and erectile toxicity. The historical RT dose and PSA-trigger strategy differ from contemporary practice. Modern decision-making therefore requires integration with RADICALS-RT, RAVES and GETUG-AFU 17 rather than treating EORTC 22911 as a standalone timing trial.

NNT / NNH

Using the 5-year biochemical progression-free survival difference, an approximate NNT is 5. This is an NNT for preventing biochemical progression at 5 years, not for preventing metastasis or death.

NNH is not calculated here because a single harmonized fixed-time serious-toxicity endpoint is not reported in the pivotal efficacy result.
