Bladder Cancer · Medical · Non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer

SWOG 8507

After induction BCG for high-risk NMIBC, does a maintenance BCG programme reduce recurrence compared with observation?

Population384 high-risk patients with recurrent Ta/T1 disease or carcinoma in situ who had responded to induction BCG.InterventionMaintenance BCG: three weekly instillations at 3 and 6 months, then every 6 months through 3 years.ComparatorNo maintenance BCG after induction.Key endpointRecurrence-free survival.

Absolute Effect

5-year recurrence-free survival
60% vs 41% (+19 points)

Maintenance BCG produced a large absolute improvement in recurrence-free survival.

Treatment completion
Only 16% completed all scheduled maintenance courses

The efficacy result therefore has to be interpreted together with substantial treatment burden and incomplete delivery.

Clinical interpretation

SWOG 8507 established the importance of maintenance BCG for recurrence prevention after induction in high-risk NMIBC.

The study did not establish a comparable mortality benefit, and it predates modern BCG-shortage strategies and newer bladder-preservation agents.

Toxicity / burden

Maintenance requires repeated intravesical procedures. Historical reports describe substantial grade 3–4 toxicity and incomplete completion of the prescribed schedule.

NNT

Using the 5-year RFS difference, NNT ≈ 6. This is an NNT for preventing recurrence by 5 years, not for preventing progression or death.
