Cervical Cancer · Radiation · Definitive / Curative · Stage IB–IVA / locally advanced

EMBRACE II

Can a protocolized, risk-adapted programme of image-guided IMRT, weekly cisplatin and MRI-guided adaptive brachytherapy achieve excellent local, nodal and systemic control while reducing severe morbidity in locally advanced cervical cancer?

Registry: NCT03617133 · One underlying study; aliases and follow-up publications consolidated · Evidence reviewed 3 November 2026.

Prospective, multicentre interventional cohort study with protocolized radiotherapy and chemotherapy; not a randomized comparative trial.Prospective cohort · ESTRO 2025 results

Study design, population and eligibility

Design and sample

Prospective, multicentre interventional cohort study with protocolized radiotherapy and chemotherapy; not a randomized comparative trial.

The EMBRACE Collaborative Group reported 1,449 accrued patients from 49 centres between 2016 and December 2021. The ESTRO completed-studies catalogue later lists 1,452; secondary reviews report other analysis denominators. This page uses the 1,449 accrual figure from the study-group results presentation and does not mix denominators.

Eligibility

FIGO 2009 stage IB–IVA cervical cancer treated with definitive chemoradiotherapy and image-guided adaptive brachytherapy. The cohort includes risk-adapted nodal field selection and patients with varied tumour and nodal risk. It is not a randomized comparison and outcomes reflect a selected, protocol-accredited international-centre population.

Treatment and comparator

External-beam RT: IMRT/VMAT 45 Gy in 25 fractions with daily image guidance, 5-mm PTV margins and risk-adapted elective nodal volumes. Involved nodes receive simultaneous integrated boost, generally 55–57 Gy.

Concurrent chemotherapy: Weekly cisplatin 40 mg/m², with at least five cycles intended.

Brachytherapy: MRI-guided adaptive brachytherapy with combined intracavitary/interstitial applicator techniques. Combined EBRT+BT planning aims include HR-CTV D90 90–95 Gy EQD2₁₀ (hard lower limit >85 Gy); GTVres D98 >95 Gy (hard >90 Gy).

Key OAR planning aims (EQD2₃): bladder D2cc <80 Gy; rectum <65 Gy; rectovaginal point <65 Gy; sigmoid and bowel D2cc <70 Gy. Hard prescribed-dose limits are generally bladder <90 Gy, rectum/rectovaginal point/sigmoid/bowel <75 Gy. Overall treatment time target ≤50 days.

Endpoints

EMBRACE II is a multi-endpoint benchmarking study, not a conventional trial with a single randomized primary endpoint. It evaluates local, nodal and systemic control; overall, disease-specific and disease-free survival; physician-assessed morbidity; patient-reported outcomes and quality of life; and dose–volume relationships for target and organs at risk.

Key efficacy and clinical outcomes

At the ESTRO 2025 presentation (data cut November 2024), three-year actuarial outcomes were: local control 93%, nodal control 91%, distant control 88%, overall disease control 80%, OS 87%, disease-specific survival 88% and DFS 78%.

For a high-risk subgroup (any N+, T3 or T4), three-year OS was 84%, DSS 86% and DFS 73%. In an INTERLACE-like subgroup without para-aortic nodal metastases, OS was 89%, DSS 91% and DFS 80%.

Crude grade 3–5 morbidity was 8.9% overall: urinary 2.8%, gastrointestinal 3.7%, vaginal 3.2%; fistula 1%. The presentation reported approximately 1% grade 4–5 morbidity in the specified organ categories.

Absolute effect and NNT/NNH

There is no randomized control group, so comparative absolute risk reduction, NNT and NNH cannot be calculated. The most informative absolute outcomes are the three-year control/survival rates and the crude grade 3–5 morbidity rate. These must not be interpreted as causal superiority over EMBRACE I or other cohorts.

Trial-specific toxicity, grade 3–5 harms and treatment burden

Crude grade 3–5 morbidity was 8.9%; urinary 2.8%, GI 3.7%, vaginal 3.2%, and fistula approximately 1%. The study-group presentation reported grade 4–5 morbidity around 1% in the specified organ categories. These are cohort-level morbidity figures and should not be relabelled as randomized treatment-related adverse-event rates.

Treatment burden is substantial: 25 EBRT fractions, weekly cisplatin, repeated MRI-based brachytherapy applications, and stringent overall treatment time. The protocol seeks completion within 50 days.

Quality of life

Patient-reported outcomes and QoL were explicit study endpoints. The accessible overall-results presentation provides the principal disease-control and morbidity estimates but does not provide a sufficiently detailed numerical QoL comparison for extraction here. No QoL difference is inferred.

Subgroups and applicability

The study reports high-risk (any N+, T3/T4) and para-aortic-node-negative subgroups. These are descriptive prognostic subgroups, not randomized treatment interactions. Risk-adapted elective nodal target selection is a core protocol component.

Maturity, limitations and critical interpretation

Design: Prospective interventional cohort, not randomized. Outcomes cannot isolate the causal contribution of IMRT, nodal SIB, cisplatin, MRI-guided brachytherapy or treatment-time reduction individually.

Reporting: The overall results were presented at ESTRO 2025; a full peer-reviewed final outcomes publication was not identified in this audit. Study denominators vary slightly across public sources (1,449, 1,452 and 1,482), reflecting different report/analysis definitions. This page clearly uses the study-group presentation's 1,449 accrual count.

Generalizability: Accreditation, specialist brachytherapy expertise and protocol compliance may limit transferability to centres without comparable resources. QoL detail and full time-to-event confidence intervals were not available in the accessible summary.

Clinical interpretation: EMBRACE II is a major prospective benchmark for modern definitive cervical chemoradiotherapy: risk-adapted IMRT/VMAT, nodal boost, weekly cisplatin and MRI-guided intracavitary/interstitial brachytherapy produced excellent three-year local control and survival with lower reported severe morbidity than historical benchmarks. It supports protocolized high-quality delivery, but it is not a randomized comparison proving that any single component improves outcomes.
