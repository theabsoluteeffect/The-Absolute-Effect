The Absolute Effect

SORCE

Renal Cell Carcinoma · Adjuvant Therapy

Clinical question

Does adjuvant sorafenib improve disease-free survival after nephrectomy in patients at intermediate or high risk of recurrent RCC?

Trial context

SORCE was a randomized placebo-controlled trial evaluating three years of sorafenib after nephrectomy in patients with intermediate- or high-risk renal cell carcinoma.

Key result

SORCE did not demonstrate a disease-free survival benefit for adjuvant sorafenib compared with placebo. The trial therefore did not establish postoperative sorafenib as a standard treatment.

Absolute Effect perspective

A negative randomized trial is clinically informative. Targeted therapy with established activity in metastatic RCC cannot be assumed to provide a recurrence benefit when moved into the adjuvant setting.

Clinical takeaway

The appropriate interpretation is absence of demonstrated benefit, not evidence that adjuvant sorafenib is effective but merely difficult to tolerate. Treatment burden must be considered alongside the randomized disease-control result.
