Gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma · HER2-positive · First-line · Reviewed 17 November 2026

HERIZON-GEA-01

Does zanidatamab plus chemotherapy, with or without tislelizumab, improve outcomes compared with trastuzumab plus chemotherapy in previously untreated HER2-positive advanced gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma?

DesignPhase III · randomized · open-label · 1:1:1
Population914 patients with untreated HER2-positive advanced GEA
ArmsZanidatamab + tislelizumab + chemo; zanidatamab + chemo; trastuzumab + chemo
Primary endpointPFS by blinded independent central review
Key secondary endpointOverall survival
MaturityPrimary NEJM analysis

Design, population and treatment

HERIZON-GEA-01 was an international randomized phase III study of 914 patients with previously untreated unresectable locally advanced or metastatic HER2-positive gastroesophageal adenocarcinoma. HER2 positivity was defined by IHC 3+ or IHC 2+ with ISH amplification. Patients were randomized 1:1:1 to zanidatamab plus tislelizumab plus chemotherapy, zanidatamab plus chemotherapy, or trastuzumab plus chemotherapy. The primary efficacy endpoint was PFS by blinded independent central review; OS was a key secondary endpoint with a prespecified testing hierarchy.

Treatment and comparator

Zanidatamab arms: Zanidatamab 1,800 mg IV for body weight <70 kg or 2,400 mg IV for ≥70 kg every 3 weeks. In the triplet arm, tislelizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks was added.

Chemotherapy options: CAPOX (oxaliplatin 130 mg/m² IV day 1 plus capecitabine 1,000 mg/m² orally twice daily days 1–14, every 3 weeks) or fluorouracil/cisplatin (cisplatin 80 mg/m² day 1 plus fluorouracil 800 mg/m²/day continuous infusion days 1–5, every 3 weeks), per protocol.

Control: Trastuzumab 8 mg/kg IV loading dose then 6 mg/kg every 3 weeks plus the same chemotherapy options.

Endpoints and follow-up

Primary endpoint: PFS assessed by blinded independent central review. OS was a key secondary endpoint. The primary NEJM report provides the randomized efficacy analysis; the accessible abstract does not provide a complete patient-reported QoL analysis. Interpret the doublet and triplet comparisons separately because the OS result for the zanidatamab doublet did not meet conventional statistical significance.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 83.3% of the triplet arm, 73.8% of the zanidatamab doublet arm and 74.5% of the trastuzumab control arm in the primary NEJM report. A separate 2026 conference safety analysis reported grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events of 71.8%, 59.0% and 59.6%, respectively, and grade 5 treatment-related events of 2.4%, 0.3% and 1.3%. Keep all-cause grade ≥3 AEs distinct from treatment-related events.

Quality of life

The primary NEJM report did not provide a full HRQoL analysis. The ESMO GI 2026 secondary HRQoL abstract (494O; DOI 10.1016/j.annonc.2026.05.534) reported EORTC QLQ-C30 completion above 95% through cycle 34 and above 60% at end of treatment. Global health status/QoL and most domain scores were numerically similar across arms. Physical-function changes at cycle 2 day 1 were small (−2.0 and −3.7 points versus control for the two zanidatamab-containing arms), below the 7.5-point threshold cited by the abstract; most patients maintained or improved physical functioning through cycles 2–8. This is a secondary descriptive analysis, not evidence of a QoL advantage or a basis for NNT.

Absolute effect and NNT/NNH

No NNT/NNH is calculated. The verified primary outcomes are time-to-event medians and hazard ratios; comparable fixed-time absolute event rates are not used here.

Interpretation and limitations

The triplet improved PFS and OS versus trastuzumab plus chemotherapy. The zanidatamab doublet improved PFS, but its OS comparison was not statistically significant (P=0.06). These are separate treatment comparisons; do not describe both experimental regimens as having proven OS benefit. Toxicity was numerically higher with the triplet, including more grade ≥3 all-cause adverse events.

Current-practice context · reviewed 20 November 2026

Current NCCN guideline version, section, reference number and recommendation category were not verified because the complete guideline/reference list was inaccessible. No NCCN recommendation is claimed. The trial is important new phase III evidence for HER2-positive advanced GEA; regulatory status and guideline incorporation should be checked in the relevant jurisdiction before treating it as a universal standard.
