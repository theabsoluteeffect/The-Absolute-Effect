Chlorophyllin TNT toxicity prevention

A small randomized trial found that oral sodium copper chlorophyllin reduced grade 2 or higher acute gastrointestinal, hematologic or genitourinary toxicity during total neoadjuvant therapy for nonmetastatic mid/low rectal cancer.

Study identity and design

Phase II, two-centre, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized clinical trial in India. The primary analysis included 73 participants (37 assigned chlorophyllin and 36 placebo).

Nonmetastatic locally advanced adenocarcinoma of the mid or lower rectum treated with a total-neoadjuvant-therapy pathway. The accessible primary abstract reports 73 included patients; detailed exclusion criteria and stratification factors were not reported in the abstract.

Intervention and comparator

Experimental: Sodium copper chlorophyllin 750 mg/day orally, started 10–14 days before TNT and continued to 3 months after TNT.

Comparator: Matched placebo alongside the same short-course RT, chemotherapy and brachytherapy-boost pathway.

Key efficacy outcome

Primary endpoint: grade ≥2 combined acute GI, hematologic or GU toxicity. Events occurred in 24/37 (64.9%; reported 65%) with chlorophyllin versus 34/36 (94.4%; reported 94%) with placebo. Absolute risk reduction 29.6 percentage points (90% CI 15.2–43.9); RR 0.69 (90% CI 0.56–0.86); p=0.003.

Key secondary endpoint: grade ≥3 combined acute toxicity occurred in 10/37 (27.0%; reported 27%) versus 19/36 (52.8%; reported 53%); absolute reduction 25.8 percentage points (90% CI 7.5–44.0); RR 0.51 (90% CI 0.30–0.86); p=0.03. Grade 3 GI toxicity was 1/37 (3%) versus 8/36 (22%), p=0.01.

Safety and absolute effect

All 73 patients completed radiotherapy. Grade ≥3 combined acute toxicity was lower with chlorophyllin; grade 3 GI toxicity was 3% versus 22%. No unexpected serious drug-related adverse events were reported in the accessible abstract.

Quality of life: Not reported in the accessible primary abstract.

Treatment burden / discontinuation: Chemotherapy dose reductions occurred in 4/37 (11%) versus 10/36 (28%), p=0.07. All patients completed RT.

Interpretation and limitations

This is a promising supportive-care signal in a small phase II trial, not evidence that chlorophyllin improves tumour control or survival. The absolute toxicity reductions are clinically interesting but require replication and longer follow-up before routine adoption.

Limitations: Only 73 patients from two centres; the primary source available for this run was the peer-reviewed abstract, not the full article. No quality-of-life or long-term oncologic outcomes were reported; confidence intervals are 90%.
