GCIG INTERLACE

In locally advanced cervical cancer, does short-course induction chemotherapy before chemoradiotherapy improve survival?

One underlying study; aliases and follow-up reports consolidated. Primary discipline: Medical.

Population and eligibility

500 patients with locally advanced cervical cancer planned for definitive chemoradiotherapy.

Patients fit for induction chemotherapy and definitive chemoradiotherapy; not evidence for metastatic disease or patients unable to tolerate the regimen.

Sample size

500 randomised/evaluable as reported.

Intervention

Six weekly cycles of carboplatin AUC 2 plus paclitaxel 80 mg/m², followed by standard cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy and brachytherapy.

Comparator

Standard cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy and brachytherapy alone.

Endpoints and results

Co-primary endpoints: PFS and OS; recurrence and toxicity were also assessed.

  • 5-year PFS 72% vs 64%; HR 0.65 (95% CI 0.46–0.91), p=0.013.
  • 5-year OS 80% vs 72%; HR 0.60 (95% CI 0.40–0.91), p=0.015.
  • Grade ≥3 adverse events 59% vs 48%.

Toxicity, discontinuation and quality of life

Trial-specific harms: Grade ≥3 adverse events 59% vs 48%; induction adds acute toxicity and six weekly treatment visits before definitive treatment.

Treatment burden/discontinuation: Protect timely delivery and completion of chemoradiotherapy and brachytherapy; verify exact completion/discontinuation rates from full tables.

Quality of life: No comparative QoL estimate is reproduced here; do not infer one.

Subgroups, maturity and limitations

Subgroups: Avoid claims of subgroup-specific benefit unless supported by prespecified interaction analyses.

Maturity: Five-year PFS and OS reported.

Limitations: Open-label sequencing trial; induction is useful only if definitive local therapy remains deliverable. NNT is a landmark estimate.

Clinical interpretation

INTERLACE supports induction chemotherapy followed by definitive chemoradiotherapy in selected locally advanced cervical cancer, with survival improvement but more grade ≥3 toxicity.

Current-practice context must be checked against regional guidelines, patient fitness, biomarkers, alternatives and regulatory status before clinical application. Clinical freshness review and browser-rendered QA remain pending.
