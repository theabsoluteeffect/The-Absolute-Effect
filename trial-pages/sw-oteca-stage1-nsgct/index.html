SWENOTECA clinical stage I NSGCT management programme

Clinical question

Does vascular invasion identify patients with sufficiently high relapse risk to justify adjuvant BEP in stage I non-seminoma?

Trial context
Population
745 patients with clinical stage I non-seminomatous germ-cell tumours in a prospective SWENOTECA programme
Intervention
Risk-adapted surveillance vs one cycle adjuvant BEP
Key endpoint
Relapse rate on surveillance according to vascular invasion
Time horizon / follow-up
Median follow-up 4.7 years
Absolute Effect

What changes for patients?

SWENOTECA demonstrated why pathology matters in stage I NSGCT: among patients managed with surveillance, relapse was 41.7% with vascular invasion versus 13.2% without it. After one course of BEP, relapse was 3.2% in vascular-invasion-positive patients and 1.3% in vascular-invasion-negative patients. Because treatment allocation was not randomized, these absolute differences should not be converted into a causal NNT. NNT is therefore not assigned as a trial-level causal estimate.

NNT: Not applicable; non-randomized risk-adapted programme

Treatment burden / harm

One-cycle BEP toxicity

NNH: Not quantifiable from this nonrandomized programme; chemotherapy burden should be discussed against relapse risk

Relative effect

Risk stratification: Surveillance relapse 41.7% with vascular invasion vs 13.2% without

Prospective management programme; treatment allocation was not randomized

Clinical interpretation

Interpret the absolute difference together with the endpoint, time horizon, relative effect and limitations of the study design.
