Rectal Cancer · Radiation Oncology

RAPIDO

In high-risk locally advanced rectal cancer, does short-course radiotherapy followed by chemotherapy reduce treatment failure compared with standard long-course chemoradiotherapy?

Population
920 patients with high-risk locally advanced rectal cancer
3-year disease-related treatment failure
23.7% vs 30.4%
Absolute reduction 6.7 points; NNT 15
Pathologic complete response
≈28% vs 14%
Large increase with the experimental strategy
Grade ≥3 diarrhoea
18% vs 9%
Absolute increase 9 points; NNH 12

Absolute Effect

NNT: 15, based on 3-year disease-related treatment failure. The early disease-control advantage was clinically meaningful.

NNH: 12, based on grade ≥3 diarrhoea. Gastrointestinal toxicity increased with the intensified pathway.

Critical interpretation

RAPIDO is an important total-neoadjuvant-treatment trial, but the early reduction in treatment failure should not be equated automatically with an overall survival advantage. Longer follow-up identified a local-regional recurrence trade-off, illustrating why absolute effects must be revisited at mature time horizons.

Primary source

Bahadoer RR, et al. Lancet Oncol. 2021.
