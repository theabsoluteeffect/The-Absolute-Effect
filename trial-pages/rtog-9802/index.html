RTOG 9802

For high-risk grade 2 glioma, does adding PCV chemotherapy after radiotherapy improve survival compared with radiotherapy alone?

Population
251 patients with high-risk grade 2 glioma; age ≥40 years or age <40 with less than gross total resection.
Intervention vs comparator
Radiotherapy 54 Gy followed by six cycles of procarbazine, lomustine (CCNU) and vincristine (PCV), versus radiotherapy alone.
Sample size
251
Primary endpoint
Overall survival

Key results

Long-term analysis showed median OS 13.3 years with RT+PCV versus 7.8 years with RT alone (HR 0.59; P=.003); median PFS 10.4 versus 4.0 years (HR 0.50; P<.001).

Absolute Effect

Median OS difference 5.5 years; do not convert a median difference or HR into an NNT. Molecular reanalysis suggested benefit in IDH-mutant tumours, but these subgroup analyses were post hoc.

NNT: Not quantifiable or not appropriate for this design/end point.

NNH: Not quantifiable from the verified fixed-time toxicity data used in this summary; see toxicity and treatment burden below.

Time-to-event interpretation: Hazard ratios and median survival are relative/time-to-event measures; they are not converted into NNT unless a fixed-time absolute event estimate is available.

Toxicity and treatment burden

PCV adds myelosuppression, infection risk, nausea, fatigue and vincristine neuropathy; treatment completion can be difficult. A reliable trial-wide fixed-time toxicity risk difference is not used here, so NNH is not quantifiable from the verified summary.

Follow-up, limitations and maturity

Follow-up: Long-term follow-up approximately 11.9 years in the mature report.

Conducted before current WHO molecular classification; retrospective molecular subgroup analyses are hypothesis-generating. PCV toxicity and patient selection matter.

Clinical interpretation

For high-risk grade 2 glioma, adding PCV to RT yields a delayed but clinically important survival advantage; molecular diagnosis is essential when applying this legacy trial.

Primary publication and sources

Buckner JC, et al. N Engl J Med. 2016;374:1344–1355.
