In unresectable stage IIIA/IIIB NSCLC, does escalating definitive thoracic RT from 60 Gy to 74 Gy improve survival when given with concurrent chemotherapy? [RTOG 0617]

In unresectable stage IIIA/IIIB NSCLC, does escalating definitive thoracic RT from 60 Gy to 74 Gy improve survival when given with concurrent chemotherapy?

Population
464 eligible patients with newly diagnosed unresectable stage IIIA/IIIB NSCLC; most had good performance status and modern PET-based staging.
Sample size
464 evaluable/randomized patients for the RT-dose question.
Intervention vs comparator
Standard-dose arm: 60 Gy in 30 fractions with concurrent weekly carboplatin/paclitaxel followed by consolidation chemotherapy. High-dose arm: 74 Gy in 37 fractions with the same systemic backbone. The factorial cetuximab question is not the focus of this page.
Primary endpoint
Primary endpoint: overall survival.

Absolute Effect

Median OS was 28.7 months with 60 Gy versus 20.3 months with 74 Gy; HR 1.38 (95% CI 1.09–1.76) for 74 vs 60 Gy. Five-year OS was 32.1% versus 23.0%.

Absolute effect: At 5 years, OS was 9.1 percentage points lower with 74 Gy (23.0% vs 32.1%). This is an absolute harm from dose escalation, not merely failure to demonstrate benefit.

NNT: not applicable. The high-dose strategy was inferior; a number-needed-to-treat-for-benefit is inappropriate.

NNH: 9, based on grade ≥3 esophagitis (17.4% vs 5.0%; absolute increase 12.4 percentage points).

Follow-up: Median follow-up 5.1 years in the long-term report.

Clinical results and treatment burden

  • Intervention: Standard-dose arm: 60 Gy in 30 fractions with concurrent weekly carboplatin/paclitaxel followed by consolidation chemotherapy. High-dose arm: 74 Gy in 37 fractions with the same systemic backbone. The factorial cetuximab question is not the focus of this page.
  • Principal toxicity: The principal clinically central toxicity was severe esophageal toxicity. Grade ≥3 dysphagia was 12.1% vs 3.2% and grade ≥3 esophagitis 17.4% vs 5.0% with 74 vs 60 Gy. Grade-5 adverse events were 9 vs 3.
  • Other efficacy/recurrence findings: Grade ≥3 pulmonary toxicity was similar (19.3% vs 20.6%). Treatment-related toxicity and difficulty completing chemotherapy were greater with 74 Gy. No clinically meaningful survival subgroup overturned the main dose finding.
  • Quality of life: Quality-of-life analyses supported the clinical relevance of increased esophageal toxicity; the survival disadvantage remained the dominant result.

Eligibility and clinical context

464 eligible patients with newly diagnosed unresectable stage IIIA/IIIB NSCLC; most had good performance status and modern PET-based staging.

Limitations and maturity: The trial used conventional 2-Gy fractionation and older-era concurrent chemotherapy/supportive care. The dose comparison cannot be extrapolated to modern proton/SABR strategies or altered fractionation. The factorial cetuximab question was separately negative.

Clinical interpretation

For unresectable stage III NSCLC treated with concurrent chemoradiation, 60 Gy—not 74 Gy—became the evidence-based conventional dose ceiling; escalation produced worse survival and substantially more esophageal toxicity.

Primary / authoritative source

Bradley JD, et al. Long-Term Results of NRG Oncology RTOG 0617. J Clin Oncol. 2020.
