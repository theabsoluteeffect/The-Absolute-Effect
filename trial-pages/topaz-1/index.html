In untreated advanced biliary tract cancer, does durvalumab added to gemcitabine–cisplatin improve OS?

Trial design and population

Population / eligibility
685 patients with previously untreated unresectable locally advanced or metastatic biliary tract cancer.
Intervention vs comparator
Durvalumab 1,500 mg IV day 1 plus gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m² and cisplatin 25 mg/m² days 1 and 8 every 3 weeks for up to 8 cycles, then durvalumab maintenance, vs placebo plus GemCis.
Sample size
685
Primary endpoint
Overall survival

Key results

Updated median OS was 12.8 months with durvalumab plus GemCis vs 11.5 months with GemCis alone (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.66–0.97). At 24 months, OS was 23.6% vs 11.5%. Median PFS was 7.2 vs 5.7 months (HR 0.75).

Safety and treatment burden

Grade 3–4 adverse events were frequent in both groups due to GemCis; durvalumab adds immune-mediated events. Biliary infection, cytopenias, renal toxicity and immune hepatitis require monitoring.

Follow-up, maturity and limitations

Follow-up: Updated analysis with follow-up approaching 2 years.

Most patients had ECOG 0–1 and adequate organ function. Evidence applies to advanced BTC not amenable to curative local treatment; molecular profiling remains important for later lines.

Clinical interpretation

Adding durvalumab to GemCis provides a durable survival benefit in first-line advanced biliary tract cancer.
