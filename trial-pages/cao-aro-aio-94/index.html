Rectal Cancer · Radiation

CAO/ARO/AIO-94

In locally advanced rectal cancer, does preoperative rather than postoperative chemoradiotherapy reduce local recurrence and improve survival?

Population
823 patients with clinical stage II–III rectal cancer; 799 eligible for analysis.
Intervention vs comparator
Preoperative fluorouracil-based chemoradiotherapy followed by total mesorectal excision and adjuvant fluorouracil versus the same chemoradiotherapy delivered postoperatively.
Sample size
823
Primary endpoint
Overall survival; local recurrence was a key secondary endpoint

Reported outcome visualization

Key results

At median follow-up 134 months, 10-year OS was 59.6% with preoperative versus 59.9% with postoperative CRT (P=.85). Ten-year cumulative local relapse was 7.1% versus 10.1% (P=.048).

Absolute Effect

Absolute 10-year local relapse reduction 3.0 percentage points; NNT ≈34 to prevent one local relapse by 10 years, based on the reported cumulative incidences. No OS benefit was demonstrated.

NNT: ≈34, based on 10-year local relapse.

NNH: Not quantifiable from the verified fixed-time toxicity data used in this summary; see toxicity and treatment burden below.

Time-to-event interpretation: Hazard ratios and median survival are relative/time-to-event measures; they are not converted into NNT unless a fixed-time absolute event estimate is available.

Toxicity and treatment burden

Preoperative treatment is associated with lower acute and late toxicity than postoperative CRT in the original report, and a higher likelihood of sphincter preservation in suitable patients. NNH is not quantified here without a verified single toxicity endpoint rate.

Follow-up, limitations and maturity

Follow-up: Median follow-up 134 months.

Older fluorouracil-only treatment and staging; modern MRI-based selection, total neoadjuvant therapy and organ-preservation strategies have changed care. The trial compared timing, not CRT against no CRT.

Clinical interpretation

Preoperative CRT reduces long-term local relapse and treatment toxicity compared with postoperative CRT, without a demonstrated overall-survival advantage.

Primary publication and sources

Sauer R, et al. J Clin Oncol. 2012;30:1926–1933.
