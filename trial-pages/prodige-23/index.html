Rectal Cancer · Medical · Neoadjuvant / Preoperative

PRODIGE 23

In locally advanced rectal cancer, does neoadjuvant mFOLFIRINOX-based total treatment improve outcomes compared with standard preoperative chemoradiotherapy?

Trial design and population

Population / eligibility
461 patients aged 18–75 with cT3–4 M0 rectal adenocarcinoma eligible for intensive chemotherapy.
Intervention vs comparator
Six cycles neoadjuvant mFOLFIRINOX, then long-course CRT, surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy vs standard CRT, surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy.
Sample size
461
Primary endpoint
Disease-free survival

Key results

At 3 years, DFS was 76% with the intensified neoadjuvant strategy vs 69% with standard treatment (HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.49–0.97; P=.034). Pathological complete response was about 27.8% vs 12.1%.

Absolute Effect

Absolute 3-year DFS gain 7 percentage points; NNT 15 based on 3-year DFS.

NNT: 15 based on 3-year DFS. Use only at the stated endpoint/time horizon; median survival differences are not converted into NNT.

NNH: Not quantifiable. Grade 3–4 neutropenia, diarrhoea, neuropathy and other triplet chemotherapy toxicities increase treatment burden; irinotecan diarrhoea and oxaliplatin neuropathy require active management.

Time-to-event hazard ratios and median survival are not treated as absolute risk differences. Where no defensible fixed-time NNT/NNH is available, the source-reported endpoint is visualized instead.

Safety and treatment burden

Grade 3–4 neutropenia, diarrhoea, neuropathy and other triplet chemotherapy toxicities increase treatment burden; irinotecan diarrhoea and oxaliplatin neuropathy require active management.

Follow-up, maturity and limitations

Follow-up: Primary report median follow-up approximately 46 months; long-term updates have followed.

Requires fitness for triplet chemotherapy. Control strategy predates some modern total-neoadjuvant options; MRI risk assessment and patient selection matter.

Clinical interpretation

For fit patients with locally advanced rectal cancer, neoadjuvant mFOLFIRINOX-based treatment improves DFS and pCR compared with the trial's CRT-first strategy.
