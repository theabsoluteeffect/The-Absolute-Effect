Thoracic oncology · First-line advanced/metastatic · Phase III

OptiTROP-Lung06

Clinical question: In previously untreated PD-L1 TPS-negative advanced nonsquamous NSCLC without targetable genomic alterations, does sacituzumab tirumotecan plus pembrolizumab improve outcomes over platinum-pemetrexed plus pembrolizumab?

Randomized455 patients · 229 vs 226
PopulationPD-L1 TPS <1% · no targetable drivers
Primary endpointBICR-assessed PFS
Follow-upMedian 9.7 months · interim analysis

Design, population and treatment

International phase III, open-label, multicentre, 1:1 randomized trial. Eligible adults had previously untreated locally advanced or metastatic nonsquamous NSCLC, PD-L1 tumour proportion score below 1%, no targetable genomic alterations, and ECOG performance status 0–1. The primary report describes 455 randomized patients: 229 assigned to sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab and 226 to chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab. Approximately 92% in each arm had stage IV disease and more than 99% had adenocarcinoma histology.

Experimental: Sacituzumab tirumotecan 4 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks plus pembrolizumab 400 mg IV every 6 weeks; pembrolizumab could continue for up to 18 six-week cycles.

Comparator: Pemetrexed 500 mg/m² plus platinum (cisplatin 75 mg/m² or carboplatin AUC 5) every 3 weeks for up to four cycles, with pemetrexed maintenance, plus pembrolizumab 400 mg IV every 6 weeks for up to 18 cycles. The chemotherapy arm used a platinum choice rather than one uniform platinum agent.

Stratification: Sex, ECOG performance status and disease stage. Treatment continued until progression, unacceptable toxicity or completion/withdrawal under protocol.

The NEJM primary report verifies the trial identity, population, randomization and efficacy/safety outcomes. Exact regimen schedules and the 12-month landmark estimates were presented at ESMO Congress 2026, LBA66. The official ESMO webinar page identifies the presentation and presenter; its scientific content is access-controlled. An accessible conference report was used to transcribe the regimen details. The original protocol/registry record was not independently downloadable in this run.

Key efficacy outcome

Primary endpoint: Progression-free survival assessed by blinded independent central review (BICR), using RECIST 1.1. Overall survival was a secondary endpoint and remained immature at this interim analysis.

Median PFS was 15.0 months (95% CI 9.9–not evaluable) versus 5.7 months (95% CI 5.5–7.4); HR 0.49 (95% CI 0.36–0.65), P<0.0001. Twelve-month PFS was 51.1% versus 22.8% (absolute difference 28.3 percentage points).

Objective response rate was 50.2% (95% CI 43.6–56.9) versus 47.3% (40.7–54.1). Median duration of response was 13.6 months (95% CI 9.7–not evaluable) versus 6.8 months (4.2–10.6); HR 0.47 (95% CI 0.29–0.77). Twelve-month duration-of-response rates were 61.7% versus 27.2%.

OS was immature: median OS was not reached in either arm; HR 0.58 (95% CI 0.38–0.88). Twelve-month OS was 77.6% versus 66.4%, reported descriptively; this is not used for the NNT calculation.

Absolute benefit · 12-month PFS by BICR

One additional patient alive and progression-free at 12 months for approximately every four treated.

Calculation: 1 ÷ (0.511 − 0.228) = 3.53; rounded up to NNT 4.

NNT 4

This NNT is calculated from the same-endpoint 12-month PFS landmark estimates, not from median PFS or a hazard ratio. The landmark estimates are Kaplan–Meier estimates and should be interpreted with their follow-up and uncertainty. No NNH is calculated from adverse-event proportions because exposure duration differed between arms and a common fixed toxicity horizon was not established.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 47.6% with sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab versus 60.6% with chemotherapy plus pembrolizumab. Serious TEAEs were 35.8% versus 36.3%; dose reductions due to TEAEs were 4.8% versus 19.9%; fatal TEAEs were 2.2% versus 6.2%.

Common TEAEs included anaemia (82.5% vs 84.5%), decreased neutrophil count (47.2% vs 65.9%) and decreased appetite (40.0% vs 45.0%). Stomatitis was more frequent with sac-TMT (51.1% vs 8.4%); grade 3 stomatitis was 2.2%. Ocular-surface toxicity was 13.5% versus 2.7% and was grade 1–2. Pneumonitis was 10.5% versus 13.7%, with grade ≥3 pneumonitis in 1.7% versus 3.1%.

NNH: Not calculated. The grade ≥3 TEAE proportions are cumulative over treatment exposure, which differed between arms; no common fixed-time toxicity rate was reported. The absolute difference was 13.0 percentage points fewer grade ≥3 TEAEs with the experimental regimen, but this is not converted to an NNH.

Quality of life and treatment burden

A definitive patient-reported quality-of-life advantage was not reported in the accessible primary abstract or the conference summary reviewed for this page. No QoL benefit is inferred. The longer duration of response and lower grade ≥3 TEAE proportion are efficacy and safety findings, not substitutes for patient-reported outcomes.

Interpretation and limitations

OptiTROP-Lung06 demonstrates a substantial interim PFS improvement for sac-TMT plus pembrolizumab in the specific PD-L1 TPS <1%, driver-negative, first-line nonsquamous NSCLC population. The 12-month PFS difference supports an NNT of 4 at that landmark. The result does not establish mature OS benefit: OS was immature and the reported HR was descriptive at this analysis.

Important limitations include the relatively short median follow-up (9.7 months), open-label design, China-based trial population, restriction to PD-L1-negative disease without actionable drivers, and lack of a definitive QoL analysis in the accessible report. The regimen should not be generalized to PD-L1-positive disease or to patients with actionable drivers. The comparison is against platinum-pemetrexed plus pembrolizumab, not against all contemporary first-line strategies.
