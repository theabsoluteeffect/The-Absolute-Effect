Clinical question: In previously untreated advanced nonsquamous NSCLC without an actionable driver and with PD-L1 TPS below 1%, does sacituzumab tirumotecan plus pembrolizumab improve outcomes over pembrolizumab plus platinum-pemetrexed?

Randomized455 (229 vs 226)
RegistryNCT06711900
Primary publicationNEJM · 25 Oct 2026
Primary endpointBICR-assessed PFS

Design, population and treatment

Open-label, randomized phase III trial in adults with previously untreated locally advanced or metastatic nonsquamous NSCLC, PD-L1 TPS below 1%, ECOG 0–1, and no actionable driver for which targeted therapy was indicated. The trial was conducted in China.

Experimental: sacituzumab tirumotecan plus pembrolizumab. Control: pembrolizumab plus platinum-pemetrexed chemotherapy. Exact component doses and schedules have not been independently re-extracted from the primary protocol supplement in this audit; this remains an open verification item and the page is not release-ready.

Key efficacy outcome

Median PFS · sac-TMT + pembrolizumab
15.0 mo
Median PFS · pembrolizumab + chemotherapy
5.7 mo

Median PFS 15.0 versus 5.7 months; HR 0.49 (95% CI 0.36–0.65), P<.001. Objective response rate was 50.2% versus 47.3%; median duration of response was 13.6 versus 6.8 months. Overall-survival data were immature at the primary analysis (reported HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.38–0.88).

Bar lengths are normalized to the larger median PFS; they are not absolute risk reductions or survival probabilities.

The primary report described grade ≥3 adverse events in 47.6% versus 60.6%. Full grade 3–5 event-by-event details and a patient-reported quality-of-life analysis were not available in the abstract-level source reviewed here.

NNT/NNH: not calculated. Median PFS and hazard ratios cannot be converted into NNT; adverse-event proportions lack a verified comparable fixed exposure horizon for NNH.

Interpretation and limitations

The PFS signal is promising for the PD-L1-negative first-line population, but follow-up is short and OS is immature. The trial population and comparator should be considered when applying the result. The regimen should not be described as a guideline-preferred standard without checking the current guideline version and jurisdiction.

Current-practice context (reviewed 19 Nov 2026): NCCN provenance and recommendation category were not verified because the complete current guideline/reference list was inaccessible. Exact protocol doses and the complete safety table remain open.
