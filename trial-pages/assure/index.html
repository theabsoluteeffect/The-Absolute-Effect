ASSURE

Adjuvant sunitinib or sorafenib versus placebo after nephrectomy for high-risk RCC

Clinical question

Does postoperative targeted therapy improve disease-free survival or overall survival after nephrectomy in patients with high-risk renal cell carcinoma?

Trial at a glance

Design
Randomized, placebo-controlled phase III
Population
Resected RCC at substantial risk of recurrence
Interventions
Adjuvant sunitinib or sorafenib
Comparator
Placebo
Primary focus
Disease-free survival

Key result

The trial did not demonstrate a disease-free survival or overall survival advantage for either adjuvant sunitinib or sorafenib compared with placebo.

Absolute Effect interpretation
No convincing postoperative survival benefit demonstrated

The clinically important conclusion is negative: postoperative VEGF-targeted therapy did not provide a convincing survival benefit in an unselected high-risk RCC population, while treatment added toxicity and treatment burden.

What this means clinically

  • Do not interpret a plausible biological rationale as proof of postoperative benefit.
  • The relevant comparison is against observation/placebo after complete surgical resection.
  • Benefit must be weighed against treatment-related adverse effects and discontinuation.
Clinical takeaway

ASSURE is a foundational negative randomized trial in the evolution of adjuvant RCC therapy. It does not support routine postoperative VEGF-targeted therapy for an unselected high-risk RCC population.

Evidence pathway

RCC → Medical Oncology → Adjuvant Therapy → recurrence-risk reduction after nephrectomy.
