In systemic-treatment-naive unresectable HCC, does atezolizumab plus bevacizumab improve outcomes compared with sorafenib?

Population
501 patients with unresectable HCC, predominantly Child-Pugh A and ECOG 0–1, randomized 2:1.
Intervention vs comparator
Atezolizumab 1,200 mg IV plus bevacizumab 15 mg/kg IV every 3 weeks versus sorafenib 400 mg orally twice daily.
Sample size
501
Primary endpoint
Co-primary endpoints: overall survival and independently assessed PFS

Key results

At updated median follow-up 15.6 months, median OS was 19.2 vs 13.4 months (HR 0.66, 95% CI 0.52–0.85; descriptive P<.001); median PFS was 6.9 vs 4.3 months (HR 0.65, 95% CI 0.53–0.81). At 18 months, OS was 52% vs 40%. Grade 3/4 treatment-related AEs occurred in 43% vs 46%; grade 5 treatment-related events 2% vs <1%.

Absolute Effect

18-month OS absolute improvement 12 percentage points; NNT ≈9 at 18 months. Landmark estimate from the updated analysis; interpret with the specified cutoff and survival estimation.

NNT: ≈9, based on 18-month overall survival.

NNH: Not calculated: the selected trial-wide toxicity comparison is not a single, consistently higher fixed-time endpoint (grade 3/4 treatment-related AEs 43% vs 46%; grade 5 2% vs <1%).

Time-to-event interpretation: Hazard ratios and median survival are relative/time-to-event measures; they are not converted into NNT unless a fixed-time absolute event estimate is available.

Toxicity and treatment burden

Bleeding risk from bevacizumab is central, especially with portal hypertension/varices; endoscopic evaluation and treatment of varices before therapy are important. Grade 3/4 treatment-related toxicity was 43% vs 46%; grade 5 events were 2% vs <1%.

Follow-up, limitations and maturity

Follow-up: Median follow-up 15.6 months in the updated analysis.

Evidence applies mainly to Child-Pugh A, ECOG 0–1 patients; benefit-risk in decompensated cirrhosis is uncertain. Open-label trial and bleeding risk require careful selection. This was versus sorafenib, not other modern first-line combinations.

Clinical interpretation

Atezolizumab–bevacizumab improved survival over sorafenib and became a first-line standard for eligible advanced HCC, provided bleeding risk and portal hypertension are assessed.

Primary publication and sources

Finn RS, et al. N Engl J Med. 2020;382:1894–1905; updated analysis Finn RS, et al. J Hepatol. 2021.
