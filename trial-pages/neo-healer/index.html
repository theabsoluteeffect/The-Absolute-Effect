Breast Cancer · HER2-positive · Stage II–III · Neoadjuvant

Neo-Healer / BCTOP-H-N01

In stage II–III ERBB2-positive breast cancer, does neoadjuvant anbenitamab plus albumin-bound docetaxel (HB1801) improve total pathological complete response compared with trastuzumab, pertuzumab and docetaxel?

Design, population and eligibility

Multicentre, open-label, phase 3 registrational randomised clinical trial conducted at 61 hospitals in China. A total of 521 patients were randomised 1:1: 263 to anbenitamab plus HB1801 and 258 to trastuzumab–pertuzumab–docetaxel (THP), with or without carboplatin. Eligible patients had stage II or III ERBB2-positive breast cancer (IHC 3+ or IHC 2+ with in-situ hybridisation amplification), ECOG performance status 0–1, LVEF ≥55%, and no prior systemic therapy. Randomisation was stratified by stage, hormone-receptor status and carboplatin use.

Data cutoff was 28 January 2026. Median follow-up was less than 8 months; long-term EFS, invasive DFS and OS were not reported in this primary analysis.

Treatment and comparator

Experimental: anbenitamab 30 mg/kg IV plus HB1801 (albumin-bound docetaxel) 100 mg/m² IV on day 1 every 3 weeks for 6 cycles, with or without carboplatin AUC 6 every 3 weeks for 6 cycles.

Comparator: trastuzumab (8 mg/kg loading, then 6 mg/kg) plus pertuzumab (840 mg loading, then 420 mg) and docetaxel 75 mg/m² on day 1 every 3 weeks for 6 cycles, with or without carboplatin AUC 6. Carboplatin use was determined by investigators before randomisation based on patient/tumour characteristics.

After surgery, trastuzumab plus pertuzumab was recommended for patients achieving pCR; 14 cycles of T-DM1 were recommended for residual disease. Radiotherapy and endocrine therapy were given when clinically indicated.

Primary endpoint and efficacy

The primary endpoint was total pathological complete response (tpCR; post-neoadjuvant T0 or in-situ disease and N0), assessed by a blinded independent review committee. All 521 randomised patients were included in the intention-to-treat primary analysis.

tpCR was 164/263 (62.4%; 95% CI 56.2–68.2) versus 132/258 (51.2%; 95% CI 44.9–57.4); absolute difference 11.4 percentage points (95% CI 3.2–19.6), P=0.004. Subgroup tpCR was higher with the investigational regimen in HR-positive disease (51.7% vs 44.4%), HR-negative disease (76.3% vs 59.5%), early-stage disease (63.8% vs 51.8%) and locally advanced disease (59.6% vs 50.0%).

Absolute effect and NNT

NNT 9 for one additional tpCR at surgery: 62.4% − 51.2% = 11.4 percentage points; 1/0.114 = 8.77, rounded up to 9. This is an NNT for the pathological response endpoint, not for survival.

Toxicity, treatment burden and quality of life

Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 77/263 (29.3%) versus 73/258 (28.3%). Grade 3 neutropenia occurred in 30/263 (11.4%) versus 28/258 (10.9%). No treatment-related deaths or symptomatic left-ventricular systolic dysfunction were reported. Treatment discontinuation was 13/263 (4.9%) versus 9/258 (3.5%). The paper did not report a validated quality-of-life endpoint; patient-reported adverse events were collected weekly.

Interpretation and limitations

The investigational regimen improved the primary pathological response endpoint with similar overall grade 3–4 treatment-related toxicity. The study does not yet establish an EFS, invasive DFS or OS benefit. It was conducted exclusively in China, follow-up was less than 8 months, carboplatin use was investigator-determined before randomisation, and both investigational agents were introduced together so their individual contributions cannot be separated. Postoperative therapy was recommended according to response and was not the randomized question.

Current-practice context · reviewed 15 November 2026

This is a new phase 3 registrational report, but long-term survival outcomes and external validation remain pending. The study comparator was THP with or without carboplatin; this trial does not establish that the investigational regimen is already a guideline standard. The official NCCN Breast Cancer full guideline/reference list was not accessible in this run, so no NCCN version, recommendation category or citation number is claimed. NCCN mapping remains an open follow-up task.
