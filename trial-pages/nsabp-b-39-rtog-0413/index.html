Breast Cancer · Radiation · Stage 0–II · Partial-breast versus whole-breast irradiation

NSABP B-39 / RTOG 0413

After lumpectomy for stage 0–II breast cancer, does accelerated partial-breast irradiation provide local control equivalent to whole-breast irradiation?

Registry: NCT00103181 · One underlying randomized study; aliases and follow-up reports consolidated.

Design, population and eligibility

Design

Phase III randomized equivalence trial; 4,216 patients randomized (2,107 APBI; 2,109 whole breast).

Population

Stage 0, I or II breast cancer; 61% postmenopausal, 81% hormone-receptor-positive; 24% DCIS, 65% node-negative invasive and 10% pN1.

Eligibility

Lumpectomy with histologically negative margins and 0–3 positive axillary nodes; broad eligibility compared with later selected APBI studies.

Maturity

Median follow-up 10.2 years.

Treatment and comparator

Partial-breast irradiation: brachytherapy 34 Gy or external-beam 3D conformal radiotherapy 38.5 Gy in 10 fractions, twice daily over five treatment days.

Whole-breast irradiation 50 Gy in 25 fractions, with or without tumour-bed boost.

Primary endpoint: Ipsilateral breast tumour recurrence; equivalence margin required upper 90% CI of HR below 1.50.

Other endpoints: Recurrence-free interval, distant disease-free interval, DFS, OS and toxicity.

Key efficacy results

Ten-year IBTR-free rate was 95.2% with APBI versus 96.1% with whole-breast irradiation; corresponding recurrence risks 4.8% versus 3.9%. HR 1.22 (90% CI 0.94–1.58).

The prespecified equivalence criterion was not met because the upper confidence limit exceeded 1.50.

Ten-year recurrence-free interval was 91.8% versus 93.4%; HR 1.32 (95% CI 1.04–1.68), P=0.02. Distant disease-free interval and OS did not differ significantly.

Grade 3 toxicity was 9.6% with APBI versus 7.1% with whole breast; grade 4–5 toxicity 0.5% versus 0.3%.

Absolute effect

The 10-year IBTR risk was approximately 4.8% with APBI and 3.9% with whole-breast irradiation, an absolute difference of 0.9 percentage points. No NNT/NNH is assigned because the equivalence criterion was not met and the comparison was not a superiority trial.

NNT: Not assigned: failure to demonstrate equivalence does not establish a definitive superiority effect or a clinically stable NNT.

NNH: Not assigned; grade 3–5 toxicity differences were small and not converted to NNH without a prespecified significant harm endpoint.

Toxicity, harms and treatment burden

Grade 3 toxicity: 9.6% APBI versus 7.1% whole breast; grade 4–5: 0.5% versus 0.3%.

The APBI arm combined brachytherapy and external-beam techniques; treatment heterogeneity complicates toxicity and cosmesis interpretation.

The study's broad eligibility and twice-daily external-beam regimen limit direct transfer to current, selected APBI practice.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

The primary publication did not establish a clear global QoL advantage for APBI. Treatment convenience must be balanced against local control, toxicity and cosmetic outcomes.

Subgroups, maturity and limitations

The population included DCIS and invasive disease, node-negative and limited node-positive disease, and multiple APBI techniques.

A post hoc impression that selected low-risk patients may do similarly cannot replace the failed overall equivalence test.

Ten-year follow-up is mature for local recurrence; OS and distant outcomes were not significantly different.

Clinical interpretation

NSABP B-39/RTOG 0413 did not meet its prespecified equivalence criterion for APBI versus whole-breast irradiation, despite a small absolute 10-year recurrence difference. It should not be described as proving equivalence.

Current-practice context · 1 November 2026 (full guideline freshness review pending): Modern APBI use is based on selected clinicopathological groups and technique-specific evidence. Avoid extrapolating this broad trial to every current APBI schedule or using its results to claim universal interchangeability.
