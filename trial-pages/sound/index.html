The Absolute Effect / Evidence / SOUND

Breast Cancer · Surgical · Stage I · Axillary staging / surgical de-escalation

SOUND

In women with breast cancer ≤2 cm and negative preoperative axillary ultrasonography, can axillary surgery be omitted when nodal pathology would not change postoperative treatment?

Trial registry: NCT021? · One underlying study; follow-up publications consolidated.

Design, population and eligibility

Design

Multicentre, open-label, phase III randomized non-inferiority trial; 1:1 allocation.

Population

1,463 women enrolled; 1,405 included in the intention-to-treat analysis. Median age 60 years; median tumour size 1.1 cm; 87.8% ER-positive/HER2-negative.

Eligibility

Breast cancer ≤2 cm; clinically node-negative with negative preoperative axillary ultrasound; candidates for breast-conserving treatment. Exclusion/selection criteria and nodal imaging details are in the protocol.

Follow-up

Median 5.7 years for disease assessment.

Treatment and comparator

Sentinel lymph-node biopsy (SLNB), followed by adjuvant treatment according to standard clinicopathological factors.

No axillary surgery; breast surgery and adjuvant management otherwise based on available clinical and tumour information.

Primary endpoint: Five-year distant disease-free survival (DDFS), tested for non-inferiority.

Other endpoints: Distant recurrence, axillary recurrence, DFS, OS and adjuvant treatment recommendations.

Key efficacy results

Five-year DDFS was 97.7% with SLNB and 98.0% without axillary surgery; HR 0.84 (90% CI 0.45–1.54); non-inferiority P=0.02. The ordinary superiority log-rank P value was 0.67.

Locoregional relapses were 12/708 (1.7%) versus 11/697 (1.6%); distant metastases 13 (1.8%) versus 14 (2.0%); deaths 21 (3.0%) versus 18 (2.6%).

In the SLNB arm, 97/708 (13.7%) had positive nodes, illustrating the limited nodal yield in this selected ultrasound-negative population.

Absolute effect

The observed five-year DDFS difference was +0.3 percentage points for omission (98.0% versus 97.7%). The trial's conclusion rests on its prespecified non-inferiority analysis, not on a clinically meaningful superiority effect.

NNT: Not assigned: the comparison was designed for non-inferiority and the observed difference is small; do not interpret the reciprocal of this difference as a treatment NNT.

NNH: Not quantifiable from a harmonized fixed-time adverse-event endpoint in the primary abstract.

Toxicity, harms and treatment burden

SLNB adds an operation and risks of seroma, sensory symptoms, restricted shoulder movement and lymphedema; the trial's principal rationale was to avoid morbidity when nodal information would not change management.

Arm-specific grade 3–5 adverse-event and treatment-discontinuation rates are not consistently reported in the primary abstract; not estimated here.

The trial does not test omission when nodal status is needed to determine systemic therapy or regional nodal irradiation.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

The trial assessed treatment recommendations and patient-relevant consequences, but a numerical QoL effect is not reproduced here because it is not established in the primary abstract. Do not infer a QoL benefit solely from avoiding surgery.

Subgroups, maturity and limitations

Most participants had small, hormone-receptor-positive/HER2-negative cancers; generalizability to larger, biologically aggressive or clinically suspicious disease is limited.

The median follow-up of 5.7 years is adequate for the prespecified five-year primary endpoint but not a lifetime recurrence assessment.

The intervention is a strategy of omitting axillary staging only when missing nodal information does not alter postoperative treatment decisions.

Clinical interpretation

SOUND supports omission of SLNB in carefully selected women with tumours ≤2 cm and negative axillary ultrasound when nodal pathology would not change adjuvant treatment. It does not establish that axillary staging is unnecessary for all clinically node-negative patients.

Current-practice context · reviewed 1 November 2026: Consider contemporary de-escalation guidance and eligibility criteria. The result is most applicable to selected low-risk patients receiving breast-conserving treatment; do not extrapolate to clinically node-positive disease or situations in which nodal status changes RT or systemic treatment.
