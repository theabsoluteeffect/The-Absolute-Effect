Phase III · Randomized · Open-label · Post-EGFR-TKI progression

Dalmelitinib plus aumolertinib

Clinical question: In EGFR-mutant advanced NSCLC with MET amplification after EGFR-TKI resistance, does dual EGFR/MET inhibition improve PFS over platinum-pemetrexed chemotherapy?

Randomized314 (156 vs 158)
RegistryNCT06110663
Primary endpointBICR-assessed PFS
Median follow-up11.2 months

Design, population and treatment

Patients with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC harbouring EGFR exon 19 deletion or L858R and MET amplification by protocol-defined FISH criteria after progression on an EGFR TKI; most had previously received a third-generation EGFR TKI. ECOG performance status was 0–1.

Experimental: dalmelitinib 300 mg orally twice daily plus aumolertinib 110 mg orally once daily. Control: cisplatin 75 mg/m² or carboplatin AUC 5 plus pemetrexed 500 mg/m² every 3 weeks for 4–6 cycles, followed by pemetrexed maintenance. Crossover after progression was permitted.

Key efficacy outcome

Median PFS · dalmelitinib + aumolertinib
6.2 mo
Median PFS · platinum-pemetrexed
4.4 mo

Median PFS was 6.2 versus 4.4 months (HR 0.60, 95% CI 0.45–0.81; P<.001). Objective response rate was 59.6% versus 38.6%; median duration of response was 9.7 versus 4.4 months. Overall-survival data were immature.

Bar lengths are normalized to the larger median PFS; they are not absolute risk reductions.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in approximately 50% versus 51%. A mature overall-survival analysis and a quality-of-life result were not available in the primary report reviewed.

NNT/NNH: not calculated. Median PFS and hazard ratios cannot be converted into NNT. The adverse-event proportions are not paired with a common fixed exposure horizon suitable for NNH.

Interpretation and limitations

The result supports further study of dual EGFR/MET blockade in this biomarker-selected post-TKI population. It should not be generalized to EGFR-mutant disease without MET amplification; OS remains immature and crossover may affect later survival comparisons.

Current-practice context (reviewed 19 Nov 2026): NCCN provenance and recommendation category were not verified because the complete current guideline/reference list was inaccessible.
