The Absolute Effect / Evidence / EORTC 30904
Renal Cell Carcinoma · Surgical Oncology · Localized Disease

EORTC 30904

Clinical question

For small localized RCC, can nephron-sparing surgery preserve renal function without compromising cancer outcomes?

Trial context
Population
541 patients with solitary ≤5 cm T1–T2 N0 M0 renal masses and a normal contralateral kidney
Intervention
Nephron-sparing surgery vs radical nephrectomy
Key endpoint
Overall survival
Time horizon / follow-up
10 years
Absolute Effect

What changes for patients?

In the ITT population, 10-year OS was 75.7% with nephron-sparing surgery vs 81.1% with radical nephrectomy. The trial did not establish non-inferiority. Only 12 of 117 deaths were renal-cancer deaths, and renal function was substantially better after NSS. This study should not be interpreted as evidence that partial nephrectomy is oncologically inferior in modern appropriately selected cT1 RCC.

NNT: Not applicable; non-inferiority not demonstrated

Treatment burden / harm

Moderate renal dysfunction

NNH: Not applicable; renal dysfunction favoured NSS

Relative effect

HR: 1.50 for NSS vs RN

95% CI 1.03–2.16; non-inferiority P=.77

Clinical interpretation

Interpret the absolute difference together with the endpoint, time horizon, relative effect and limitations of the study design.
