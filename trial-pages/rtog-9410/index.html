Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer · Radiation · Locally Advanced / Resectable

RTOG 9410

For unresectable stage III NSCLC, does concurrent chemoradiotherapy improve survival compared with sequential chemoradiotherapy?

Trial design and population

Population / eligibility
610 patients with inoperable stage III NSCLC randomized to sequential or concurrent cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy.
Intervention vs comparator
Sequential chemotherapy then RT vs concurrent cisplatin-based chemotherapy with thoracic RT (60 Gy in the once-daily concurrent arm).
Sample size
610
Primary endpoint
Overall survival

Key results

Median OS was 17.0 months with once-daily concurrent CRT vs 14.6 months with sequential treatment; 5-year OS was 16% vs 10% (P=.046). Concurrent therapy increased acute esophageal toxicity.

Absolute Effect

Absolute 5-year OS difference 6 percentage points; NNT 17 based on 5-year OS. Grade 3+ esophagitis was approximately 22% vs 4%, an 18-point excess; NNH 6 based on grade 3+ esophagitis.

NNT: 17 (5-year OS). Use only at the stated endpoint/time horizon; median survival differences are not converted into NNT.

NNH: ≈6 based on grade 3+ esophagitis during treatment. Grade 3+ esophagitis about 22% vs 4%; NNH ≈6 based on grade 3+ esophagitis. Severe pulmonary and hematologic toxicity and treatment-related deaths also matter.

Time-to-event hazard ratios and median survival are not treated as absolute risk differences. Where no defensible fixed-time NNT/NNH is available, the source-reported endpoint is visualized instead.

Safety and treatment burden

Grade 3+ esophagitis about 22% vs 4%; NNH ≈6 based on grade 3+ esophagitis. Severe pulmonary and hematologic toxicity and treatment-related deaths also matter.

Follow-up, maturity and limitations

Follow-up: Mature long-term follow-up.

Older chemotherapy and RT techniques. Contemporary stage III care often includes consolidation immunotherapy after concurrent CRT; RTOG 9410 established concurrent over sequential CRT, not the full modern regimen.

Clinical interpretation

Concurrent CRT improves long-term survival versus sequential CRT in fit stage III NSCLC patients, at the cost of greater acute esophageal toxicity.
