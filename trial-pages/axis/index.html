The Absolute Effect / Renal Cell Carcinoma / Medical

AXIS

Clinical question

In metastatic RCC progressing after prior systemic therapy, does axitinib improve disease control compared with sorafenib?

Population / design

723 patients randomized 1:1 in a phase III trial; previous treatment included cytokines, sunitinib, bevacizumab plus interferon, or temsirolimus.

Treatment

Axitinib 5 mg twice daily, with protocol-allowed escalation to 7 and 10 mg twice daily, versus sorafenib 400 mg twice daily.

Absolute effect

Median PFS was 6.7 vs 4.7 months; HR 0.665 (95% CI 0.544–0.812), p<0.0001. Objective response was 19.4% versus 9.4%, an absolute increase of 10 percentage points (NNT ≈ 10 for one additional response).

In the prespecified prior-sunitinib subgroup, PFS was approximately 4.8 vs 3.4 months. Final OS analyses did not show a significant OS difference.

Harm / burden

Treatment discontinuation for toxicity occurred in 4% with axitinib versus 8% with sorafenib. Hypertension, diarrhoea and fatigue were prominent with axitinib; palmar-plantar erythrodysaesthesia and alopecia were more characteristic of sorafenib.

Interpretation

AXIS established a PFS advantage for axitinib over sorafenib in previously treated metastatic RCC, without demonstrating a corresponding OS advantage.

Primary source
