The Absolute Effect / Evidence / SAKK 01/10
Germ Cell Tumours · Radiation Oncology · Stage II Seminoma

SAKK 01/10

Clinical question

Can stage IIA/IIB seminoma be treated with markedly de-escalated carboplatin plus involved-node RT?

Trial context
Population
116 eligible patients with stage IIA or IIB classic seminoma
Intervention
Single-dose carboplatin followed by involved-node RT
Key endpoint
3-year progression-free survival
Time horizon / follow-up
Median follow-up 4.5 years
Absolute Effect

What changes for patients?

A de-escalated regimen of one carboplatin cycle followed by involved-node RT achieved 93.7% 3-year PFS. The prespecified 95% target was not met, so this should be viewed as an investigational de-escalation strategy rather than proof of equivalence to standard RT or cisplatin-based chemotherapy. NNT is not applicable in a single-arm study.

NNT: Not applicable; single-arm phase II

Treatment burden / harm

Grade 3–4 treatment-related toxicity

NNH: Not applicable; single-arm study. Grade 3–4 events included neutropenia 4%, thrombocytopenia 3%, vomiting 1%

Relative effect

Single-arm efficacy: 3-year PFS 93.7%

90% CI 88.5–96.6; target 95% not met

Clinical interpretation

Interpret the absolute difference together with the endpoint, time horizon, relative effect and limitations of the study design.
