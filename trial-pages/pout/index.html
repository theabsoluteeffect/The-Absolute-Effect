The Absolute Effect / Evidence index

Upper tract urothelial carcinoma · Medical oncology · Adjuvant after nephroureterectomy

POUT

Clinical question: After radical nephroureterectomy for pT2–T4 and/or node-positive nonmetastatic UTUC, does adjuvant platinum-based chemotherapy improve outcomes compared with surveillance?

Phase III, randomized, open-label trial · N=261 · NCT01993979.

Design, population and treatment

Population

261 patients with muscle-invasive or node-positive, nonmetastatic upper tract urothelial carcinoma after radical nephroureterectomy.

Randomization

Adjuvant chemotherapy n=132; surveillance n=129.

Intervention

Four cycles of gemcitabine plus platinum. Cisplatin or carboplatin was selected according to renal function.

Primary endpoint

Disease-free survival (DFS); overall survival was a prespecified secondary endpoint.

Final update: median follow-up 65 months; 50 DFS events in chemotherapy arm and 67 in surveillance arm.

Five-year DFS: HR 0.55 (95% CI 0.38–0.80; P=.001). Five-year OS: HR 0.68 (95% CI 0.46–1.00; P=.049). The OS analysis is borderline and was not the primary endpoint.

Restricted mean survival time difference at 65 months: 18 months for DFS (95% CI 6–30) and 11 months for OS (95% CI 1–21), favouring chemotherapy.

Interpretation and limitations

POUT provides randomized evidence supporting adjuvant gemcitabine–platinum after nephroureterectomy for patients with pT2–T4 and/or node-positive UTUC. The benefit was consistent across cisplatin/carboplatin regimens and disease stage in the reported analysis. Renal function and postoperative fitness determine platinum eligibility. The OS result is supportive but borderline, with HR CI reaching 1.00.
