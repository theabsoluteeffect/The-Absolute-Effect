Large B-cell lymphoma · Second-line · Phase II

DALY 2-EU

Clinical question: In adults with large B-cell lymphoma who are ineligible for autologous transplantation and require second-line therapy, does the CD20/CD19-directed CAR T-cell product zamtocabtagene autoleucel improve event-free survival compared with R-GemOx?

Randomised phase IITransplant-ineligible LBCL160 randomisedPrimary endpoint: EFS

Design, population and treatment

Open-label, multicentre, randomised phase II study. The primary analysis included 160 patients with large B-cell lymphoma who were ineligible for transplantation and were receiving second-line treatment after first-line therapy; 82 were assigned to zamtocabtagene autoleucel and 78 to R-GemOx.

Intervention: Zamtocabtagene autoleucel (zamto-cel), a tandem CD20/CD19-directed CAR T-cell therapy, given as a single infusion of 2.5 × 106 CAR-positive T cells/kg (maximum dose capped at 100 kg). The accessible primary abstract does not provide all lymphodepletion details.

Comparator: R-GemOx every 14 days for up to 8 cycles: rituximab 375 mg/m² day 1, gemcitabine 1,000 mg/m² and oxaliplatin 100 mg/m² on day 1 or 2. Treatment followed protocol criteria.

Primary endpoint: Event-free survival assessed by a blinded independent review committee. Median follow-up was approximately 17 months at the primary analysis.

Key efficacy outcome

Median EFS was 6.21 months with zamto-cel versus 2.53 months with R-GemOx (HR 0.39, 95% CI 0.27–0.58; p<0.0001). Median PFS was 8.5 versus 3.3 months (HR 0.43, 95% CI 0.28–0.65; p<0.0001). Overall survival was not mature at this analysis.

Safety and quality of life

Grade ≥3 treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 83% of patients receiving zamto-cel and 86% receiving R-GemOx. Frequently reported events included neutropenia (37% vs 22%), anaemia (14% vs 16%) and decreased neutrophil count (26% vs 9%). In the zamto-cel group, cytokine-release syndrome occurred in approximately 8% and immune effector cell-associated neurotoxicity syndrome in approximately 5%. Treatment-related deaths were reported in 2 patients versus 1 patient.

Quantitative patient-reported quality-of-life results were not reported in the accessible primary abstract; no QoL advantage is inferred.

Interpretation and limitations

DALY 2-EU provides phase II randomised evidence that a CD20/CD19-directed CAR T-cell strategy can improve EFS over R-GemOx in a transplant-ineligible second-line LBCL population. The comparison is against R-GemOx rather than every contemporary cellular or bispecific option; longer follow-up and mature OS are needed. The trial should not be interpreted as a direct comparison with other CAR T products.

Current-practice context · reviewed 20 November 2026: The trial is relevant to second-line treatment selection for transplant-ineligible LBCL. Exact NCCN recommendation section/reference provenance was not verified because the complete current guideline and reference list were not accessible in this run.
