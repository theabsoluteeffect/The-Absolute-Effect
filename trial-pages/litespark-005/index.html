Renal cell carcinoma · Medical oncology · Phase III

LITESPARK-005

Belzutifan versus everolimus after prior immune-checkpoint and VEGF-targeted therapy in advanced clear-cell renal cell carcinoma. Primary report: 2024; patient-reported outcomes: 2025. Evidence review: 23 November 2026.

DesignMulticentre, open-label, randomized, active-controlled phase III trial; 1:1 allocation.
Population746 adults with advanced clear-cell RCC, Karnofsky performance status ≥70, measurable disease, progression on or after both PD-1/PD-L1 therapy and a VEGF-targeted agent, and no more than three prior systemic lines.
Dual primary endpointsProgression-free survival (PFS) by blinded independent central review using RECIST 1.1, and overall survival (OS).
Data maturityPFS/response analysis: median follow-up 18.4 months. Second prespecified interim OS analysis: median follow-up 25.7 months. OS was not statistically significant at that analysis.

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility: Adults with advanced clear-cell RCC, Karnofsky performance status ≥70 and measurable disease by RECIST 1.1. Patients had to have progressed during or after treatment with an anti-PD-1/PD-L1 agent and a VEGF tyrosine-kinase inhibitor, given sequentially or in combination, and could have received no more than three prior systemic lines. Previous everolimus was not permitted.

Randomization: 746 participants were assigned 1:1 to belzutifan (n=374) or everolimus (n=372). Randomization was stratified by IMDC prognostic-risk category and the number of previous VEGF-targeted/VEGFR-targeted therapies.

Intervention: Belzutifan 120 mg orally once daily, with protocol-directed dose reductions for toxicity, continued until progression or unacceptable adverse events.

Comparator: Everolimus 10 mg orally once daily until progression or unacceptable adverse events.

Setting: Previously treated advanced clear-cell RCC after exposure to both an immune-checkpoint inhibitor and VEGF-targeted therapy. This is not a first-line comparison and should not be extrapolated to patients who have not received both drug classes.

Primary endpoints: PFS assessed by blinded independent central review and OS. Objective response rate (ORR), safety and patient-reported outcomes were secondary endpoints. The primary publication reports prespecified interim analyses; distinguish the PFS result from the non-significant OS analysis.

PFS: At the first prespecified interim analysis (median follow-up 18.4 months), median PFS was 5.6 months in each group. The PFS hazard ratio was 0.75 (95% CI 0.63–0.90; P=0.002). The 18-month PFS estimates were 24.0% with belzutifan and 8.3% with everolimus, an absolute difference of 15.7 percentage points.

OS: At the second prespecified interim analysis (median follow-up 25.7 months), median OS was 21.4 months with belzutifan and 18.1 months with everolimus. The OS hazard ratio was 0.88 (95% CI 0.73–1.07; P=0.20); 18-month OS was 55.2% versus 50.6%. The OS analysis did not demonstrate a statistically significant difference.

ORR: 21.9% with belzutifan versus 3.5% with everolimus (P<0.001). This is a response proportion assessed over trial follow-up, not a prespecified fixed-time landmark; no NNT is calculated from this comparison.

Absolute effect and NNT

18-month PFS: 24.0% versus 8.3% alive without progression, absolute benefit 15.7 percentage points. Calculation: 1/(0.240−0.083)=6.37; rounded up, NNT 7 to have one additional patient alive without progression at 18 months. This is a time-specific estimate based on the same-horizon Kaplan–Meier rates, not a conversion of the hazard ratio or median PFS.

Absolute PFS benefit 15.7 percentage points (24.0%−8.3%). Calculation: ceil[1/0.157]=7.

OS: The 18-month absolute difference was 4.6 percentage points (55.2%−50.6%), but the interim OS comparison was not statistically significant (HR 0.88; P=0.20); no NNT is assigned.

ORR: The absolute response difference was 18.4 percentage points, but no fixed assessment horizon is specified for an NNT calculation; no NNT is assigned.

Safety and treatment burden

Grade ≥3 adverse events of any cause occurred in 61.8% with belzutifan and 62.5% with everolimus. Grade 5 adverse events occurred in 3.5% and 5.3%, respectively. Adverse-event-related treatment discontinuation was less frequent with belzutifan (5.9% versus 14.7%). Anemia and hypoxia are clinically important on-target toxicities of HIF-2α inhibition and require monitoring and management.

No NNH is calculated: the reported cumulative adverse-event percentages are not presented as comparable fixed-time risks at a common landmark. They are shown descriptively and should not be converted into an NNH by dividing 100 by the difference.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

The prespecified PRO analysis included 366 belzutifan-treated and 354 everolimus-treated participants who received at least one dose and completed at least one PRO assessment. At baseline, questionnaire completion exceeded 94%; at week 17 it exceeded 55% in both groups. At week 17, between-group least-squares mean differences favored belzutifan for FKSI-DRS disease-related symptoms (+1.5 points; 95% CI 0.7–2.2) and EORTC QLQ-C30 global health status/QoL (+6.4 points; 95% CI 3.2–9.6). Physical-functioning difference was 2.5 points (95% CI −0.6 to 5.5); role-functioning difference was 4.2 points (95% CI 0.1–8.4).

Time to deterioration was not significantly different for physical functioning (median 19.3 versus 13.8 months; HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.72–1.20) or role functioning (12.0 versus 10.2 months; HR 0.88, 95% CI 0.69–1.11). PRO findings support symptom/QoL stability with belzutifan versus worsening with everolimus, but the open-label design and incomplete week-17 questionnaire completion should be considered.

Interpretation and limitations

LITESPARK-005 supports belzutifan as an active later-line option for advanced clear-cell RCC after both immune-checkpoint and VEGF-targeted therapy. The clinically important signal is improved PFS at the 18-month landmark and a higher response rate; the median PFS values were identical, illustrating why the survival curves and fixed-time rates are more informative than medians alone in this setting. The interim OS analysis was not statistically significant and should not be described as an OS benefit.

Limitations include the open-label design, the heavily pretreated and selected trial population, interim OS maturity, and the fact that the NNT is a time-specific estimate based on Kaplan–Meier landmark rates. Anemia and hypoxia require active monitoring. These findings do not establish superiority over other active subsequent-line options that were not directly compared.

Current-practice context · reviewed 23 November 2026

The evidence applies to adults with advanced clear-cell RCC who have already received both a PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitor and VEGF-targeted therapy. Belzutifan is a later-line HIF-2α-directed option in this population; the page reports the randomized trial evidence and does not infer a guideline category from the trial result.

The current official NCCN Kidney Cancer full guideline and its reference list were not independently accessible during this review. No NCCN version, section, reference number or recommendation category is claimed. A separate NCCN mapping/freshness task remains open; this access limitation does not invalidate the independently verified primary trial evidence.
