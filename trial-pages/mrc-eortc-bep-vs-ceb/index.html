The Absolute Effect

MRC/EORTC BEP vs CEB

Germ Cell Tumours · Good-Risk Metastatic NSGCT

Clinical question

Can four cycles of BEP provide superior disease control and survival compared with four cycles of CEB in good-risk metastatic nonseminomatous germ-cell tumours?

Trial context

The randomized MRC/EORTC study compared four cycles of bleomycin, etoposide and cisplatin with four cycles of carboplatin, etoposide and bleomycin in patients with good-risk metastatic nonseminomatous germ-cell tumours.

Key result

Four cycles of BEP produced superior disease control and survival compared with CEB. One-year failure-free survival was approximately 91% versus 77%, and 3-year survival approximately 97% versus 90%.

Absolute Effect perspective

The absolute survival difference is clinically meaningful because cure is the objective in good-risk metastatic germ-cell cancer. The result also illustrates that replacing cisplatin with carboplatin is not a neutral substitution when cure rates are the primary endpoint.

Clinical takeaway

BEP became the benchmark cisplatin-based regimen for good-risk metastatic NSGCT on the basis of superior disease control and survival in this randomized comparison. Toxicity, particularly bleomycin-related pulmonary risk, remains relevant to individual regimen selection.
