Breast Cancer · Medical Oncology

APHINITY

In early HER2-positive breast cancer receiving adjuvant chemotherapy and trastuzumab, does adding pertuzumab improve invasive disease-free survival and overall survival?

Population
4804 patients with HER2-positive early breast cancer, predominantly node-positive or otherwise high-risk node-negative disease.
Intervention / comparator
Adjuvant chemotherapy + trastuzumab + pertuzumab versus chemotherapy + trastuzumab + placebo.
Primary/major efficacy result
8-year iDFS: 88.4% vs 85.8%; absolute improvement 2.6 points; NNT 39.
Overall survival
8-year OS: 92.7% vs 92.0%; HR 0.83 (95% CI 0.68–1.02), P=.078; absolute difference 0.7 points.

Absolute Effect

8-year iDFS: 88.4% vs 85.8%; absolute improvement 2.6 points; NNT 39.

Clinical interpretation: The long-term iDFS benefit is concentrated in node-positive disease; 8-year iDFS was 86.1% vs 81.2% in node-positive patients. Overall survival has not shown a statistically significant difference.

Toxicity / treatment burden: Grade ≥3 adverse events were more frequent with pertuzumab; cardiac events remained uncommon. No defensible single-timepoint NNH for severe toxicity.

Important limitations

Open-label design and treatment evolution after progression can affect absolute survival comparisons. Absolute effects are reported only where a reliable common timepoint or directly reported rate permits calculation; otherwise NNT/NNH is not forced from medians or hazard ratios.

Primary source

Loibl S, et al. J Clin Oncol. 2024;42:...; von Minckwitz G, et al. N Engl J Med. 2017;377:122-131.
