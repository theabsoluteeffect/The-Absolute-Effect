Design, population and treatment

Design: Double-blind, placebo-controlled, 1:1 randomized phase III trial with independent dMMR and pMMR cohorts.

Eligibility and population: 816 patients were enrolled: 225 in the dMMR cohort and 591 in the pMMR cohort. The primary efficacy analysis included 225 dMMR patients (112 pembrolizumab, 113 placebo) and 588 pMMR patients (293, 295). The safety populations were 215 dMMR patients (109, 106) and 550 pMMR patients (276, 274); these safety denominators, not the efficacy denominators, underpin the grade ≥3 adverse-event percentages below. Prior adjuvant chemotherapy was permitted after a treatment-free interval of at least 12 months.

Intervention: Pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks with paclitaxel 175 mg/m² and carboplatin AUC 5 for 6 cycles, followed by pembrolizumab 400 mg IV every 6 weeks for up to 14 maintenance cycles.

Comparator: Matched placebo with the same paclitaxel/carboplatin chemotherapy and maintenance schedule.

Randomization and endpoint: Patients were randomized 1:1 within the dMMR and pMMR cohorts. Randomization was stratified by MMR status, ECOG performance status (0/1 versus 2), and prior adjuvant chemotherapy (yes/no). The primary endpoint was investigator-assessed progression-free survival (PFS) within each MMR cohort, assessed using RECIST 1.1.

Key efficacy outcome

At 12 months, dMMR PFS was 74% versus 38% (HR 0.30, 95% CI 0.19–0.48; p<0.001). In pMMR disease, median PFS was 13.1 versus 8.7 months (HR 0.54, 95% CI 0.41–0.71; p<0.001). The dMMR 12-month landmark difference is 36 percentage points; landmark NNT 3 (ceil[1/0.36]).

Data maturity: Initial primary PFS analysis: median follow-up was approximately 12 months in dMMR and 7.9 months in pMMR; OS was immature. The 2025 peer-reviewed secondary analysis also reported immature OS. A later 2026 ASCO abstract and NRG Oncology release describe longer OS follow-up, but their accessible summaries differ slightly in data cut-off/estimates and remain under source-level reconciliation. Primary versus secondary endpoints are kept distinct; no survival estimate is inferred from a hazard ratio or median alone.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade ≥3 adverse events: dMMR 63.3% (69/109) versus 47.2% (50/106); pMMR 55.1% (152/276) versus 45.3% (124/274). Grade 5 adverse events occurred in 1 versus 2 dMMR patients and 6 versus 2 pMMR patients; attribution differs from all-cause grade ≥3 rates.

Delivery and discontinuation: Six 3-weekly chemotherapy cycles followed by up to 14 six-weekly maintenance infusions; immune-mediated toxicity and prolonged treatment duration require consideration.

Quality of life and patient experience

Health-related quality of life was a key secondary outcome assessed only in the pMMR cohort using FACT-En-TOI, the short FACT–GOG Neurotoxicity subscale, PROMIS-Fatigue, PROMIS-Physical Function and a single-item treatment-distress measure. The primary publication describes these instruments but does not provide numerical domain results in its main report; no dMMR PRO assessment was performed. A separate numerical PRO report remains to be checked.

Where a separate patient-reported outcome publication exists, it is treated as a secondary analysis attached to this underlying study and not as a separate trial page. Unextracted domains remain explicitly open rather than inferred.

Secondary and later follow-up evidence

Peer-reviewed secondary analysis: Eskander et al., Nature Medicine 2025;31:1539–1546 (published 5 March 2025; DOI 10.1038/s41591-025-03566-1; PMID 40044930). In this secondary analysis (n=810), OS remained immature: pMMR HR 0.79 (95% CI 0.53–1.17; one-sided nominal P=0.1157) and dMMR HR 0.55 (95% CI 0.25–1.19; one-sided nominal P=0.0617). Exploratory BICR-assessed PFS HRs were 0.64 (95% CI 0.49–0.85; P=0.0008) in pMMR and 0.45 (95% CI 0.27–0.73; P=0.0005) in dMMR. These are secondary/exploratory analyses, not the primary investigator-assessed PFS endpoint.

Later OS update identified, not yet integrated: ASCO 2026 abstract 5502 (DOI 10.1200/JCO.2026.44.16_suppl.5502) and an NRG Oncology release dated 29 May 2026 report longer OS follow-up. The publisher’s full abstract could not be retrieved in this review, and the accessible abstract/release summaries differ slightly in data cut-off and point estimates. The numeric update is therefore held for source-level reconciliation; no 48-month NNT is assigned.

Interpretation and limitations

Adding pembrolizumab to carboplatin/paclitaxel improved PFS in both dMMR and pMMR advanced/recurrent endometrial cancer. The largest relative effect was in dMMR disease; toxicity and maintenance burden must be weighed.

Limitations: PFS was tested separately in dMMR and pMMR cohorts; follow-up at the initial analysis was short and OS was immature. The 2025 peer-reviewed secondary analysis also reported immature OS. Later 2026 meeting material reports more mature OS, but exact estimates are not integrated until the full abstract and subsequent release are reconciled. NNT applies only to 12-month dMMR PFS, not the overall trial.
