FAST-Forward

Clinical question: Can adjuvant breast or chest-wall radiotherapy be shortened from 3 weeks to 5 fractions over 1 week without compromising local control or late normal-tissue outcomes?

Registry: ISRCTN19906132 · One underlying study; aliases and follow-up publications consolidated.

Phase III, multicentre, open-label randomized non-inferiority trialEvidence cut-off: 3 November 2026

Design, population and eligibility

Design

Phase III, multicentre, open-label randomized non-inferiority trial

Sample size

4,110 enrolled; 4,087 included in the 10-year intention-to-treat analysis: 40 Gy/15 fractions n=1,358; 27 Gy/5 fractions n=1,362; 26 Gy/5 fractions n=1,367.

Eligibility

Patients with completely excised early breast cancer requiring adjuvant breast or chest-wall irradiation. The main trial did not establish the same precision for comprehensive nodal irradiation; the axillary substudy is smaller.

Follow-up / maturity

Follow-up and endpoint maturity are stated with the reported efficacy results; further long-term updates are noted where applicable.

Treatment and comparator

40 Gy in 15 fractions over 3 weeks versus 27 Gy in 5 daily fractions over 1 week versus 26 Gy in 5 daily fractions over 1 week. The 26 Gy schedule is the key clinically adopted regimen.

Primary and secondary endpoints

Primary endpoint: ipsilateral breast tumour recurrence at 5 years with a prespecified non-inferiority margin. Ten-year update reports cumulative ipsilateral breast recurrence and late normal-tissue effects; axillary substudy reports 5-year efficacy.

Key efficacy results

At median follow-up 10.1 years, 10-year cumulative ipsilateral breast recurrence was 3.6% (95% CI 2.7–4.9) with 40 Gy, 2.9% (2.1–4.0) with 27 Gy, and 2.1% (1.5–3.1) with 26 Gy. The original 5-year primary analysis established non-inferiority of 26 Gy and 27 Gy to 40 Gy. The numerical 10-year differences do not establish superiority.

Ten-year clinician-reported moderate/marked breast or chest-wall effects: 13.1% with 40 Gy, 19.3% with 27 Gy and 14.4% with 26 Gy.

The 5-fraction schedules met the original non-inferiority objective at 5 years. The 10-year numerical recurrence differences are descriptive; no superiority claim is made.

Absolute effect

At 10 years, the observed cumulative recurrence was 2.1% with 26 Gy/5 versus 3.6% with 40 Gy/15 (descriptive difference 1.5 percentage points). Because this is a non-inferiority trial and the 10-year update is not a superiority test, no NNT for recurrence prevention is assigned. Ten-year moderate/marked late effects were 14.4% with 26 Gy and 13.1% with 40 Gy.

Trial-specific toxicity and treatment burden

Ten-year clinician-reported moderate/marked breast or chest-wall effects: 13.1% (40 Gy), 19.3% (27 Gy) and 14.4% (26 Gy). The 26 Gy schedule showed late effects similar to the 3-week schedule; 27 Gy had a higher rate. These are clinician-reported late effects, not grade 3–5 toxicity rates. Five-fraction treatment reduces attendance from 15 to 5 fractions.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

The main publication emphasizes clinician-reported normal-tissue effects; do not infer a QoL benefit from fewer visits alone. Reduced travel and treatment burden are clear logistical advantages, but patient-reported outcomes should be cited from the relevant dedicated analyses if quantified.

Subgroups and applicability

The 10-year main trial supports breast/chest-wall treatment. The axillary substudy enrolled 469 (466 ITT), with median follow-up 7.0 years and 32 locoregional recurrences; its sample size limits precision for nodal irradiation independently.

Current-practice context · reviewed 3 November 2026

26 Gy in 5 fractions over 1 week is supported as a standard adjuvant breast/chest-wall schedule for appropriate patients. Care is required when applying the regimen to comprehensive regional nodal irradiation; the substudy is reassuring but smaller.

Limitations and critical interpretation

The main trial was designed for non-inferiority, not superiority. The 27 Gy arm had higher late effects. The axillary cohort is smaller and ongoing follow-up is needed. Do not infer 10-year superiority from numerically lower recurrence in the 26 Gy arm.

Bottom line: FAST-Forward provides mature 10-year evidence supporting 26 Gy in five fractions for adjuvant breast/chest-wall RT, with major treatment-burden reduction. Nodal application requires attention to the smaller substudy evidence base.
