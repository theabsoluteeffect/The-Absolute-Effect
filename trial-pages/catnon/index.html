CATNON

In newly diagnosed 1p/19q non-codeleted grade 3 astrocytoma, does temozolomide given after radiotherapy improve survival, and does concurrent temozolomide add benefit?

Population
751 adults with newly diagnosed 1p/19q non-codeleted grade 3 glioma; final molecular analysis identified 444 IDH-mutant tumours
Intervention
Radiotherapy followed by 12 cycles of adjuvant temozolomide, with or without concurrent TMZ
Comparator
Radiotherapy without adjuvant temozolomide

Overall survival

The big takeaway

Adjuvant temozolomide is the critical component. In IDH-mutant, 1p/19q non-codeleted grade 3 astrocytoma, radiotherapy followed by 12 cycles of adjuvant TMZ substantially prolongs survival. The final CATNON analysis did not demonstrate an additional overall-survival benefit from concurrent TMZ during radiotherapy.

In the final analysis, after a median follow-up of 10.9 years and 499 observed events, adjuvant TMZ improved OS in IDH-mutant tumours with HR 0.54 (95% CI 0.42–0.69; P<0.0001). Median OS in patients with IDH-mutant tumours receiving adjuvant TMZ was 12.5 years. By contrast, concurrent TMZ did not significantly improve OS in the IDH-mutant subgroup (HR 0.81, 95% CI 0.63–1.04; P=.09).

The practical treatment sequence established by CATNON is therefore radiotherapy followed by 12 cycles of adjuvant temozolomide, rather than assuming that the Stupp-style concurrent-plus-adjuvant approach automatically adds benefit in this disease.

Absolute Effect interpretation

NNT: not quantifiable for the primary endpoint (overall survival). CATNON is a time-to-event trial and its mature headline result is most naturally expressed through the hazard ratio and median survival rather than a manufactured NNT. A median-survival difference should not be converted into an NNT.

This is an important Absolute Effect principle: NNT not being quantifiable does not mean the treatment lacks a large absolute effect. Here, the clinically meaningful finding is the long-term survival prolongation with adjuvant TMZ in IDH-mutant tumours.

NNH: not quantifiable. No single fixed-time toxicity endpoint provides a sufficiently central and robust basis for a trial-level NNH. Treatment-related toxicity and treatment burden should be reported directly.

Critical review

CATNON is especially important because it separates two commonly conflated concepts: temozolomide given concurrently with radiotherapy and temozolomide given after radiotherapy. The final molecular analysis shows that the clinically important survival effect is concentrated in the adjuvant phase and in IDH-mutant tumours.

The result also illustrates why treatment intensity should not be inferred from another tumour type. The absence of a demonstrated concurrent-TMZ OS benefit means that adding more chemotherapy around radiotherapy is not automatically better. The trial supports a specific sequence: definitive radiotherapy followed by prolonged adjuvant TMZ.
