The Absolute Effect / Germ Cell Tumours / Medical

MRC TE23

Clinical question

In poor-prognosis metastatic germ-cell tumours, does an intensified CBOP/BEP induction regimen produce a higher favourable response rate than standard BEP?

Population / design

89 patients randomized at 16 UK centres: 43 CBOP/BEP and 46 BEP. Patients had at least one IGCCCG poor-prognosis feature; primary sites included testis and mediastinum.

Treatment

Standard BEP: four cycles over 12 weeks. CBOP/BEP: 2 cycles CBOP, 2 cycles BO, then 3 cycles BEP with lower-dose bleomycin.

Absolute effect

Favourable response was 74.4% with CBOP/BEP versus 60.9% with BEP: absolute increase 13.5 percentage points. The study was a phase II trial and was not powered for definitive between-arm PFS/OS comparison.

At 1 year, PFS was 65% versus 43% (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.33–1.06); at 2 years, OS was 67% versus 61%.

Harm / burden

Grade ≥3 toxicity was 96% with CBOP/BEP vs 63% with BEP. Toxicity deaths occurred in 3/43 versus 2/46 patients. The intensified regimen therefore produced a substantial acute toxicity burden.

Interpretation

TE23 generated a higher favourable response signal but did not establish a new standard because of major toxicity, limited sample size and lack of definitive phase III power.

Primary source

European Urology 2015 / MRC TE23.
