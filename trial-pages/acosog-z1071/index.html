Breast Cancer · Surgical · Stage III / locoregional · Diagnosis / Staging / Risk Assessment

ACOSOG Z1071 (Alliance)

Clinical question: In patients presenting with biopsy-proven node-positive breast cancer who become clinically node-negative after neoadjuvant chemotherapy, how reliably does sentinel lymph-node surgery identify residual axillary disease?

Registry: NCT00881361 · One underlying study; aliases and follow-up publications consolidated.

Prospective multicentre diagnostic-accuracy trialEvidence cut-off: 2 November 2026

Design, population and eligibility

Design

Prospective, multicentre study; all patients underwent sentinel-node surgery and completion axillary lymph-node dissection (ALND), allowing calculation of the false-negative rate.

Sample size

756 women enrolled; 663 evaluable with cN1 disease; 649 underwent neoadjuvant chemotherapy followed by SLN surgery and ALND. Primary FNR analysis included 525 cN1 patients with ≥2 sentinel nodes removed.

Eligibility

Clinical T0–4, N1–2, M0 breast cancer treated with neoadjuvant chemotherapy. The primary FNR analysis focused on cN1 patients with at least two sentinel nodes identified.

Follow-up / maturity

Diagnostic-accuracy endpoint assessed at surgery; subsequent clipped-node analyses refined the technique. Not a randomized comparison of ALND omission.

Treatment and comparator

Procedure evaluated: Sentinel lymph-node surgery after neoadjuvant chemotherapy, followed by completion ALND in the study to establish the reference standard. Dual mapping with blue dye and radiolabelled colloid was encouraged.

Comparator: ALND served as the reference standard, not as a randomized treatment arm. The study was designed to estimate the false-negative rate (FNR).

Primary and secondary endpoints

Primary endpoint: FNR of SLN surgery among initially cN1 patients with at least two sentinel nodes examined. Secondary analyses included nodal pCR, mapping technique and number of sentinel nodes retrieved.

Key efficacy results

Among 525 cN1 patients with ≥2 SLNs removed, 215 had no residual nodal cancer, giving nodal pCR 41.0% (95% CI 36.7–45.3). Among patients with residual nodal disease, 39 had negative SLNs but positive ALND nodes: FNR 12.6% (90% Bayesian credible interval 9.85–16.05%). This exceeded the prespecified 10% target.

FNR was 10.8% with dual tracer versus 20.3% with a single mapping agent; 9.1% with ≥3 SLNs versus 21.1% with two SLNs. In the clipped-node follow-up, FNR was 6.8% when the clipped node was retrieved within the SLN specimen versus 19.0% when the clipped node was found only in the ALND specimen.

These are diagnostic-accuracy measures, not recurrence or survival rates. The clipped-node analysis is a subsequent technical analysis of the same trial cohort.

Absolute effect

NNT/NNH is not applicable to the diagnostic-accuracy primary endpoint. The clinically relevant measure is FNR: 12.6% overall in the specified ≥2-node subgroup, improved with dual tracer, retrieval of ≥3 nodes and confirmation of the clipped node.

Trial-specific toxicity and treatment burden

Because ALND was performed as a reference standard, the trial does not compare surgical morbidity between SLNB alone and ALND. It therefore cannot quantify an NNT/NNH for lymphedema or shoulder morbidity. The clinical rationale for improved staging is to avoid unnecessary ALND when nodal response is complete while not missing residual disease.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

No randomized QoL comparison was conducted. Patient-reported morbidity and arm function were not the primary focus of this diagnostic-accuracy study.

Subgroups and applicability

FNR improved with dual tracer and retrieval of ≥3 nodes. In the clipped-node follow-up, confirming removal of the originally biopsy-positive clipped node reduced FNR to 6.8%. These technical findings underpin targeted axillary dissection (TAD); they do not independently validate omission of ALND for residual nodal disease.

Current-practice context · reviewed 2 November 2026

Contemporary post-NACT axillary staging in initially node-positive patients generally emphasizes marking the biopsy-proven positive node, dual-tracer mapping, retrieval of the clipped node and multiple sentinel nodes. Residual nodal disease still requires management according to current surgical and regional nodal treatment guidance.

Limitations and critical interpretation

This was not randomized to SLNB alone versus ALND and cannot prove oncologic safety of omitting ALND. FNR was above the prespecified threshold in the primary analysis. The clipped-node analysis is a later technical subset analysis, not a separate randomized trial.

Bottom line: Z1071 showed that conventional SLNB after NACT in initially cN1 disease had an unacceptably high FNR. Its legacy is technical refinement—dual tracer, ≥3 nodes and clipped-node retrieval—not blanket ALND omission.
