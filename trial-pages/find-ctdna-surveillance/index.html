Colorectal cancer · Surveillance · Phase III randomized trial

FIND

A randomized surveillance-strategy trial testing whether serial circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA) testing can increase the proportion of patients with recurrence who receive curative-intent metastasis-directed treatment.

DesignPhase III, open-label, multicentre RCT
PopulationStage I–III CRC after R0 resection
Randomized728; mITT 584
Primary endpointCurative-intent treatment among patients with recurrence

Design, population and treatment

FIND was a prospective, multicentre, open-label, 1:1 randomized phase III trial conducted at six academic centres in China. Adults with histologically confirmed stage I–III colorectal cancer who underwent curative-intent R0 resection were assigned to ctDNA-guided surveillance or standard surveillance. A total of 728 patients were randomized (363 vs 365); the modified intention-to-treat population comprised 584 patients (289 vs 295) after prespecified post-randomization exclusions.

Eligibility included age 18 years or older, resected stage I–III CRC, ECOG performance status 0–2, no radiographic distant metastasis before surgery, and anticipated survival of at least 12 months. The primary analysis was conducted in the modified intention-to-treat population.

Surveillance strategies

ctDNA-guided arm

  • Plasma ctDNA before surgery, within 1 month after surgery, then every 3 months for 2 years.
  • A positive ctDNA result triggered immediate contrast-enhanced CT.
  • If CT was negative, CT was repeated every 2 months and ctDNA every 3 months until two consecutive negative ctDNA tests; thereafter, standard imaging surveillance resumed.

Control arm

  • Standard postoperative imaging and clinical follow-up according to the study protocol.
  • No serial ctDNA-triggered imaging pathway.

Key efficacy outcome

Primary endpoint: proportion of patients with radiologically confirmed recurrence who received curative-intent metastasis-directed treatment. The endpoint is conditional on recurrence, rather than a population-level event-free or overall-survival endpoint.

Bars are normalized within each comparison. Denominators and endpoint definitions are shown explicitly; these are not population-level NNT estimates.

  • Primary endpoint: 25/52 (48.1%) versus 13/55 (23.6%); risk ratio 2.03 (95% CI 1.19–3.57), P=.008; reported absolute difference 24.4 percentage points.
  • Recurrence rates were similar: 52/289 (18.0%) versus 55/295 (18.6%); HR 1.02 (95% CI 0.70–1.49), P=.919.
  • Median time to clinical recurrence was 9.5 versus 13.4 months (P<.001), a median lead-time difference of 3.9 months.
  • Among patients with recurrence confined to liver and/or lung, curative-intent metastasis-directed treatment was more frequent in the ctDNA-guided arm (22/52, 42.3% versus 10/55, 18.2%; P=.002).
  • Post-recurrence PFS favoured ctDNA-guided surveillance (HR 0.51, 95% CI 0.30–0.87; P=.014). Overall-survival benefit has not been established; follow-up was 23.3 months (range 3.0–32.1).

NNT/NNH assessment

NNT/NNH not calculated. The primary endpoint is conditional on a post-randomization event (recurrence), so the recurrence subgroup is not a baseline randomized population. The conditional 24.4-point difference must not be converted into a population-level NNT. Recurrence incidence itself was similar between arms, and mature OS data are unavailable.

Safety and absolute effect

This was a surveillance intervention rather than a systemic-treatment comparison. The primary publication did not report a comparative grade 3–5 treatment-toxicity endpoint. Additional blood sampling, contrast imaging and radiation exposure are potential burdens, but their comparative rates were not quantified in the accessible primary report. Patient-reported outcomes were planned; mature QoL results were not reported.

Interpretation and limitations

ctDNA-guided surveillance increased curative-intent treatment among patients who developed recurrence, without reducing the observed recurrence rate. This supports a potential role in earlier identification of oligometastatic recurrence, but does not yet establish improved DFS or OS. The primary endpoint is conditional on recurrence; the analysis was conducted in a modified intention-to-treat population; follow-up was short; and the study was conducted in six academic centres in China using a specific assay and imaging pathway. Longer follow-up, external validation, patient-reported outcomes and cost-effectiveness data are needed before changing routine surveillance pathways.

Current-practice context · reviewed 27 November 2026

ctDNA-guided intensified surveillance increased curative-intent metastasis-directed treatment among patients who developed recurrence, but the reported interim analysis did not establish a recurrence or survival benefit. ctDNA-guided changes to adjuvant treatment remain investigational; this result should not be used to escalate systemic therapy by itself.
