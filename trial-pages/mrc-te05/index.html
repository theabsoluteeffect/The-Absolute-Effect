The Absolute Effect / Evidence / MRC TE05
Germ Cell Tumours · Medical Oncology · Stage I Non-seminoma

MRC TE05

Clinical question

Can short-course BEP substantially reduce relapse in high-risk stage I non-seminoma?

Trial context
Population
114 eligible patients with high-risk clinical stage I NSGCT
Intervention
Two cycles adjuvant BEP in high-risk stage I NSGCT
Key endpoint
Relapse
Time horizon / follow-up
Median follow-up 4 years
Absolute Effect

What changes for patients?

Two cycles of BEP produced very low relapse rates in this prospective high-risk stage I cohort: two relapses occurred among 114 eligible patients, including one patient without germ-cell tumour on central pathology review; among the 104 patients confirmed to have GCT, only one relapsed. Because the study was single-arm, a causal NNT cannot be assigned. The study supports the effectiveness of short-course cisplatin-based adjuvant therapy but does not provide a randomized absolute treatment effect.

NNT: Not applicable; single-arm prospective study

Treatment burden / harm

Treatment-related toxicity

NNH: Not quantifiable as an NNH; one treatment-related cerebrovascular death occurred and pulmonary-function changes were monitored

Relative effect

Relapse rate: 2 relapses among 104 histologically confirmed GCT patients

95% CI excluded a true relapse rate >5% in confirmed GCT

Clinical interpretation

Interpret the absolute difference together with the endpoint, time horizon, relative effect and limitations of the study design.
