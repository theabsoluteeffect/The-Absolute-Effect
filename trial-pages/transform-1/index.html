Myelofibrosis · Haemato-Oncology · Phase III

TRANSFORM-1

JAK-inhibitor-naive intermediate-2/high-risk myelofibrosis; positive spleen-volume primary endpoint with no significant parallel symptom-score benefit.

DesignRandomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase III
Population252 patients; navitoclax+ruxolitinib n=125, placebo+ruxolitinib n=127
Primary endpointSVR35 at week 24
Data maturityWeek-24 primary response; longer-term outcomes ongoing

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 252 JAK-inhibitor-naive adults with intermediate-2 or high-risk myelofibrosis and ECOG performance status ≤2 were randomized 1:1: navitoclax plus ruxolitinib n=125, placebo plus ruxolitinib n=127.

Intervention: Navitoclax 200 mg orally once daily (or 100 mg with escalation to 200 mg) plus ruxolitinib dosed according to the approved label and platelet count.

Comparator: Placebo plus ruxolitinib dosed according to the approved label.

Primary endpoint: ≥35% spleen-volume reduction from baseline at week 24.

Follow-up: Primary week-24 response; progression and survival follow-up continue.

Registry: NCT04472598.

Primary SVR35 at week 24 was achieved in 63.2% versus 31.5%, P<0.0001; absolute difference 31.7 percentage points. SVR35 at any time occurred in 76.8% versus 44.1% (nominal P<0.0001). Mean total symptom score change at week 24 was −10.2 versus −11.6, P=0.2852, showing no parallel symptom-score advantage. Exploratory ≥20% variant allele-frequency reduction occurred in 58.5% (95% CI 49.0–67.5) versus 45.5% (36.4–54.8).

Safety and absolute effect

Grade 3–4 thrombocytopenia occurred in 54.0% versus 19.2%, grade 3–4 neutropenia in 40.3% versus 8.8%, and any-grade diarrhoea in 41.9% versus 16.8%. Cytopenias were generally manageable and reversible with dose adjustments.

NNT 4

63.2% versus 31.5%; absolute difference 31.7 percentage points at week 24. Calculation: ceil(100 / 31.7) = 4.

Quality of life and treatment burden

Total symptom score change did not differ significantly at week 24 (−10.2 vs −11.6; P=0.2852); the primary spleen-volume endpoint therefore should not be equated with symptomatic benefit.

Interpretation and limitations

TRANSFORM-1 improves spleen-volume response, but the symptom-score endpoint did not show a statistically significant parallel benefit. It is positive for its primary endpoint, not a uniformly positive patient-reported outcome trial.

The primary spleen-volume response is a surrogate. No significant parallel TSS benefit was shown, and navitoclax adds substantial thrombocytopenia/neutropenia. Long-term progression and survival remain follow-up questions.

Current-practice context · reviewed 17 November 2026

Interpret spleen-volume response alongside symptoms, cytopenias, platelet monitoring and dose interruptions. NCCN provenance is not verified because the complete guideline/reference list was inaccessible; no recommendation category or citation number is assigned.
