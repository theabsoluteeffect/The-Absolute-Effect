Gynaecological Oncology · Cervical cancer · Low-risk early-stage surgery

SHAPE

In carefully selected women with low-risk early-stage cervical cancer, is simple hysterectomy non-inferior to radical hysterectomy for pelvic recurrence?

Design, population and eligibility

International, multicentre, randomised phase 3 non-inferiority trial. Seven hundred patients were randomised 1:1 to simple hysterectomy or radical hysterectomy. Eligibility was restricted to low-risk disease: FIGO 2009 stage IA2–IB1, tumour size ≤2 cm, limited stromal invasion and no radiologically suspicious nodes; squamous-cell carcinoma, adenocarcinoma and adenosquamous carcinoma were eligible.

The primary endpoint was pelvic recurrence at 3 years, with a prespecified non-inferiority margin of 4 percentage points. Median follow-up was approximately 4.5 years.

Treatment and comparator

Intervention: simple hysterectomy with pelvic lymph-node assessment.

Comparator: radical hysterectomy with pelvic lymph-node assessment.

Three-year pelvic recurrence was 2.52% after simple hysterectomy versus 2.17% after radical hysterectomy; absolute difference 0.35 percentage points (90% CI −1.62 to 2.32), meeting the non-inferiority criterion.

Absolute effect and NNT/NNH

NNT/NNH not calculated. This was a non-inferiority trial, not a superiority trial. The 0.35-point recurrence difference is small and its confidence interval crosses zero; an NNT/NNH would imply a superiority or harm estimate not established by the design.

Surgical morbidity and quality of life

Surgery-related adverse events occurred in 42.6% after simple hysterectomy versus 50.6% after radical hysterectomy (P=0.04). Urinary retention within 4 weeks was 0.6% versus 11.0%, and beyond 4 weeks 0.6% versus 9.9%. Urinary incontinence within 4 weeks was 2.4% versus 5.5%, and beyond 4 weeks 4.7% versus 11.0%. Patient-reported urinary and sexual function outcomes favoured simple hysterectomy at selected time points.

The primary report does not provide a single consolidated grade 3–5 toxicity rate; the reported surgery-related event and functional outcomes should not be misrepresented as zero grade 3–5 events. Patient-reported follow-up completion declined over time, limiting later estimates.

Interpretation and limitations

Simple hysterectomy was non-inferior to radical hysterectomy for pelvic recurrence in a narrowly selected low-risk population and reduced urinary morbidity. The findings do not support de-escalation for larger tumours, deep stromal invasion, suspicious nodes or patients outside the trial criteria. The trial does not establish equivalence for all early cervical cancer.

Current-practice context · reviewed 15 November 2026

The NCCN Cervical Cancer Version 2.2026 guideline article record is indexed in PubMed, but the complete guideline and reference list were not accessible in this run; no NCCN section, category or reference number is claimed. The ESGO/ESTRO/ESP 2023 guideline predates the SHAPE publication and cannot be used to infer post-SHAPE recommendation wording. Apply the trial only to patients matching its low-risk eligibility and verify the current local guideline pathway.
