Cervical Cancer · Surgical · Localized / Early-stage

SHAPE

In carefully selected patients with low-risk early-stage cervical cancer, can simple hysterectomy replace radical hysterectomy without compromising pelvic control?

Population and eligibility

700 patients with low-risk cervical cancer, principally FIGO 2009 IA2–IB1, lesions ≤2 cm; pelvic lymph-node assessment was performed.

Sample size

700 randomized (350 per group); median follow-up 4.5 years

Treatment and comparator

Simple hysterectomy with pelvic lymph-node assessment vs radical hysterectomy with pelvic lymph-node assessment.

Featured endpoint

3-year pelvic recurrence (non-inferiority margin 4 percentage points)

Analysis horizon

3 years

Registry: NCT01658930.

Key results and relative effect

Three-year pelvic recurrence 2.52% after simple hysterectomy vs 2.17% after radical hysterectomy. Absolute difference 0.35 percentage points; 90% CI −1.62 to 2.32 percentage points. Simple hysterectomy met the prespecified non-inferiority criterion.

Absolute Effect

Primary endpoint: Pelvic recurrence at 3 years was a non-inferiority outcome (2.52% after simple hysterectomy vs 2.17% after radical hysterectomy). Do not calculate an NNT from this non-inferiority comparison.

Secondary urinary morbidity: Persistent urinary retention beyond 4 weeks occurred in 2/338 (0.6%) after simple versus 34/344 (9.9%) after radical hysterectomy: absolute reduction 9.3 percentage points; NNT 11 (ceil[100/(9.9−0.6)]). Persistent urinary incontinence beyond 4 weeks occurred in 16/338 (4.7%) versus 38/344 (11.0%): absolute reduction 6.3 points; NNT 16 (ceil[100/(11.0−4.7)]).

NNH: Not calculated; the reported functional outcomes show benefit, and no defensible fixed-time excess-harm endpoint is established for NNH.

Trial-specific toxicity and treatment burden

Simple hysterectomy reduced urinary morbidity. Urinary incontinence beyond 4 weeks was 4.7% after simple versus 11.0% after radical hysterectomy (P=0.003); urinary retention beyond 4 weeks was 0.6% versus 9.9% (P<0.001). Within 4 weeks, incontinence was 2.4% versus 5.5% (P=0.048) and retention 0.6% versus 11.0% (P<0.001).

This is a surgical de-escalation trial, not a systemic therapy study; CTCAE grade 3–5 treatment toxicity is not the relevant endpoint. The primary publication reports patient-reported outcomes and surgical morbidity; do not convert the non-inferiority recurrence result into an NNT.

Subgroups, maturity and limitations

This is a non-inferiority/de-escalation result, not evidence that simple hysterectomy is superior. Applicability depends on strict low-risk eligibility, tumour size, stromal invasion and nodal assessment.

Distinguish prespecified from exploratory subgroups, mature from immature endpoints, and superiority from non-inferiority. Later analyses may use different cutoffs and safety definitions.
