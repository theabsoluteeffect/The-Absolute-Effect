The Absolute Effect

NSABP B-18

Breast Cancer · Neoadjuvant Therapy

Clinical question

Does neoadjuvant doxorubicin plus cyclophosphamide improve surgical outcomes and long-term disease control compared with postoperative chemotherapy in operable breast cancer?

Trial context

NSABP B-18 was a foundational randomized trial comparing chemotherapy given before surgery with the same chemotherapy given after surgery in women with operable breast cancer.

Key findings

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy increased clinical and pathologic tumour response and increased the proportion of women able to undergo breast-conserving surgery. Long-term overall survival was not improved simply by changing the timing of the same chemotherapy.

Absolute Effect perspective

The major benefit of the neoadjuvant approach in B-18 is surgical downstaging and assessment of treatment response in vivo, rather than a demonstrated survival advantage over postoperative chemotherapy. Response endpoints should therefore not be presented as substitutes for patient-important survival outcomes.

Clinical takeaway

NSABP B-18 established the clinical value of preoperative systemic therapy in operable breast cancer and provided the foundation for later neoadjuvant trials using pathologic response to guide treatment development.
