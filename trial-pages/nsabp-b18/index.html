NSABP B-18

Neoadjuvant doxorubicin plus cyclophosphamide versus postoperative chemotherapy

Clinical question

Does neoadjuvant doxorubicin plus cyclophosphamide improve surgical outcomes and long-term disease control compared with postoperative chemotherapy in operable breast cancer?

Trial at a glance

Design
Foundational randomized trial
Population
Women with operable breast cancer
Comparison
Same chemotherapy before versus after surgery
Key outcomes
Clinical/pathologic response, breast conservation, survival

Key findings

Neoadjuvant chemotherapy increased clinical and pathologic tumour response and increased the proportion of women able to undergo breast-conserving surgery. Long-term overall survival was not improved simply by changing the timing of the same chemotherapy.

Absolute Effect perspective
Benefit was primarily surgical downstaging, not survival

The major benefit of the neoadjuvant approach in B-18 is surgical downstaging and assessment of treatment response in vivo, rather than a demonstrated survival advantage over postoperative chemotherapy.

Clinical takeaway

NSABP B-18 established the clinical value of preoperative systemic therapy in operable breast cancer. Response endpoints should not be presented as substitutes for patient-important survival outcomes.
