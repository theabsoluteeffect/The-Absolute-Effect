The Absolute Effect / Evidence / BIG 1-98

Breast Cancer · Medical · Stage I–III · Adjuvant endocrine therapy

BIG 1-98

In postmenopausal women with hormone-receptor-positive early breast cancer, does adjuvant letrozole improve outcomes compared with tamoxifen, and do sequential regimens improve on letrozole alone?

Trial registry: NCT00004205 · One underlying study; follow-up publications consolidated.

Design, population and eligibility

Design

International phase III randomized trial with two-arm and four-arm randomization options.

Population

8,010 patients in the trial; monotherapy comparison included 4,922 women randomized to five years of letrozole or tamoxifen.

Eligibility

Postmenopausal women with steroid hormone receptor-positive early breast cancer after surgery; adjuvant chemotherapy permitted as clinically indicated.

Maturity

Primary long-term monotherapy analysis at median 8.7 years; later follow-up reported at longer intervals.

Treatment and comparator

Letrozole 2.5 mg orally once daily for five years; sequential arms used two years of one endocrine agent followed by three years of the other.

Tamoxifen 20 mg orally once daily for five years; four-arm randomization also included tamoxifen→letrozole and letrozole→tamoxifen sequences.

Primary endpoint: Disease-free survival (DFS) in the randomized adjuvant endocrine strategy.

Other endpoints: Overall survival, distant recurrence-free interval, breast cancer-free interval and treatment tolerability.

Key efficacy results

At median follow-up 8.7 years, ITT DFS HR for letrozole versus tamoxifen was 0.86 (95% CI 0.78–0.96); OS HR 0.87 (95% CI 0.77–0.999).

Distant recurrence-free interval HR 0.86 (95% CI 0.74–0.998); breast cancer-free interval HR 0.86 (95% CI 0.76–0.98).

In the sequential-treatment analysis, no significant differences were found for the prespecified endpoints comparing either sequence with letrozole monotherapy. Eight-year ITT DFS estimates were 78.6% for letrozole, 77.8% for letrozole→tamoxifen and 77.3% for tamoxifen→letrozole.

Absolute effect

The ITT DFS hazard was reduced by 14% with letrozole (HR 0.86). A comparator-specific fixed-time DFS pair is not reproduced here from the primary abstract; therefore no absolute NNT is assigned.

NNT: Not calculated from the HR; fixed-time comparator rates must be taken from the full Kaplan–Meier analysis before assigning NNT.

NNH: Not quantified as a fixed-time grade ≥3 adverse-event endpoint.

Toxicity, harms and treatment burden

Tamoxifen: more thromboembolic events, vaginal bleeding and vasomotor symptoms. Letrozole: more arthralgia/myalgia, vaginal dryness, osteoporosis and fractures; cardiac events require context.

Sequential therapy did not improve efficacy over letrozole monotherapy in the randomized comparison; it may be considered for tolerability and individual risk.

Trial-specific grade 3–5 toxicity and treatment discontinuation rates are not consistently captured in the cited long-term abstract; no rates are fabricated.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

Quality-of-life and symptom trade-offs differ by endocrine agent. The choice may depend on baseline bone health, thromboembolic risk, arthralgia and patient preference; do not imply a global QoL advantage from DFS results alone.

Subgroups, maturity and limitations

Long-term follow-up found continued trends favouring letrozole, with time-dependent contralateral breast cancer effects and potential carryover from tamoxifen.

The monotherapy and sequential-treatment populations arise from different randomization options; do not pool denominators or compare non-randomized treatment sequences as if they were one four-arm trial.

The later long-term follow-up HR for DFS was 0.91 (95% CI 0.81–1.01), emphasizing attenuation and the importance of follow-up and analysis population.

Clinical interpretation

BIG 1-98 established an aromatase inhibitor as an effective adjuvant endocrine option in postmenopausal hormone-receptor-positive early breast cancer. Letrozole improved DFS and showed a modest OS advantage versus tamoxifen in the 8.7-year ITT analysis; sequential switching did not outperform letrozole monotherapy.

Current-practice context · 1 November 2026 (full guideline freshness review pending): Adjuvant endocrine therapy is individualized by menopausal status, recurrence risk, tolerance and bone/thrombotic comorbidity. The historical five-year comparisons should be integrated with contemporary duration, ovarian suppression and targeted adjuvant strategies.
