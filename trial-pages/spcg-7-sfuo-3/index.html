Prostate Cancer · Radiation · Locally advanced/high-risk localized disease

SPCG-7/SFUO-3

In locally advanced prostate cancer, does adding local radiotherapy to endocrine therapy reduce prostate-cancer mortality?

Population875 men; 78% had T3 disease, PSA <70, N0M0.InterventionEndocrine therapy plus local radiotherapy.ComparatorEndocrine therapy alone.Primary endpointProstate-cancer-specific survival.

Absolute Effect

10-year prostate-cancer mortality
11.9% vs 23.9% (−12.0 points)

RR 0.44 (95% CI 0.30–0.66).

10-year overall mortality
29.6% vs 39.4% (−9.8 points)

RR 0.68 (95% CI 0.52–0.89).

Other outcomes

PSA recurrence was markedly lower with combined therapy: 25.9% versus 74.7% at 10 years. Urinary, rectal and sexual problems were somewhat more frequent after adding RT.

Clinical interpretation

This trial provides direct randomized evidence that local RT adds substantial disease-specific and overall survival benefit to endocrine therapy in locally advanced/high-risk localized prostate cancer.

The absolute mortality reduction is large enough to make the effect clinically tangible, while toxicity and treatment duration remain part of the trade-off.

NNT / NNH

At 10 years, NNT ≈ 9 to prevent one death from prostate cancer and ≈ 11 to prevent one death from any cause. A fixed-time NNH is not calculated because the pivotal report does not provide a single harmonized severe-toxicity endpoint.
