Bladder Cancer · Medical Oncology

DANUBE

In previously untreated unresectable locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma, does durvalumab alone or durvalumab plus tremelimumab improve overall survival over platinum chemotherapy?

Population
1032 patients with untreated unresectable locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma.
Intervention / comparator
Durvalumab 1500 mg IV every 4 weeks; or durvalumab 1500 mg + tremelimumab 75 mg every 4 weeks for four doses followed by durvalumab; versus gemcitabine-platinum chemotherapy for up to six cycles.
Primary/major efficacy result
Neither coprimary endpoint was met: durvalumab OS HR 0.89 (95% CI 0.71–1.11), P=.30 in high PD-L1 disease; durvalumab+tremelimumab OS HR 0.85 (95% CI 0.72–1.02), P=.075 in the ITT population.
Overall survival
Median OS: 14.4 vs 12.1 months for durvalumab versus chemotherapy in high PD-L1 disease; 15.1 vs 12.1 months for durvalumab+tremelimumab versus chemotherapy in ITT.

Absolute Effect

Neither coprimary endpoint was met: durvalumab OS HR 0.89 (95% CI 0.71–1.11), P=.30 in high PD-L1 disease; durvalumab+tremelimumab OS HR 0.85 (95% CI 0.72–1.02), P=.075 in the ITT population.

Clinical interpretation: The trial illustrates that activity and response do not necessarily translate into a statistically significant survival benefit in an unselected first-line urothelial population.

Toxicity / treatment burden: Grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs: 14% durvalumab, 27% durvalumab+tremelimumab, 60% chemotherapy. NNH not applicable because severe toxicity was lower, not higher, with immunotherapy.

Important limitations

Open-label design and treatment evolution after progression can affect absolute survival comparisons. Absolute effects are reported only where a reliable common timepoint or directly reported rate permits calculation; otherwise NNT/NNH is not forced from medians or hazard ratios.

Primary source

Powles T, et al. Lancet Oncol. 2020;21:1574-1588.
