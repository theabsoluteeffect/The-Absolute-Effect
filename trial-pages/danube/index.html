Bladder Cancer · Medical Oncology

DANUBE

In untreated unresectable locally advanced/metastatic urothelial carcinoma, do durvalumab or durvalumab + tremelimumab improve OS over platinum chemotherapy?

Population
1,032 patients; ECOG 0–1; untreated unresectable locally advanced/metastatic urothelial carcinoma.
Treatment
Durvalumab 1500 mg IV every 4 weeks; or durvalumab 1500 mg + tremelimumab 75 mg every 4 weeks for up to four doses followed by durvalumab; versus gemcitabine + cisplatin/carboplatin for up to six cycles.
Coprimary OS
Durvalumab vs chemotherapy: HR 0.89 (95% CI 0.71–1.11), P=.30 in high PD-L1 disease.
Combination OS
Durvalumab+tremelimumab vs chemotherapy: HR 0.85 (95% CI 0.72–1.02), P=.075 in ITT. Median OS 15.1 vs 12.1 months.

Absolute Effect

No defensible fixed-time absolute OS benefit is used because the coprimary analyses were reported primarily as medians and hazard ratios and neither endpoint met its prespecified criterion.

Safety: Grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs were 14% with durvalumab, 27% with durvalumab+tremelimumab, and 60% with chemotherapy. Serious treatment-related AEs were 9%, 23%, and 16%, respectively.

Deaths from study-drug toxicity: 2 (1%) durvalumab, 2 (1%) combination, and 1 (<1%) chemotherapy.

Interpretation & limitations

DANUBE did not meet either coprimary endpoint. The study nevertheless provides important randomized evidence about the limits of unselected first-line checkpoint blockade in urothelial carcinoma and the additional toxicity of CTLA-4 combination therapy.

Primary source

Powles T, et al. Lancet Oncol. 2020;21:1574–1588.
