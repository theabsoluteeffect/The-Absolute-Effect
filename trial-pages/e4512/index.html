Non-small-cell lung cancer · Adjuvant ALK-targeted therapy · Phase III

ECOG-ACRIN E4512 (ALCHEMIST-ALK): adjuvant crizotinib versus observation

A negative phase III trial testing whether adjuvant crizotinib improves disease-free survival after resection of ALK-positive early-stage NSCLC.

DesignRandomized, controlled phase III
PopulationResected ALK-positive NSCLC, tumour ≥4 cm or N+
Randomized166 (85 vs 81); central ALK+ ITT 153
Primary endpointDisease-free survival

Study design and eligibility

Patients with completely resected NSCLC measuring at least 4 cm or with nodal involvement, negative margins, ALK positivity by local or central testing, ECOG 0–1 and no neoadjuvant therapy were randomized 1:1, stratified by stage, prior radiotherapy and sex. Adjuvant chemotherapy was permitted but not mandatory. Of 166 enrolled patients, 153 (75 crizotinib; 78 control) had centrally confirmed ALK-positive tumours and formed the primary intention-to-treat population.

The trial initially used double-blind placebo control; after 40 patients had enrolled (19 assigned placebo), the control strategy changed to observation. Accrual stopped after FDA approval of adjuvant alectinib.

Intervention and comparator

Crizotinib

250 mg orally twice daily in 21-day cycles for up to 2 years. Dose reduction was permitted to 200 mg twice daily and then 250 mg once daily for toxicity.

Control

Observation after surgery, with placebo used in the initial blinded period for 18 patients before the protocol changed to observation.

Primary endpoint and efficacy

At median follow-up 65.7 months (IQR 37.5–85.6), median DFS was 74.6 months (95% CI 71.2 to not assessable) with crizotinib and 75.1 months (95% CI 64.7 to not assessable) with observation. HR 1.08 (95% CI 0.61–1.90; 90% CI 0.67–1.73), P=.80.

With 17 deaths across both groups, OS did not differ significantly (HR 0.49, 95% CI 0.17–1.43; 90% CI 0.20–1.21; P=.19). Recurrence occurred in 23/75 patients in the crizotinib group and 26/78 in the observation group (26/78).

NNT/NNH assessment

NNT/NNH not applicable. The trial did not demonstrate a benefit, and the reported median DFS and hazard ratio cannot be converted into an NNT. No comparable fixed-time absolute DFS rates were reported in the accessible primary abstract.

Safety, treatment burden and quality of life

In the crizotinib safety population, 46/79 (58%) experienced grade ≥3 adverse events of any attribution; one death was not considered treatment-related. Serious adverse events occurred in 21/79 (27%). The most frequent grade ≥3 events were diarrhoea (8/79, 10%), oedema (4/79, 5%) and hypertension (4/79, 5%). The observation arm did not provide a comparable systemic-treatment safety denominator; only 18 patients received placebo before the protocol changed to observation. Crizotinib median exposure was 13.5 months; 32/79 (41%) completed 2 years, 23/79 (29%) stopped because of adverse events, 13/79 (16%) because of patient preference, 46/79 (58%) had dose interruptions and 17/79 (22%) had dose reductions. Quality-of-life outcomes were not reported. Chest CT was performed every 6 months from randomization through year 5, then low-dose non-contrast CT annually from years 5–10.

Interpretation and current context

Adjuvant crizotinib did not prolong DFS and should not be substituted for alectinib. This is agent-specific evidence: the positive ALINA trial evaluated alectinib, not crizotinib. E4512 was underpowered after slow accrual and early closure, so it does not refute ALINA; it does show that adjuvant benefit should not be assumed to be a class effect across ALK inhibitors. Limitations

  • Only 166 of 168 planned patients were enrolled; the study was underpowered. The sample-size target was reduced from 378 to 168 and the primary endpoint changed from OS to DFS in 2018 because of slow accrual.
  • Primary endpoint changed from overall survival to DFS and the control strategy changed from placebo to observation during the trial.
  • Accrual stopped after approval of adjuvant alectinib; OS was immature with 17 deaths.
  • Only 153 patients had centrally confirmed ALK-positive tumours; crossover and protocol changes limit interpretation.
