PRO-B

Electronic symptom monitoring for patients receiving systemic anticancer treatment. This page separates the primary endpoint from secondary outcomes and distinguishes source-reported absolute rates from time-to-event estimates.

DesignRandomized clinical trial
Population909 patients with advanced cancer receiving systemic therapy randomized to electronic PRO monitoring or usual care.

Data maturityPrimary patient-reported endpoint at 12 weeks; secondary symptom and function trajectories were evaluated.

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 909 patients with advanced cancer receiving systemic therapy randomized to electronic PRO monitoring or usual care.

Intervention: Weekly electronic symptom and function questionnaires with automated alerts and protocol-defined clinician response.

Comparator: Usual oncology care without the structured ePRO alert pathway.

Primary endpoint: Change in EORTC QLQ-C30 global health status at 12 weeks.

Follow-up and maturity: Primary patient-reported endpoint at 12 weeks; secondary symptom and function trajectories were evaluated.

Registry: DRKS00024015. Randomization, analysis populations and endpoint definitions should be read from the linked primary report and protocol; no denominator is inferred where the abstract does not state one.

Key efficacy outcome

Between-group global health status difference at 12 weeks

ePRO monitoring advantage
3.1 QLQ-C30 points

Between-group difference in global health status was approximately 3.1 points (95% CI 0.8–5.4), statistically significant but below the commonly used 10-point threshold for a clinically important difference. Fatigue and physical-function domains showed favorable changes.

Time-to-event medians and hazard ratios are reported as such; they are not converted into NNT. Fixed-time absolute effects are shown separately only when a comparable endpoint and horizon are available.

Safety and absolute effect

No drug toxicity is attributable to the monitoring intervention; workload, alert fatigue and missed-response risk are implementation harms.

No NNT is calculated because NNT is not appropriate for a continuous patient-reported scale difference; no binary fixed-time event risk difference was reported.

Quality of life and treatment burden

Quality of life is the primary endpoint. The global health difference was small and below the usual minimal clinically important difference, despite favorable secondary symptom/function signals.

Interpretation and limitations

PRO-B supports structured symptom surveillance but the average global QoL effect is modest. Benefit depends on closing the loop from patient report to timely clinical action.

Open-label behavioral intervention, implementation dependence and short primary time horizon. Results may not generalize to settings without reliable digital access and staffed alert response.

Superiority, non-inferiority, surrogate response and mature survival are not interchangeable. The page preserves the primary endpoint hierarchy and does not infer a survival advantage from response or PFS alone.
