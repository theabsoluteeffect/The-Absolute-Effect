Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 924 patients with metastatic breast cancer were randomized; 909 were included in the primary analysis across 52 German breast-cancer centres. Patients were adults receiving systemic therapy and able to complete electronic questionnaires.

Intervention: Weekly electronic patient-reported symptom/function questionnaires with automated alerts to the clinical team and protocol-defined follow-up of concerning responses.

Comparator: Usual care with less frequent PRO collection and no equivalent alert-triggered intervention pathway.

Primary endpoint: Patient-reported fatigue at 6 months; physical function and global quality of life were secondary outcomes.

Follow-up and maturity: Primary 6-month PRO endpoint; follow-up continued to 12 months.

Registry: DRKS00024015. Arm denominators and endpoint definitions are retained from the primary report; no missing denominator is inferred.

Adjusted mean fatigue score at 6 months was 54.5 versus 59.9; mean difference −5.4 points (95% CI −6.6 to −4.1), P<0.001, exceeding the prespecified minimal clinically important difference of 3.3. Physical-function difference was +4.0 points (95% CI 2.6–5.4), exceeding its 3.2-point threshold. Global QoL difference was +0.8 points (95% CI 0.02–1.5), below its 2.9-point threshold.

Time-to-event medians and hazard ratios are not converted into NNT. Fixed-time effects are shown only when the primary source supplies a comparable endpoint and horizon.

Safety and absolute effect

No pharmacologic toxicity is attributable to the intervention. Implementation risks include alert burden, delayed response, digital-access inequity and workflow dependence; these should be considered separately from drug adverse events.

No NNT is calculated: the primary endpoints are continuous patient-reported scales.

Quality of life and treatment burden

The primary fatigue outcome and physical-function domain improved by clinically meaningful amounts. Global QoL did not reach its prespecified minimal clinically important difference.

Interpretation and limitations

Weekly ePRO monitoring linked to alert-based clinical action improved fatigue and physical functioning in metastatic breast cancer. The effect is a supportive-care/process intervention, not an anticancer drug effect.

Open-label behavioral intervention in a German metastatic breast-cancer population with digital access; effects depend on alert triage and timely clinician response. Do not generalize automatically to all advanced cancers.

Superiority, non-inferiority, surrogate response and mature survival are not interchangeable. The source hierarchy and analysis population are explicit.
