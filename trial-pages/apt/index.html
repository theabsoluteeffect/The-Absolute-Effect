APT — Adjuvant Paclitaxel and Trastuzumab

Clinical question: In small, node-negative HER2-positive early breast cancer, can adjuvant weekly paclitaxel plus trastuzumab provide durable disease control with less chemotherapy intensity?

Registry: NCT00542451 · One underlying study; aliases and follow-up publications consolidated.

Phase II · single armEvidence cut-off: 2 November 2026

Design, population and eligibility

Design

Prospective, multicentre, open-label, single-arm phase II study; no randomized comparator.

Sample size

410 enrolled; 406 initiated protocol therapy and formed the treated analysis population. Median age 55 years; 67% HR-positive.

Eligibility

HER2-positive invasive breast cancer, node-negative (six patients had nodal micrometastases), tumour ≤3 cm; this was a selected lower-risk population and not a randomized comparison.

Follow-up / maturity

Median 10.8 years (IQR 7.1–11.4) in the final analysis. Thirty-one iDFS events were observed.

Treatment and comparator

Intervention: Paclitaxel 80 mg/m² weekly for 12 weeks with trastuzumab (4 mg/kg loading then 2 mg/kg weekly during paclitaxel; subsequently trastuzumab 6 mg/kg every 3 weeks) to complete 1 year of anti-HER2 therapy.

Comparator: None. The single-arm design does not estimate comparative efficacy against observation or multi-agent chemotherapy.

Primary and secondary endpoints

Primary endpoint was 3-year invasive disease-free survival (iDFS). Long-term follow-up reported iDFS, recurrence-free interval (RFI), overall survival (OS) and breast cancer-specific survival (BCSS).

Key efficacy results

At 10.8 years, 10-year iDFS was 91.3% (95% CI 88.3–94.4), RFI 96.3% (95% CI 94.3–98.3), OS 94.3% (95% CI 91.8–96.8) and BCSS 98.8% (95% CI 97.6–100.0).

Among 31 iDFS events, six were ipsilateral locoregional recurrences, nine contralateral breast cancers, six distant recurrences and ten all-cause deaths. The 3-year iDFS in the original report was 98.7% (95% CI 97.6–99.8); 7-year DFS was 93% and 7-year RFI 97.5%.

Absolute effect

No NNT or NNH is estimable: APT was single-arm and had no randomized comparator. The 10-year outcome rates are prognostic estimates under the regimen, not absolute treatment effects.

Trial-specific toxicity and treatment burden

In the initial safety report, grade 3–4 neuropathy occurred in 4%, neutropenia in 4%, transaminitis in 3%, leukopenia in 2%, fatigue in 2% and hypersensitivity reactions in 2%. Reversible symptomatic grade 3 left-ventricular systolic dysfunction occurred in 2 patients (0.5%).

356/406 (88%) completed all 52 weeks; 24 stopped for protocol-specified toxicity and six for other toxicities. Paclitaxel-associated neuropathy and one year of trastuzumab monitoring are the principal treatment burdens.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

No randomized comparative QoL endpoint was reported. Patient-reported QoL benefit cannot be inferred from the single-arm efficacy results.

Subgroups and applicability

Most patients had HR-positive disease (67%). Exploratory HER2DX analyses associated a higher continuous risk score with worse iDFS (HR per 10-unit increment 1.24, 95% CI 1.00–1.52; p=0.047) and RFI (HR 1.45, 95% CI 1.09–1.93; p=0.011); this is prognostic exploration, not a validated treatment-selection interaction.

Current-practice context · reviewed 2 November 2026

The paclitaxel–trastuzumab approach remains a commonly used de-escalated adjuvant option for selected small node-negative HER2-positive tumours. Tumour size, nodal status, comorbidity and individual recurrence risk matter; the single-arm study does not establish equivalence to more intensive regimens in higher-risk disease.

Limitations and critical interpretation

Single-arm phase II design, modest cohort size, few breast-cancer events and no control group limit causal inference. iDFS includes events not necessarily attributable to the index breast cancer. The 10-year estimates should not be interpreted as a comparative absolute benefit.

Bottom line: APT supports a tolerable, less intensive adjuvant option for carefully selected small node-negative HER2-positive cancers, with excellent long-term breast cancer-specific outcomes; it does not prove non-inferiority to multi-agent chemotherapy.
