Does adding nivolumab to platinum-doublet chemotherapy before surgery improve outcomes in resectable NSCLC?

358Randomized patients
179 vs 179Nivolumab + chemotherapy vs chemotherapy

Population and treatment

Patients with resectable stage IB–IIIA NSCLC (AJCC 7th edition). Three cycles of neoadjuvant nivolumab plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy versus chemotherapy alone, followed by surgery. No adjuvant nivolumab was mandated by the trial design.

Primary endpoints: event-free survival (EFS) and pathological complete response (pCR). Long-term report: planned overall-survival analysis.

Efficacy

Overall survival · 5 years

At median follow-up 68.4 months, 5-year OS was 65.4% vs 55.0%; HR 0.72 (95% CI 0.523–0.998; P=.048).

Pathological complete response

pCR was 24.0% vs 2.2% (estimated treatment difference 21.6 percentage points; P<.0001). Approximate NNT for one additional pCR: 5.

NNT values are reciprocals of reported fixed-time absolute differences (or pCR rate difference), rounded up. They are endpoint-specific and should not be interchanged.

Toxicity and treatment burden

Grade 3–4 treatment-related adverse events were 33.5% vs 36.9% in the initial report. Treatment discontinuation due to treatment-related AEs was 10.2% vs 9.7%. Definitive surgery was performed in approximately 83% vs 75%.

No excess-harm NNH is indicated by these reported aggregate grade 3–4 treatment-related AE rates. The 5-year OS report reported no new safety signals.

Do not conflate treatment-related grade 3–4 AEs from the initial report with all-cause AE categories from regulatory safety tables. Detailed toxicity and perioperative complications should be cited to the relevant source table.

Interpretation

Three cycles of neoadjuvant nivolumab plus platinum-doublet chemotherapy significantly improved pCR and EFS, with a statistically significant 5-year OS benefit in the planned long-term analysis. The evidence supports the neoadjuvant chemoimmunotherapy strategy in eligible resectable NSCLC.
