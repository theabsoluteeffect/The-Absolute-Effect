Large B-cell lymphoma · Haemato-oncology / cellular therapy · Phase III

ZUMA-7

Second-line axicabtagene ciloleucel versus salvage chemoimmunotherapy followed by autologous transplantation for primary refractory or early-relapsed large B-cell lymphoma. Primary publication: 2022; prespecified OS follow-up: 2023. Evidence review: 24 November 2026.

DesignInternational, open-label, randomized phase III trial; 1:1 allocation.
Population359 adults with large B-cell lymphoma refractory to first-line therapy or relapsing within 12 months; 180 axi-cel and 179 standard care.
Primary endpointEvent-free survival by blinded central review.
Data maturityPrimary EFS analysis median follow-up 24.9 months; prespecified OS analysis median follow-up 47.2 months.

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility: Adults with histologically confirmed large B-cell lymphoma that was refractory to first-line chemoimmunotherapy or relapsed within 12 months after completing first-line therapy. Patients with later relapse were not the population tested.

Randomization: 359 patients were randomized 1:1 to axi-cel (n=180) or standard care (n=179). Randomization was stratified by response to first-line therapy and second-line age-adjusted International Prognostic Index.

Intervention: Axicabtagene ciloleucel as a single infusion at a target dose of 2×10⁶ anti-CD19 CAR-T cells/kg after lymphodepletion with fludarabine 30 mg/m²/day and cyclophosphamide 500 mg/m²/day on days −5 to −3. Optional bridging glucocorticoids were permitted; bridging chemotherapy was not.

Comparator: Two or three cycles of protocol-specified investigator-selected salvage chemoimmunotherapy (R-ICE, R-DHAP/R-DHAX, R-ESHAP or R-GDP). Patients with complete or partial response proceeded to high-dose chemotherapy and autologous stem-cell transplantation.

Primary endpoint: EFS by blinded central review, defined as progression, initiation of new lymphoma therapy or death from any cause. OS and response were key secondary outcomes.

Primary EFS: At median follow-up 24.9 months, median EFS was 8.3 versus 2.0 months; HR 0.40 (95% CI 0.31–0.51; P<0.001). EFS at 24 months was 41% versus 16%.

Response: Overall response was 83% versus 50%, with complete response in 65% versus 32%.

Overall survival: At median follow-up 47.2 months, median OS was not reached with axi-cel and was 31.1 months with standard care. Four-year OS was 54.6% versus 46.0%; HR 0.73 (95% CI 0.54–0.98; P=0.03). The standard-care arm had substantial subsequent off-protocol cellular therapy, including axi-cel.

Safety and treatment burden

In the primary analysis, grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 91% with axi-cel and 83% with standard care. Grade ≥3 cytokine-release syndrome occurred in 6% and grade ≥3 neurologic events in 21% of axi-cel-treated patients. These are distinct toxicity categories and should not be added together. In the prespecified OS follow-up, no new treatment-related deaths had occurred since the primary EFS analysis; grade ≥3 infections were reported in 16.5% versus 11.9% in the safety populations.

Validated quality-of-life results were not reported in the primary or OS follow-up publications. CAR-T treatment requires specialist centre access, lymphodepletion, monitoring for CRS/ICANS, and management of prolonged cytopenias and hypogammaglobulinaemia.

Interpretation and limitations

ZUMA-7 established a survival advantage for second-line axi-cel in primary refractory or early-relapsed large B-cell lymphoma. The result should not be extrapolated to relapse more than 12 months after first-line treatment. The comparator pathway depended on chemosensitivity and transplant eligibility; many patients in the standard-care arm did not reach transplantation. Subsequent cellular therapy in the standard-care group may attenuate the intention-to-treat OS contrast.

Current-practice context · reviewed 24 November 2026

For primary refractory disease or relapse within 12 months, second-line CAR-T is an evidence-supported pathway. For later relapse, salvage chemoimmunotherapy and autologous transplantation remain a distinct decision pathway. The current full NCCN B-cell lymphoma guideline/reference list was not accessible for exact mapping; no NCCN version, section, reference number or recommendation category is claimed.
