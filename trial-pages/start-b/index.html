The Absolute Effect / Evidence / Breast Cancer

Breast Cancer · Radiation · Stage I · Adjuvant / Postoperative

START-B

Clinical question: After breast surgery, does 40 Gy in 15 fractions provide long-term local control and late effects comparable to 50 Gy in 25 fractions?

Registry / identifier: ISRCTN59368779 · One underlying study; aliases and follow-up publications consolidated.

Phase III · randomizedEvidence cut-off: 8 November 2026

Design, population and eligibility

Design

UK multicentre randomized trial comparing conventional and hypofractionated whole-breast radiotherapy after surgery.

Sample size

2,215 women: 1,105 assigned 50 Gy/25 fractions and 1,110 assigned 40 Gy/15 fractions.

Eligibility

Women with early breast cancer pT1–3a pN0–1 M0 after primary surgery; immediate reconstruction was excluded.

Follow-up / maturity

Median follow-up 9.9 years; 10-year locoregional relapse and late normal-tissue outcomes reported.

Treatment and comparator

Intervention: 40 Gy in 15 fractions of 2.67 Gy over 3 weeks.

Comparator: 50 Gy in 25 fractions of 2 Gy over 5 weeks.

Primary endpoint: Local-regional tumour relapse and late normal-tissue effects.

Key efficacy outcomes

10-year locoregional relapse: 4.3% with 40 Gy/15 vs 5.5% with 50 Gy/25 HR 0.77 (95% CI 0.51–1.16); p=0.21.

Late effects: Less breast shrinkage, telangiectasia and oedema with 40 Gy/15 Reported in long-term follow-up.

The absolute 10-year locoregional relapse difference was −1.2 percentage points (40 Gy/15 minus 50 Gy/25). The HR confidence interval crossed 1 and p=0.21. The trial did not establish a formal non-inferiority claim using a prespecified margin in the 10-year comparison.

NNT / NNH and absolute benefit–harm balance

No NNT is presented: the observed recurrence difference was not statistically significant and the trial's primary inference is long-term comparative effectiveness, not superiority.

No NNH is calculated. Late breast effects were less frequent with 40 Gy/15, but the page does not invent absolute rates where the primary summary does not provide them.

Toxicity, grade 3–5 harms and treatment burden

Long-term assessments found significantly less breast shrinkage, telangiectasia and breast oedema with 40 Gy/15. The source abstract does not provide grade 3–5 toxicity rates; these are not inferred.

15 fractions over 3 weeks rather than 25 fractions over 5 weeks: ten fewer treatment visits and a shorter course. No concurrent chemotherapy was part of the radiotherapy comparison.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

The START programme assessed quality of life; the ten-year report emphasizes late normal-tissue effects. A distinct long-term QoL advantage is not claimed here.

Subgroups and applicability

The trial included pT1–3a pN0–1 M0 disease. It is foundational for moderate hypofractionation; individual decisions still depend on anatomy, reconstruction, nodal targets and contemporary planning.

Critical appraisal and limitations

The trial compared two fractionation schedules in a historical treatment era. No superiority was demonstrated; absence of a significant difference is not proof of equivalence or non-inferiority.

Clinical interpretation

40 Gy in 15 fractions is a landmark moderate-hypofractionation regimen with durable local control and a favourable late-effect profile. Its practical advantage is reduced treatment burden; do not overstate the p=0.21 result as a formal NI test.
