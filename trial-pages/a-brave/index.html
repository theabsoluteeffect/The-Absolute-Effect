The Absolute Effect / Evidence / Breast Cancer

Breast Cancer · Medical · Stage II–III · Adjuvant / Postoperative

A-BRAVE

Clinical question: Does one year of adjuvant avelumab improve DFS after standard curative treatment in high-risk early TNBC?

Registry: NCT02926196 · One underlying study; follow-up publications consolidated.

Phase III · randomized · Evidence cut-off: 8 November 2026

Design, population and eligibility

Design

Investigator-initiated, multicentre randomized phase III study of one year of adjuvant avelumab versus observation in high-risk early TNBC.

Sample size

466 randomized: 383 in residual-disease post-neoadjuvant stratum B and 83 in high-risk primary-surgery stratum A.

Eligibility

High-risk early TNBC after curative surgery and systemic chemotherapy; either residual invasive disease after neoadjuvant chemotherapy or high pathological nodal/tumour risk after primary surgery and adjuvant chemotherapy.

Follow-up / maturity

Median follow-up 52.1 months; primary DFS event target reached in February 2024; published 2025.

Treatment and comparator

Intervention: Avelumab 10 mg/kg IV every 2 weeks for 52 weeks.

Comparator: Observation after completion of standard surgery and (neo)adjuvant chemotherapy.

Primary endpoint: Co-primary DFS in the intention-to-treat population and post-neoadjuvant residual-disease stratum B.

Key efficacy outcomes

3-year DFS, ITT: 68.3% avelumab vs 63.2% control HR 0.81 (95% CI 0.61–1.09); p=0.172.

3-year DFS, stratum B: 66.9% vs 60.7% HR 0.80 (95% CI 0.58–1.10); p=0.170.

3-year OS: 84.8% vs 76.3% HR 0.66 (95% CI 0.45–0.97); descriptive secondary analysis.

Neither co-primary DFS endpoint was met. The OS signal was a descriptive secondary analysis and should not be described as confirmatory superiority. PD-L1 status was prognostic but not predictive for DFS benefit (interaction p=0.155).

NNT / NNH

No NNT: the co-primary DFS endpoints were negative (p=0.172 ITT; p=0.170 stratum B). The descriptive OS difference is not a confirmatory primary endpoint and should not be used to calculate a definitive NNT.

No NNH is calculated from the available primary abstract. Grade-specific immune-related toxicity and discontinuation rates require full safety-table extraction; they are not inferred.

Toxicity, grade 3–5 harms and treatment burden

Avelumab is associated with immune-mediated adverse events, infusion reactions and potential endocrinopathies. The abstracted primary report does not provide a complete grade 3–5 event table; the page deliberately does not fabricate rates.

Avelumab every two weeks for 52 weeks creates repeated infusion visits and immune-toxicity surveillance after completion of curative treatment.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

No established QoL advantage is demonstrated in the primary DFS report. Treatment burden and immune-related toxicity must be weighed against the absence of proven DFS benefit.

Subgroups and applicability

Stratum B was the larger post-neoadjuvant residual-disease group. PD-L1 was prognostic but not predictive for DFS benefit in the primary report. A 2026 TIL correlative publication is exploratory and does not constitute a new trial or validated selection biomarker.

Critical appraisal and limitations

Negative co-primary DFS endpoints; OS is descriptive; relatively modest sample size; biomarker analyses are exploratory. Avelumab is not interchangeable with perioperative pembrolizumab regimens.

Clinical interpretation

A-BRAVE did not demonstrate a DFS benefit from one year of adjuvant avelumab after curative treatment of high-risk TNBC. The descriptive OS signal merits follow-up but does not override the negative primary endpoints.

Contemporary-practice note (8 November 2026): Historical trial results are separated from current practice. Comprehensive guideline-level freshness review remains pending for this batch; use current disease-specific guidance and multidisciplinary context.
