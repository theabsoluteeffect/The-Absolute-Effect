EORTC 22881/10882 (Boost versus No Boost)

After complete lumpectomy and whole-breast irradiation for early breast cancer, does a 16 Gy tumour-bed boost improve long-term local control, and what is the fibrosis trade-off?

Registry: NCT02295033 · One underlying randomized study; aliases and follow-up reports consolidated.

Design, population and eligibility

Design

EORTC phase III randomized trial within breast-conserving therapy; 5,569 randomized overall.

Population

5,318 patients after microscopically complete lumpectomy randomized to no boost or 16 Gy boost; 251 incompletely excised cases were separately randomized between boost doses.

Eligibility

Early breast cancer treated with lumpectomy and whole-breast irradiation; analysis of the complete-excision cohort is the relevant 0 Gy versus 16 Gy comparison.

Maturity

Long-term follow-up to 20 years for local recurrence and late toxicity.

Treatment and comparator

Whole-breast irradiation 50 Gy in 25 fractions followed by a 16 Gy tumour-bed boost (8 fractions of 2 Gy).

Whole-breast irradiation 50 Gy in 25 fractions without a tumour-bed boost.

Primary endpoint: Local recurrence after breast-conserving treatment.

Other endpoints: OS, fibrosis, cosmesis and age-related local control.

Key efficacy results

At 20 years, cumulative ipsilateral breast tumour recurrence was 12.0% with boost versus 16.4% without boost; HR 0.65 (99% CI 0.52–0.81), P<0.0001.

At 20 years, severe fibrosis was 5.2% with boost versus 1.8% without boost.

Twenty-year OS was 59.7% with boost versus 61.1% without boost; HR 1.05 (99% CI 0.92–1.19), P=0.323. The boost primarily improves local control, not survival.

The 10-year report showed local recurrence 6.2% with 16 Gy versus 10.2% without boost; severe fibrosis at 10 years 4.4% versus 1.6%.

Absolute effect

Absolute 20-year local recurrence reduction was 4.4 percentage points (16.4% to 12.0%); approximate NNT = 23 over 20 years. Severe fibrosis increased by 3.4 percentage points (1.8% to 5.2%); approximate NNH = 29 over 20 years. Both are fixed-horizon estimates from the same randomized cohort.

NNT: Approximately 23 at 20 years to prevent one local recurrence.

NNH: Approximately 29 at 20 years for one additional case of severe fibrosis.

Toxicity, harms and treatment burden

Severe fibrosis at 20 years: 5.2% with boost versus 1.8% without boost; absolute increase 3.4 points.

The boost adds 16 Gy in eight fractions, increasing treatment visits and acute/late tissue burden.

Grade 3–5 systemic toxicity and treatment discontinuation are not applicable as systemic treatment endpoints; the relevant late harm is breast fibrosis and cosmetic change.

Quality of life and patient-reported outcomes

The boost may reduce local recurrence but increases fibrosis and can affect breast appearance, firmness and patient satisfaction. The trial's primary evidence supports a local-control trade-off rather than a survival or global QoL gain.

Subgroups, maturity and limitations

The absolute benefit is larger in younger patients with higher baseline recurrence risk; the boost decision should be risk-adapted rather than automatic.

The complete-excision 5,318-patient cohort is distinct from the 251 patients with microscopically incomplete excision who were randomized to different boost doses.

Twenty-year results are mature; the late toxicity trade-off becomes more visible with long follow-up.

Clinical interpretation

A 16 Gy tumour-bed boost improves long-term local control after complete lumpectomy but does not improve overall survival and increases severe fibrosis. The absolute benefit is greatest in younger or higher-risk patients; omission may be reasonable in selected low-risk older patients.

Current-practice context · 1 November 2026 (full guideline freshness review pending): Use contemporary risk-adapted boost indications considering age, grade, margins, biology and expected life span. Modern hypofractionation and boost schedules differ from the historical regimen, so avoid assuming identical toxicity rates.
