PROFIT

Clinical question: In intermediate-risk localized prostate cancer, can 60 Gy in 20 fractions replace dose-escalated 78 Gy in 39 fractions?

Design: Phase III randomized non-inferiority trial; 1,206 men with intermediate-risk prostate cancer. 60 Gy/20 fractions over 4 weeks versus 78 Gy/39 fractions over 7.8 weeks. ADT was generally not used. Median follow-up 6 years.

Primary result

5-year biochemical/clinical failure-free survival was approximately 85% in both arms. HR 0.96 (90% CI 0.77–1.20). The result met the prespecified non-inferiority criterion.

78 Gy/39 fx ≈85%
60 Gy/20 fx ≈85%

Absolute difference ≈0 points. NNT: not applicable because the purpose was to establish non-inferiority, not superiority.

Toxicity

Acute and late grade ≥2 gastrointestinal and genitourinary toxicity were broadly similar between schedules. The principal advantage was treatment burden: 20 fractions over 4 weeks versus 39 fractions over almost 8 weeks.

Methodology

PROFIT deliberately targeted intermediate-risk disease and compared 60/20 with a higher conventional dose of 78/39. This differs from CHHiP, which included a broader localized population, compared against 74/37 and used short-course ADT in most patients.

CHHiP vs PROFIT

CHHiP: broader T1b–T3a population, 74/37 comparator, three-arm design, short-course ADT in most patients.
PROFIT: intermediate-risk population, 78/39 comparator, two-arm design, generally no ADT. Therefore the trials are complementary rather than interchangeable.

Our take

PROFIT strengthens the case that 60/20 is an efficient definitive schedule for appropriately selected intermediate-risk prostate cancer. Its key contribution is showing non-inferior disease control against a dose-escalated 78-Gy schedule without routine ADT. The absolute clinical “benefit” is not fewer failures; it is preservation of cancer control while approximately halving the number of treatment visits.
