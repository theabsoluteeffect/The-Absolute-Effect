Treatment burden
19 fewer fractions

20 fractions instead of 39 — approximately 49% fewer treatment visits and about 3.8 weeks less treatment time.

Toxicity

Acute and late grade ≥2 gastrointestinal and genitourinary toxicity were broadly similar between schedules. The major practical advantage was reduction in treatment burden.

Important: NNH is not calculated here because the available page-level data do not provide a defensible fixed-time absolute toxicity difference.

Methodology

PROFIT deliberately targeted intermediate-risk disease and compared 60/20 with a higher conventional dose of 78/39. ADT was generally not used. The prespecified objective was to establish non-inferior disease control.

Our take

PROFIT supports 60 Gy in 20 fractions as an efficient definitive schedule for appropriately selected intermediate-risk prostate cancer. Its key contribution is preservation of cancer control against a dose-escalated 78-Gy schedule without routine ADT. The patient-level advantage is substantially lower treatment burden, not a demonstrated survival benefit.

Limitations

The findings apply to the trial population and treatment era. Non-inferiority should not be interpreted as proof that the shorter schedule is superior. Toxicity comparisons should be interpreted using the trial's prespecified definitions and follow-up.
