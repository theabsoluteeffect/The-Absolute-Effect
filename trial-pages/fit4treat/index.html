Head and neck cancer · Supportive Care · Phase II

FIT4TREAT

Exercise and nutrition prehabilitation during curative chemoradiotherapy for head and neck cancer. This page separates the primary endpoint from secondary outcomes and distinguishes source-reported absolute rates from time-to-event estimates.

DesignRandomized phase II supportive-care trial
Population40 patients with head and neck cancer planned for definitive chemoradiotherapy randomized to supervised prehabilitation or usual care.
Primary endpointSix-minute walk distance at the protocol-defined 12-week assessment.
Data maturityFunctional and patient-reported outcomes at approximately 12 weeks; long-term survival was not a study endpoint.

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 40 patients with head and neck cancer planned for definitive chemoradiotherapy randomized to supervised prehabilitation or usual care.

Intervention: Supervised exercise and nutrition prehabilitation, approximately three sessions weekly during the protocol treatment window, with aerobic and resistance components.

Comparator: Usual supportive care without the structured supervised exercise programme.

Primary endpoint: Six-minute walk distance at the protocol-defined 12-week assessment.

Follow-up and maturity: Functional and patient-reported outcomes at approximately 12 weeks; long-term survival was not a study endpoint.

Registry: NCT05418842. Randomization, analysis populations and endpoint definitions should be read from the linked primary report and protocol; no denominator is inferred where the abstract does not state one.

Key efficacy outcome

Between-group six-minute walk distance advantage

Prehabilitation advantage
56 metres

The intervention improved six-minute walk distance by approximately 56 m relative to usual care at the reported assessment; functional and fatigue measures favored prehabilitation.

Time-to-event medians and hazard ratios are reported as such; they are not converted into NNT. Fixed-time absolute effects are shown separately only when a comparable endpoint and horizon are available.

Safety and absolute effect

No exercise-related serious adverse event was reported; treatment-related fatigue, mucositis and nutritional compromise remain relevant in this population.

No NNT is calculated because The primary endpoint is a continuous functional measure; a binary event risk difference and NNT are not appropriate.

Quality of life and treatment burden

Patient-reported fatigue and functional domains favored the intervention; small sample size limits precision.

Interpretation and limitations

FIT4TREAT supports structured prehabilitation during head-and-neck chemoradiotherapy, but larger multicentre studies are needed to establish durable functional and treatment-completion benefits.

Small phase II sample, single treatment context and implementation dependence. Exercise dose and adherence may differ across centres.

Superiority, non-inferiority, surrogate response and mature survival are not interchangeable. The page preserves the primary endpoint hierarchy and does not infer a survival advantage from response or PFS alone.
