First-line treatment of adult Philadelphia chromosome-positive acute lymphoblastic leukemia. This page separates the primary endpoint from secondary outcomes and distinguishes source-reported absolute rates from time-to-event estimates.

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 236 adults with newly diagnosed Ph-positive ALL randomized to a chemotherapy-free ponatinib/blinatumomab strategy or imatinib-based chemotherapy.

Intervention: Ponatinib 30 mg orally daily with blinatumomab continuous infusion at 28 μg/day in protocol-defined cycles; treatment was chemotherapy-sparing.

Comparator: Imatinib plus multiagent chemotherapy according to the study protocol.

Primary endpoint: Event-free survival.

Follow-up and maturity: 24-month EFS landmark reported; longer-term OS and relapse follow-up continue.

Registry: NCT04722848. Randomization, analysis populations and endpoint definitions should be read from the linked primary report and protocol; no denominator is inferred where the abstract does not state one.

Estimated 24-month EFS 86% versus 67%; HR 0.46, P<0.001. The 19-percentage-point absolute difference corresponds to an NNT of 6 at 24 months.

Time-to-event medians and hazard ratios are reported as such; they are not converted into NNT. Fixed-time absolute effects are shown separately only when a comparable endpoint and horizon are available.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade 3–4 toxicity was lower overall with the chemotherapy-free strategy, but ponatinib-associated arterial/vascular events, hypertension and blinatumomab-related neurologic toxicity remain important.

NNT 6Event-free survival · 24 months

86% versus 67%; absolute benefit 19 percentage points. Alive without protocol-defined event at 24 months (Kaplan–Meier estimate). Calculation: ceil(100 / (86 − 67)) = ceil(5.26) = 6.

Quality of life and treatment burden

A formal QoL advantage was not established in the primary abstract.

Interpretation and limitations

This is practice-changing randomized evidence supporting a potent TKI plus blinatumomab strategy in newly diagnosed adult Ph-positive ALL, subject to risk-adapted selection and guideline/regulatory mapping.

The trial is specific to Ph-positive ALL and requires attention to vascular risk, molecular response monitoring and subsequent therapy. Longer follow-up is needed for cure and late relapse.

Superiority, non-inferiority, surrogate response and mature survival are not interchangeable. The page preserves the primary endpoint hierarchy and does not infer a survival advantage from response or PFS alone.
