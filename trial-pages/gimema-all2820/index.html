GIMEMA ALL2820: ponatinib plus blinatumomab versus imatinib plus chemotherapy in adult Ph-positive ALL

A randomized phase III trial of a chemotherapy-free TKI–immunotherapy strategy in newly diagnosed adult Philadelphia chromosome-positive acute lymphoblastic leukaemia.

DesignOpen-label, randomized phase III, 2:1
PopulationNewly diagnosed adult Ph+ ALL
Randomized236 (158 vs 78)

Study design and population

GIMEMA ALL2820 was an international, open-label, randomized phase III study enrolling adults aged 18 years or older with newly diagnosed BCR::ABL1-positive acute lymphoblastic leukaemia. Patients were randomized 2:1 to ponatinib plus blinatumomab or imatinib plus chemotherapy. The intention-to-treat population comprised 236 patients: 158 in the intervention arm and 78 in the control arm. Median age was 57 and 55 years, respectively.

The primary endpoint was event-free survival (EFS). Events included failure to achieve complete molecular response, death from any cause, toxicity, or resistance, including development of an ABL1 mutation. Molecular response was assessed centrally using BCR::ABL1/ABL1 ratios.

Interventions

Ponatinib plus blinatumomab

  • Glucocorticoid prephase: 60 mg/m²/day for age ≤65 years or 40 mg/m²/day for age >65 years, from 6 days before treatment through day 31.
  • Ponatinib: 45 mg/day for age ≤65 years (30 mg/day for age >65 years) during induction through day 70; the protocol schematic reduces the dose to 30 mg/day after day 22 in the younger group.
  • At least 2 cycles of IV blinatumomab at 28 μg/day, up to 5 cycles, administered concurrently with ponatinib; ponatinib continued during consolidation and post-consolidation.
  • CNS prophylaxis used intrathecal methotrexate, cytarabine and methylprednisolone.

Imatinib plus chemotherapy

  • Age-adapted chemotherapy: 6 cycles for age ≤65 years and 4 cycles for age >65 years.
  • Imatinib 800 mg/day for age ≤65 years or 600 mg/day for age >65 years, continued until progression or unacceptable toxicity.
  • Protocol-specified crossover to ponatinib plus blinatumomab was permitted for persistent MRD or emergence of an ABL1 mutation.

Primary endpoint and efficacy

At a median follow-up of 23.7 months (range 0.1–48.5), estimated 24-month EFS was 86% (95% CI 80–92) with ponatinib–blinatumomab and 67% (95% CI 56–79) with imatinib–chemotherapy (P<.001). The publication did not report a hazard ratio for the primary endpoint in the main report.

Absolute benefit and NNT

Absolute EFS benefit at 24 months: 86% − 67% = 19.0 percentage points. NNT = 1/0.19 = 5.26, rounded up to 6 patients treated for one additional patient to be event-free at 24 months.

  • 24-month cumulative incidence of relapse: 5% (95% CI 1–8) versus 13% (95% CI 3–22).
  • 24-month disease-free survival: 91% (95% CI 86–96) versus 84% (95% CI 74–95).
  • Complete haematologic response by day 70: 149/158 (94%) versus 62/78 (79%).
  • 31 control-arm patients crossed over to ponatinib–blinatumomab; the crossover complicates interpretation of OS.

Safety, treatment burden and quality of life

Grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 53/158 (34%) in the ponatinib–blinatumomab arm and 37/78 (47%) in the imatinib–chemotherapy arm. The most frequent severe events with ponatinib–blinatumomab were infections, decline in general condition, cardiovascular events, neurologic/psychiatric disorders and hepatic disorders. Treatment discontinuation because of toxicity occurred in 5/158 (3%) versus 6/78 (8%).

Quality of life was a secondary endpoint, but patient-reported results were not reported in the primary publication. Secondary-endpoint confidence intervals were not adjusted for multiple testing.

Interpretation and limitations

Ponatinib plus blinatumomab improved 24-month EFS and molecular EFS compared with imatinib plus chemotherapy in this study. Follow-up remains short, OS is immature, 31 control patients crossed over, and secondary endpoint analyses were not multiplicity-adjusted. Longer follow-up is needed to establish durable survival and late toxicity outcomes.
