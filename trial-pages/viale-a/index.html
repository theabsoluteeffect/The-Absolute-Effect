VIALE-A

In newly diagnosed AML unsuitable for intensive induction, does venetoclax plus azacitidine improve survival over azacitidine alone?

One underlying study; aliases and follow-up reports consolidated. Primary discipline: Medical.

Population and eligibility

431 patients with newly diagnosed AML ineligible for intensive induction because of age or comorbidity.

Newly diagnosed AML unsuitable for intensive chemotherapy; not a comparison against intensive induction in fit patients.

Sample size

431 randomised/evaluable as reported.

Intervention

Venetoclax daily with ramp-up plus azacitidine 75 mg/m² days 1–7 of each 28-day cycle.

Comparator

Placebo plus azacitidine on the same schedule.

Endpoints and results

Primary OS; remission, response duration and safety secondary.

  • Median OS 14.7 vs 9.6 months; HR 0.66 (95% CI 0.52–0.85; p<0.001).
  • CR/CRi 66.4% vs 28.3%.
  • Grade 3–4 febrile neutropenia 42% vs 19%; serious AEs 83% vs 73%.
  • AEs led to permanent venetoclax discontinuation in 24%.

Toxicity, discontinuation and quality of life

Trial-specific harms: Prolonged cytopenias, febrile neutropenia and infection; grade 3–4 febrile neutropenia 42% vs 19%.

Treatment burden/discontinuation: Permanent venetoclax discontinuation due to AEs 24%; frequent marrow assessment, dose holds and cycle delays.

Quality of life: No numerical comparative QoL estimate asserted here.

Subgroups, maturity and limitations

Subgroups: Molecular risk and remission depth inform prognosis; population is specifically unfit for intensive induction.

Maturity: Phase III primary report 2020; later follow-up and molecular analyses exist.

Limitations: Control was azacitidine alone, not intensive chemotherapy. Cytopenia management and infection prevention are essential.

Clinical interpretation

VIALE-A established venetoclax plus azacitidine for AML patients unfit for intensive induction, improving OS/remission but increasing myelosuppression and management burden.

Use current regional guidelines, patient fitness, biomarkers, competing risks, alternatives and regulatory status before clinical application. Clinical freshness review and browser-rendered QA remain pending.
