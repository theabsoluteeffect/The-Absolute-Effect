KEYNOTE-057

For patients with BCG-unresponsive CIS who decline or are ineligible for cystectomy, can pembrolizumab provide bladder-preserving disease control?

Population
Cohort A included 96 evaluable patients with BCG-unresponsive high-risk NMIBC with carcinoma in situ, with or without papillary tumours, who declined or were ineligible for radical cystectomy.
Experimental treatment
Pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks for up to 24 months or until recurrence, progression or unacceptable toxicity.
Comparator
Single-arm phase II study; no randomized comparator.
Study design
Single-arm phase II study

Clinical complete response at 3 months was 41% (39/96); median duration of response was 16.2 months. Of responders, 46% maintained a response for at least 12 months.

Critical appraisal

Single-arm study; response is not equivalent to cure and recurrences remain common. Radical cystectomy remains the standard for surgically fit patients with BCG-unresponsive high-risk disease; bladder-preserving therapy requires close cystoscopic surveillance and shared decision-making.

Absolute-effect discipline: NNT/NNH is not calculated unless a defensible absolute difference and a common time horizon are available. Median survival differences and hazard ratios alone are not converted into NNT.

Current-practice context

Reviewed 2026-10-14; this is a dated evidence-context note, not a substitute for local guidelines or individual clinical judgement.

Pembrolizumab remains a bladder-preserving option for selected patients with BCG-unresponsive CIS who are ineligible for or decline cystectomy in the US. Newer bladder-preserving strategies and local approvals should be reviewed before treatment selection.
