Breast Cancer · Medical Oncology · ER-positive / HER2-negative · Advanced / metastatic · Post-CDK4/6 inhibitor

evERA Breast Cancer

Clinical question: After progression or recurrence following CDK4/6 inhibitor plus endocrine therapy, does giredestrant plus everolimus improve outcomes over standard endocrine therapy plus everolimus?

DesignPhase III RCTGlobal, open-label, 1:1 randomization
Randomized population373183 vs 190; 207 had ESR1-mutated tumours
Median follow-up18.4 / 18.7 moTreatment / control at 16 July 2025 cutoff

Population and eligibility

Women and men with ER-positive, HER2-negative, unresectable locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer not amenable to curative treatment. Patients had progressed after at least 6 months of CDK4/6 inhibitor plus endocrine therapy in the advanced/metastatic setting, or recurred during/within 12 months of adjuvant CDK4/6 inhibitor plus endocrine therapy (after at least 12 months of adjuvant endocrine therapy, including at least 6 months with a CDK4/6 inhibitor). If CDK4/6 inhibitor plus endocrine therapy was not the most recent treatment, progression after at least 4 months on the most recent endocrine therapy was required. Measurable disease by RECIST 1.1 or evaluable bone metastases was permitted.

Key exclusions included more than two prior endocrine lines in advanced disease, prior chemotherapy in the advanced/metastatic setting, or prior oral SERD treatment. Central ctDNA testing determined ESR1 mutation status. The trial deliberately enriched for ESR1-mutated disease (207/373; 55.5%).

Treatment and comparator

ExperimentalControl
Giredestrant 30 mg orally once daily + everolimus 10 mg orally once dailyInvestigator-choice endocrine therapy (exemestane initially; fulvestrant or tamoxifen permitted after protocol amendment) + everolimus 10 mg orally once daily

Both regimens continued until progression or unacceptable toxicity. Premenopausal/perimenopausal women and men received a GnRH agonist. Randomization was stratified by prior fulvestrant, ESR1 mutation status and visceral disease. Alcohol-free dexamethasone mouthwash was strongly recommended to prevent/treat everolimus-associated stomatitis.

Primary endpoint and efficacy

The primary endpoint was investigator-assessed PFS, tested hierarchically first in the ESR1-mutated population and then in all randomized patients. PFS was time from randomization to RECIST 1.1 progression or death. BICR-assessed PFS was a secondary assessment and was similar to investigator-assessed PFS.

ESR1-mutated · n=207 (102 vs 105)10.0 vs 5.5 moHR 0.38 (95% CI 0.27–0.54); P<0.001
Overall population · n=373 (183 vs 190)8.8 vs 5.5 moHR 0.56 (95% CI 0.44–0.71); P<0.001

Response

Among patients with measurable disease, confirmed ORR was 26.6% (95% CI 18.0–36.7) versus 13.8% (95% CI 7.6–22.5) in the ESR1-mutated population; median duration of response was 14.9 versus 7.3 months. In the overall population, ORR was 23.8% versus 11.7%, with median duration of response 12.7 versus 7.7 months.

Overall survival: immature interim analysis

At 18 months, estimated OS in the ESR1-mutated population was 71.5% versus 50.9% (59% maturity; HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.38–1.02). In the overall population it was 75.1% versus 62.0% (67% maturity; HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.47–1.00). Neither OS analysis crossed its prespecified significance boundary; these estimates are exploratory/interim, not evidence of established OS benefit.

Toxicity and treatment burden

Safety population: 182 versus 186 patients. Any-grade treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 98.9% versus 96.8%. Common events were stomatitis (47.3% vs 48.9%), diarrhoea (26.9% vs 22.6%) and anaemia (23.6% vs 21.0%).

Grade 3–4 stomatitis occurred in 2.7% versus 3.2% (no grade 4 or higher events); grade 3–4 anaemia in 6.0% versus 8.6%; grade 3–4 pneumonitis in 2.7% in each group. Serious adverse events occurred in 29.1% versus 23.1%. Fatal adverse events occurred in 2.7% in each group; one treatment-related fatal event was reported in each arm.

Everolimus discontinuation due to adverse events occurred in 17.0% versus 11.8%; discontinuation of giredestrant or standard endocrine therapy occurred in 8.2% versus 6.5%. Median treatment duration was 7.1 months for giredestrant and 6.0 months for everolimus versus 4.6 and 4.1 months, respectively, in the control arm. Bradycardia was uncommon (3.8% vs 0.5%), all grade 1 and resolved; no photopsia was reported.

Health-related quality-of-life and other endpoints were designated for future publications; quantitative QoL benefit is not reported in this primary analysis.

Absolute effect: NNT / NNH

No NNT or NNH assigned. The primary PFS results are reported as medians and hazard ratios, without a verified fixed-time PFS event-rate difference in the primary text. The 18-month OS estimates are immature secondary outcomes that did not cross prespecified significance boundaries. Neither median PFS nor a hazard ratio should be converted into an NNT.

Interpretation and limitations

  • Statistically significant PFS benefit was demonstrated in the prespecified ESR1-mutated population and in the overall population.
  • The overall cohort was enriched for ESR1-mutated tumours (55.5%); this exceeds the expected prevalence in an unselected post-CDK4/6 population.
  • In the exploratory ESR1-wild-type subgroup, median PFS was 5.7 versus 5.5 months (HR 0.84, 95% CI 0.59–1.18); a clear benefit was not established.
  • The trial was open-label and the primary endpoint was investigator-assessed, although BICR PFS was similar.
  • OS was immature and did not cross prespecified boundaries. The sponsor participated in trial design, data collection, analysis and manuscript preparation.
  • QoL and several secondary outcomes were not reported in this primary publication.

Current-practice context · reviewed 17 November 2026

On 16 November 2026, the US FDA approved giredestrant 30 mg orally once daily plus everolimus for adults with ER-positive, HER2-negative, ESR1-mutated locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer whose disease recurred during/after endocrine-based adjuvant therapy or progressed on at least one endocrine-based regimen in the metastatic setting. FoundationOne Liquid CDx was authorised as a companion diagnostic. This is a US regulatory decision; do not assume identical approval or reimbursement in other jurisdictions.

The approval is biomarker-restricted. The exploratory ESR1-wild-type subgroup did not show a clear PFS benefit; the trial should not be used to justify unselected giredestrant-everolimus treatment. The FDA summary reports the ESR1-mutated PFS HR 0.38 and median PFS 10.0 versus 5.5 months. Exact NCCN Breast Cancer recommendation category/reference mapping was not verified in this run; that follow-up remains open.
