Gynaecological Oncology · Cervical cancer · High-risk locally advanced disease

KEYNOTE-A18 / ENGOT-cx11 / GOG-3047

In newly diagnosed high-risk locally advanced cervical cancer, does adding pembrolizumab to definitive cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy improve progression-free and overall survival?

Design, population and eligibility

Phase 3, international, randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial. A total of 1,060 patients were randomised: 529 to pembrolizumab plus chemoradiotherapy and 531 to placebo plus chemoradiotherapy. Eligible patients had newly diagnosed high-risk locally advanced cervical cancer (FIGO 2014 stage IB2–IIB with positive nodes, or stage III–IVA), without prior definitive treatment; squamous, adenocarcinoma and adenosquamous histologies were represented.

Co-primary endpoints: progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). The PFS analysis had median follow-up 17.9 months; the OS analysis had median follow-up 29.9 months.

Treatment and comparator

Experimental: pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks for 5 cycles during concurrent chemoradiotherapy, followed by pembrolizumab 400 mg IV every 6 weeks for up to 15 additional cycles. Chemoradiotherapy comprised weekly cisplatin 40 mg/m² with external-beam radiotherapy and brachytherapy per protocol.

Comparator: matching placebo on the same schedule plus identical cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy.

Key efficacy outcomes

PFS at 24 months

Pembrolizumab + CRT
67.8%
Placebo + CRT
57.3%

24-month PFS was 67.8% versus 57.3%; HR 0.70 (95% CI 0.55–0.89), P=0.0020.

OS at 36 months

Pembrolizumab + CRT
82.6%
Placebo + CRT
74.8%

36-month OS was 82.6% versus 74.8%; HR 0.67 (95% CI 0.50–0.90), P=0.0040.

Absolute effect and NNT

Time-specific NNT 10 to prevent one PFS event by 24 months: 67.8% − 57.3% = 10.5 percentage points; 1/0.105 = 9.52, rounded up to 10.

Time-specific NNT 13 to prevent one death by 36 months: 82.6% − 74.8% = 7.8 percentage points; 1/0.078 = 12.82, rounded up to 13.

These are approximate time-specific NNTs derived from published Kaplan–Meier landmark rates, not from median survival or hazard ratios.

Toxicity, treatment burden and quality of life

Grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 413/528 patients (78%) versus 371/530 (70%). Immune-mediated adverse events occurred in 206/528 (39%) versus 90/530 (17%). The safety denominators differ slightly from the randomised efficacy populations. No fixed-time NNH is calculated from cumulative treatment-emergent toxicity. A quantitative patient-reported quality-of-life advantage was not reported in the cited primary efficacy publications.

Interpretation and limitations

Adding pembrolizumab improved both PFS and OS in this high-risk locally advanced population. The benefit must be balanced against higher grade ≥3 and immune-mediated toxicity, the duration of maintenance immunotherapy, and the need for careful selection and monitoring. The results do not automatically extend to lower-risk early cervical cancer or to patients unable to receive definitive cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy.
