Cervical Cancer · Medical · Locally Advanced

KEYNOTE-A18

Does pembrolizumab added to chemoradiotherapy and maintenance improve outcomes in high-risk locally advanced cervical cancer?

Population and treatment

1,060 adults with newly diagnosed high-risk locally advanced cervical cancer (FIGO 2014 stage IB2–IIB with node-positive disease, or stage III–IVA); 529 were assigned to pembrolizumab–chemoradiotherapy and 531 to placebo–chemoradiotherapy across 176 centres in 30 countries.

Intervention: pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks for 5 cycles with cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy (cisplatin 40 mg/m² weekly for 5 cycles, external-beam radiotherapy and brachytherapy), followed by pembrolizumab 400 mg IV every 6 weeks for 15 cycles. Comparator: matching placebo with the same chemoradiotherapy and maintenance schedule.

Primary endpoints: progression-free survival (investigator assessed per RECIST 1.1, with histopathological confirmation where applicable) and overall survival. The OS result below is the protocol-specified second interim analysis. Registry: NCT04221945.

Results

First interim analysis: median follow-up 17.9 months; 24-month PFS was 68% with pembrolizumab–chemoradiotherapy versus 57% with placebo–chemoradiotherapy (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.55–0.89; P=0.0020). Median PFS was not reached in either group.

Second interim OS analysis: data cutoff 8 January 2024; median follow-up 29.9 months. Median OS was not reached in either group; 36-month OS was 82.6% versus 74.8% (HR for death 0.67, 95% CI 0.50–0.90; P=0.0040).

Toxicity and treatment burden

At the second interim analysis, grade ≥3 adverse events occurred in 413/528 patients (78%) with pembrolizumab–chemoradiotherapy and 371/530 (70%) with placebo–chemoradiotherapy. Potentially immune-mediated adverse events occurred in 206/528 (39%) versus 90/530 (17%). Anaemia, decreased white-cell count and decreased neutrophil count were among the common grade ≥3 events.

Patient-reported global health status and physical functioning were broadly similar between groups. A conventional NNH is not calculated for the aggregate treatment-emergent AE comparison because it is a cumulative treatment-period measure rather than a fixed-time risk endpoint.

Interpretation and limitations

For the trial-defined high-risk locally advanced population, adding pembrolizumab to definitive cisplatin-based chemoradiotherapy and continuing it as maintenance improved PFS and subsequently met the protocol-specified OS objective. These results should not be extrapolated to lower-risk or otherwise ineligible cervical cancer populations.

The OS analysis remains an interim analysis with median follow-up under 3 years; late recurrence and longer-term toxicity require continued follow-up. Keep the first PFS and second OS data cutoffs distinct. Guideline-version freshness is tracked separately and is not claimed as verified in this run.
