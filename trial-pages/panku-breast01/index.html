Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 383 Chinese women randomized and treated: 190 to izalontamab brengitecan and 193 to physician-choice chemotherapy. Eligibility required progression after endocrine therapy, CDK4/6 inhibitor and one or two chemotherapy lines including taxane.

Intervention: Izalontamab brengitecan 2.5 mg/kg IV on days 1 and 8 every 3 weeks.

Comparator: Physician-choice chemotherapy: eribulin 1.4 mg/m², vinorelbine 25 mg/m² or gemcitabine 1000 mg/m² IV days 1 and 8 every 3 weeks; or capecitabine 1000–1250 mg/m² orally twice daily days 1–14 every 3 weeks.

Primary endpoint: PFS by blinded independent central review per RECIST 1.1 in the modified intention-to-treat population.

Follow-up and maturity: Median PFS follow-up 15.2 months (95% CI 13.9–16.8); OS immature.

Registry: NCT06343948. Arm denominators are given where available; missing values are not inferred.

Median PFS 8.2 months (95% CI 6.9–8.6) versus 4.0 months (2.9–4.3); HR 0.41 (95% CI 0.33–0.53), log-rank P<0.0001.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade ≥3 treatment-related AEs occurred in 162/190 (85%) versus 127/193 (66%). Haematologic toxicity predominated; 138/190 (73%) received platelet-elevating agents, 28/190 (15%) platelet transfusions and 18/190 (10%) red-cell transfusions. Two treatment-related deaths occurred in the ADC arm.

No NNT is calculated: Median PFS and HR are time-to-event measures; no common fixed-time absolute PFS rate was reported for NNT.

Quality of life and treatment burden

No definitive QoL advantage was reported; OS remains immature.

Interpretation and limitations

PANKU-Breast01 significantly improves PFS over physician-choice chemotherapy in heavily pretreated HR+/HER2− disease, without established OS benefit. The previous page's HR confidence interval and comparator details were corrected.

All participants were Chinese women; global generalizability and earlier-line use remain unproven. High hematologic toxicity and two treatment-related deaths are important.
