PANKU-Breast01

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 383 patients with HR-positive, HER2-negative unresectable locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer after endocrine therapy and chemotherapy; randomized to ADC or physician-choice chemotherapy.

Intervention: Izalontamab brengitecan 2.5 mg/kg intravenously on days 1 and 8 of each 21-day cycle.

Comparator: Investigator-choice single-agent chemotherapy, selected from protocol-permitted agents including eribulin, capecitabine, gemcitabine or vinorelbine.

Primary endpoint: Progression-free survival by blinded independent review.

Follow-up and maturity: Primary PFS analysis reported; overall survival remains immature.

Registry: NCT06343948. Randomization, analysis populations and endpoint definitions should be read from the linked primary report and protocol; no denominator is inferred where the abstract does not state one.

Key efficacy outcome

Median progression-free survival

Izalontamab brengitecan
8.2 months
Chemotherapy
4 months

Median PFS 8.2 versus 4.0 months; HR 0.41 (95% CI 0.32–0.52), P<0.0001. The magnitude of PFS benefit was consistent with a substantial improvement over chemotherapy; OS is not mature.

Time-to-event medians and hazard ratios are reported as such; they are not converted into NNT. Fixed-time absolute effects are shown separately only when a comparable endpoint and horizon are available.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade ≥3 treatment-related adverse events occurred in approximately 85% versus 66%; myelosuppression, neutropenia, anemia and gastrointestinal toxicity are important treatment burdens.

No NNT is calculated because Median PFS and HR are time-to-event measures; no verified common fixed-time PFS rate was used to calculate NNT.

Quality of life and treatment burden

No definitive quantitative QoL advantage was reported in the primary abstract.

Interpretation and limitations

This is a positive phase III result in heavily pretreated HR-positive, HER2-negative disease. The ADC result is not a direct comparison with other ADCs or endocrine-targeted combinations.

Open-label design and physician-choice chemotherapy introduce heterogeneity. Follow-up is insufficient for a mature OS conclusion; do not extrapolate the PFS result to untreated or endocrine-sensitive disease.

Superiority, non-inferiority, surrogate response and mature survival are not interchangeable. The page preserves the primary endpoint hierarchy and does not infer a survival advantage from response or PFS alone.
