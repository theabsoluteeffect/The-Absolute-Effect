In advanced hepatocellular carcinoma unsuitable for or progressing after locoregional therapy, does sorafenib improve survival compared with placebo?

Population
602 patients with advanced HCC not amenable to curative or locoregional therapy, predominantly Child-Pugh A and ECOG 0–2, without previous systemic treatment.
Sample size
602 randomized patients (299 sorafenib; 303 placebo).
Intervention vs comparator
Sorafenib 400 mg orally twice daily continuously until progression or unacceptable toxicity versus matching placebo.
Primary endpoint
Co-primary endpoints: overall survival and time to symptomatic progression; radiologic progression was secondary.

Absolute Effect

Median OS was 10.7 vs 7.9 months; HR 0.69 (95% CI 0.55–0.87), P<.001. One-year OS was 44% vs 33%.

Absolute effect: One-year OS improved by 11 percentage points (44% vs 33%). Radiologic progression occurred later: median 5.5 vs 2.8 months, P<.001.

NNT: 10, based on 1-year overall survival (absolute improvement 11 percentage points).

NNH: 13, based on grade 3 hand–foot skin reaction (8% vs <1%; using the reported <1% comparator rate gives an approximate NNH of about 13–15 rather than a falsely precise integer).

Follow-up: The trial was stopped early at the second planned interim analysis after 321 deaths because the prespecified survival boundary was crossed.

Clinical results and treatment burden

  • Intervention: Sorafenib 400 mg orally twice daily continuously until progression or unacceptable toxicity versus matching placebo.
  • Principal toxicity: Drug-related adverse events were predominantly grade 1–2 gastrointestinal, constitutional and dermatologic effects. Grade 3 diarrhea was 8% vs 2%, and grade 3 hand–foot skin reaction 8% vs <1%. Dose reduction occurred in 26% vs 7%; discontinuation due to adverse events was 38% vs 37%.
  • Other efficacy/recurrence findings: Symptomatic progression, one of the co-primary endpoints, was not significantly different (median 4.1 vs 4.9 months; P=.77). This distinction matters: the survival benefit was driven despite no significant improvement in that co-primary symptom endpoint.
  • Quality of life: QoL was not the primary endpoint; treatment burden was reflected by dose modifications and characteristic toxicities.

Eligibility and clinical context

602 patients with advanced HCC not amenable to curative or locoregional therapy, predominantly Child-Pugh A and ECOG 0–2, without previous systemic treatment.

Limitations and maturity: The population was highly selected for liver function and performance status, and sorafenib is no longer the contemporary first-line standard in many patients. The trial remains foundational for proving that systemic therapy can extend survival in advanced HCC.

Clinical interpretation

SHARP was the landmark proof that systemic therapy could prolong survival in advanced HCC, but the absolute gain was modest and accompanied by characteristic chronic treatment toxicity.

Primary / authoritative source

Llovet JM, et al. Sorafenib in Advanced Hepatocellular Carcinoma. N Engl J Med. 2008;359:378–390.
