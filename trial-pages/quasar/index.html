Colorectal Cancer · Medical

QUASAR

After apparently curative resection of colorectal cancer, does adjuvant fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy improve outcomes compared with observation?

Population
3,239 patients after curative resection; 91% had colon cancer and 71% had stage II disease.
Intervention vs comparator
Fluorouracil plus folinic acid versus observation.
Sample size
3,239
Primary endpoint
Overall survival

Reported outcome visualization

Key results

The trial estimated an absolute 5-year survival benefit of 3.6 percentage points (95% CI approximately 1.0–6.0) assuming 20% 5-year mortality without chemotherapy; recurrence was also reduced.

Absolute Effect

Estimated NNT ≈28 for one additional survivor at 5 years (1/0.036), conditional on the trial's stated baseline-risk assumption. The absolute gain varies with baseline recurrence risk.

NNT: ≈28, based on 5-year overall survival under the trial's stated baseline-risk assumption.

NNH: Not quantifiable from the verified fixed-time toxicity data used in this summary; see toxicity and treatment burden below.

Time-to-event interpretation: Hazard ratios and median survival are relative/time-to-event measures; they are not converted into NNT unless a fixed-time absolute event estimate is available.

Toxicity and treatment burden

Fluoropyrimidines can cause diarrhoea, mucositis, cytopenias and rare severe toxicity including cardiotoxicity; DPD deficiency is a critical modern safety consideration. NNH is not calculated without a comparable prespecified fixed-time toxicity endpoint.

Follow-up, limitations and maturity

Follow-up: Median follow-up approximately 5.5 years.

Mixed colorectal population and historical fluorouracil regimens; the absolute benefit is small and depends on baseline risk. The trial does not imply that every stage II patient should receive chemotherapy.

Clinical interpretation

Adjuvant fluoropyrimidine offers a small average survival gain after resection; decisions in stage II disease should be risk-adapted rather than based on relative effect alone.

Primary publication and sources

QUASAR Collaborative Group. Lancet. 2007;370:2020–2029.
