Gastrointestinal malignancies · Supportive Care · Phase II

ACT-GI

Persistent chemotherapy-induced thrombocytopenia. Source review 17 November 2026; primary and follow-up analyses are kept distinct.

DesignPhase II · primary report
Population47 adults with GI malignancies receiving chemotherapy and persistent CIT (platelets ≤85×10⁹/L on day 1 of a scheduled cycle despite adequate recovery time) were randomized 1:1: avatrombopag n=23, placebo n=24.
Primary endpointComposite successful correction of CIT and prevention of recurrence: platelets ≥100×10⁹/L during lead-in, no chemotherapy dose modification/delay, and platelets ≥100×10⁹/L at the end of the on-study cycle.
Follow-upPlanned interim analysis after 20 patients per arm completed the double-blind period; trial stopped early for overwhelming efficacy.

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 47 adults with GI malignancies receiving chemotherapy and persistent CIT (platelets ≤85×10⁹/L on day 1 of a scheduled cycle despite adequate recovery time) were randomized 1:1: avatrombopag n=23, placebo n=24.

Intervention: Avatrombopag 40 mg orally once daily. During lead-in, chemotherapy was held for up to 2 weeks; after platelet recovery to ≥100×10⁹/L, patients resumed one full-dose chemotherapy cycle while continuing study drug.

Comparator: Matching placebo with the same chemotherapy-hold and on-cycle protocol.

Primary endpoint: Composite successful correction of CIT and prevention of recurrence: platelets ≥100×10⁹/L during lead-in, no chemotherapy dose modification/delay, and platelets ≥100×10⁹/L at the end of the on-study cycle.

Follow-up and maturity: Planned interim analysis after 20 patients per arm completed the double-blind period; trial stopped early for overwhelming efficacy.

Registry: NCT05772546. The primary analysis population and denominators are stated explicitly.

Key efficacy outcome

Primary composite endpoint

Avatrombopag (16/23)
70 %
Placebo (4/24)
17 %

Primary endpoint achieved by 16/23 (70%; 95% CI 47–87) versus 4/24 (17%; 95% CI 5–37), Z=3.67, P<0.001. Platelet recovery during lead-in occurred in 83% versus 46%.

Safety and absolute effect

AEs occurred in 78% versus 46%; serious AEs 17% versus 0%. No serious AE was study-drug-related; no treatment-related AE led to death or study-drug discontinuation. Two clinically relevant bleeding events occurred in the avatrombopag arm, including an intracranial haemorrhage.

NNT 2

70% versus 17%; absolute benefit 53 percentage points at End of one on-study chemotherapy cycle. Platelet recovery ≥100×10⁹/L, no dose modification/delay, and no recurrent CIT at cycle end. Calculation: ceil(100 / (70 − 17)) = 2.

Quality of life and treatment burden

No QoL endpoint was reported.

Interpretation and limitations

ACT-GI provides randomized evidence that avatrombopag can correct persistent CIT and prevent recurrence during one subsequent chemotherapy cycle. The endpoint is a composite supportive-care outcome, not tumour response or survival.

Small phase II trial stopped early for efficacy; only one on-study chemotherapy cycle was tested. Thrombotic/bleeding risk and generalizability beyond GI malignancies require caution.
