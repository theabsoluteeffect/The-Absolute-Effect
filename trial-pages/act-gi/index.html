Gastrointestinal cancers · Supportive Care · Phase II

ACT-GI

Persistent chemotherapy-induced thrombocytopenia during gastrointestinal cancer treatment. This page separates the primary endpoint from secondary outcomes and distinguishes source-reported absolute rates from time-to-event estimates.

DesignRandomized, placebo-controlled phase II trial
Population47 patients with gastrointestinal malignancies and persistent chemotherapy-induced thrombocytopenia
Primary endpointPlatelet response during the lead-in phase, defined by protocol platelet recovery without rescue intervention.
Data maturityShort phase II response assessment across lead-in and chemotherapy on-cycle phases.

Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 47 patients with gastrointestinal malignancies and persistent chemotherapy-induced thrombocytopenia; 23 received avatrombopag and 24 placebo.

Intervention: Avatrombopag 40 mg orally daily during the lead-in period, with protocol-directed dose adjustment and an on-cycle course during chemotherapy; dose escalation was permitted according to platelet response.

Comparator: Matching placebo with protocol-defined chemotherapy management.

Primary endpoint: Platelet response during the lead-in phase, defined by protocol platelet recovery without rescue intervention.

Follow-up and maturity: Short phase II response assessment across lead-in and chemotherapy on-cycle phases.

Registry: NCT05772546. Randomization, analysis populations and endpoint definitions should be read from the linked primary report and protocol; no denominator is inferred where the abstract does not state one.

Key efficacy outcome

Platelet response

Platelet response was 70% versus 17% (P=0.001); absolute difference 53 percentage points. The result supports thrombopoietin-receptor agonist activity in persistent CIT but is based on a small randomized cohort.

Time-to-event medians and hazard ratios are reported as such; they are not converted into NNT. Fixed-time absolute effects are shown separately only when a comparable endpoint and horizon are available.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade ≥3 adverse events and bleeding/thrombotic events were monitored; no treatment-related death was reported in the primary abstract. Thromboembolic risk remains relevant for clinical use.

Quality of life and treatment burden

No validated patient-reported QoL benefit was reported.

Interpretation and limitations

ACT-GI provides randomized evidence for avatrombopag in persistent CIT in GI cancer. It does not yet establish routine use for all chemotherapy-associated thrombocytopenia.

Small phase II sample, short follow-up and a platelet-response endpoint rather than chemotherapy dose intensity or survival. The result should not be generalized to all tumour sites or all CIT phenotypes.

Superiority, non-inferiority, surrogate response and mature survival are not interchangeable. The page preserves the primary endpoint hierarchy and does not infer a survival advantage from response or PFS alone.
