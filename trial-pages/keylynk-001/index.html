Design, population and treatment

Eligibility and sample: 1367 patients randomized 1:1:1: pembrolizumab-olaparib n=455, pembrolizumab n=458 and control n=454. All had stage III–IV BRCA1/2 non-mutated epithelial ovarian, primary peritoneal or fallopian-tube cancer; prior PARP inhibitor or checkpoint inhibitor was excluded.

Intervention: All arms received five cycles of carboplatin plus paclitaxel (or docetaxel) with pembrolizumab 200 mg IV every 3 weeks or matched placebo. Maintenance: pembrolizumab 200 mg q3w for up to 35 cycles plus olaparib 300 mg orally twice daily for up to 2 years, or matched placebo. Bevacizumab was permitted at investigator discretion.

Comparator: Two comparators: pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy followed by pembrolizumab/placebo maintenance; and placebo plus chemotherapy followed by placebo maintenance.

Primary endpoint: PFS, tested hierarchically first in PD-L1 CPS ≥10 and then in the intention-to-treat population.

Follow-up and maturity: Final PFS analysis at median follow-up 49.6 months; OS data were not reported as a definitive mature benefit in this analysis.

Registry: NCT03740165. The primary analysis population and denominators are stated explicitly.

At final analysis, pembrolizumab-olaparib improved PFS versus control: HR 0.66 (95% CI 0.53–0.83) in CPS ≥10 and HR 0.71 (0.61–0.84) in ITT. At first interim analysis HRs were 0.63 and 0.68, respectively. Pembrolizumab alone was not significant in CPS ≥10 (HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.77–1.19; P=0.33); therefore formal ITT testing for this arm was not performed.

Safety and absolute effect

Grade ≥3 treatment-related AEs occurred in 297/452 (66%) with pembrolizumab-olaparib, 255/456 (56%) with pembrolizumab and 232/454 (51%) with control. Serious treatment-related AEs were 24%, 21% and 9%; treatment-related deaths occurred in 4, 1 and 0 patients, respectively. Neutropenia and anaemia were common.

No NNT is calculated: The verified abstract reports HRs and hierarchical PFS testing but does not provide a common fixed-time absolute PFS difference suitable for NNT.

Quality of life and treatment burden

No definitive QoL superiority was established in the abstract; maintenance duration and hematologic burden are important.

Interpretation and limitations

The PFS signal is driven by pembrolizumab plus olaparib maintenance after chemotherapy, not pembrolizumab alone. The previous staged page's two-arm/median-PFS simplification was inadequate and is corrected.

Three-arm hierarchical design; pembrolizumab alone did not improve PFS and cannot be interpreted as equivalent to the olaparib-containing arm. OS benefit is not established in this report.
