The Absolute Effect / Evidence / NSABP B-04
Breast Cancer · Surgical Oncology · Locoregional Disease

NSABP B-04

Clinical question

Does radical mastectomy improve long-term survival compared with less extensive breast and axillary surgery in operable breast cancer?

Trial context
Population
1,665 women with operable breast cancer; clinically node-negative and node-positive cohorts
Intervention
Total mastectomy without axillary dissection vs radical mastectomy
Key endpoint
25-year overall survival
Time horizon / follow-up
25 years
Absolute Effect

What changes for patients?

At 25 years, overall survival among clinically node-negative women was 26% after total mastectomy alone versus 25% after radical mastectomy; HR 1.03 (95% CI 0.87–1.23). There was no significant survival difference. Total mastectomy plus radiation also showed no survival advantage. The major difference was better locoregional control with radiation, not survival.

NNT: Not applicable; no significant survival advantage

Treatment burden / harm

Axillary morbidity

NNH: Not quantifiable from the primary endpoint

Relative effect

HR: 1.03 for total mastectomy vs radical mastectomy in node-negative patients

95% CI 0.87–1.23; P=.72

Clinical interpretation

Interpret the absolute difference together with the endpoint, time horizon, relative effect and limitations of the study design.
