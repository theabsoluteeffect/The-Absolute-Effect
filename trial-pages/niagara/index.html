Bladder Cancer · Medical Oncology

NIAGARA

In cisplatin-eligible operable MIBC, does perioperative durvalumab + gemcitabine/cisplatin improve EFS and OS?

Population
1,063 randomized cisplatin-eligible patients with operable muscle-invasive bladder cancer.
Treatment
Four cycles of neoadjuvant durvalumab + gemcitabine/cisplatin every 3 weeks → radical cystectomy → eight cycles adjuvant durvalumab every 4 weeks, versus neoadjuvant gemcitabine/cisplatin → cystectomy.
24-month EFS
67.8% vs 59.8%; absolute benefit 8.0 points; NNT 13.
24-month OS
82.2% vs 75.2%; absolute benefit 7.0 points; NNT 15; HR 0.75 (95% CI 0.59–0.93), P=.01.

Absolute Effect

EFS: HR 0.68 (95% CI 0.56–0.82), P<.001. EFS included progression, recurrence, failure to undergo cystectomy, or death.

pCR: The trial did not show a statistically significant difference in pathological complete response, despite significant EFS and OS improvement.

Surgery: Radical cystectomy was performed in 88.0% vs 83.2%.

Safety: Grade 3–4 treatment-related AEs were 40.6% vs 40.9%; treatment-related death 0.6% in each arm.

Interpretation & limitations

The benefit is a perioperative strategy effect, not simply a neoadjuvant immunotherapy effect, because durvalumab continued after cystectomy. The trial was open-label and limited to cisplatin-eligible patients.

Primary source

Powles T, et al. N Engl J Med. 2024;391:1773–1786.
