Early vs routine oral nutritional supplementation during concurrent chemoradiotherapy

Does starting oral nutritional supplementation at the beginning of concurrent chemoradiotherapy reduce clinically important weight loss and severe radiation-induced oral mucositis compared with waiting for inadequate oral intake?

Study design and population

Multicentre, open-label, 1:1 randomized clinical trial across six tertiary hospitals in China. Adults aged 18–70 years with newly diagnosed stage II–IVA nasopharyngeal carcinoma, planned concurrent chemoradiotherapy, and baseline PG-SGA score 0–8 were eligible. Patients already consuming less than 60% of estimated requirements for at least three days were excluded.

Of 236 randomized participants, the conference report's full analysis set included 216 participants (early ONS n=107; routine ONS n=109). The peer-reviewed JAMA Oncology publication metadata are verified; numerical efficacy and safety values below are taken from the earlier ESMO 2025 LBA103 conference report.

Intervention and comparator

Early ONS: oral nutritional supplement (Ensure, 55.8 g three times daily) started at the beginning of concurrent chemoradiotherapy, alongside individualized nutrition counselling.

Routine ONS: the same nutritional counselling, with ONS introduced only when oral intake fell below 60% of estimated requirements for three consecutive days.

The study evaluated timing of nutritional support, not a new anticancer regimen. CCRT was delivered according to institutional standards.

Primary endpoint and efficacy

Primary endpoint: more than 5% body-weight loss from baseline to completion of concurrent chemoradiotherapy. In the ESMO 2025 LBA103 full analysis set, this occurred in 69.8% with early ONS versus 88.8% with routine ONS (p=0.008). In the per-protocol set, rates were 40.0% versus 64.8% (p=0.03).

Severe mucositis and treatment delivery

Grade 3 or higher radiation-induced oral mucositis was reported in 10.9% with early ONS versus 28.0% with routine ONS (p=0.011). Radiotherapy interruptions and global health-related quality-of-life scores did not differ significantly in the conference report.

Safety and quality of life

The conference report identified less severe radiation-induced oral mucositis with early ONS. Other grade 3–5 adverse-event categories and exact event counts were not provided in the accessible abstract. EORTC QLQ-C30 outcomes and radiotherapy interruptions were not significantly different; numerical domain scores were not reported.

No NNH is calculated: the accessible conference report does not provide a comparable fixed-horizon excess-harm endpoint.

Clinical interpretation

Starting ONS when CCRT begins may reduce weight loss and severe mucositis in patients with nasopharyngeal carcinoma who are not already malnourished. The strategy is not a substitute for dietitian assessment, symptom control, or escalation to enteral feeding when clinically indicated. The trial does not establish a survival benefit.

Applicability: the result is most relevant to patients with adequate baseline intake who are beginning definitive CCRT. Patients with established malnutrition or sustained intake below 60% of requirements were not represented by the randomized comparison.

Limitations and evidence provenance

  • Open-label trial conducted in six Chinese tertiary centres; external validity in other health systems and nutritional practices is uncertain.
  • Short-term endpoints assessed through CCRT completion; no mature survival endpoint.
  • The numerical outcomes and supplement dose on this page are sourced to the ESMO 2025 LBA103 conference report (Annals of Oncology; DOI 10.1016/j.annonc.2025.09.120). The later JAMA Oncology article's title, DOI and PMID were verified, but its full text was not mined because the publisher notice explicitly restricts AI text/data mining.
  • QoL was reported only as no significant between-group difference in the accessible conference report; detailed scores and missing-data handling require full-text review.
