Trial of the Day · 2026-09-07

PROTEUS: Perioperative apalutamide in high-risk localized prostate cancer

New England Journal of Medicine · Perioperative Therapy · 2109 men with newly diagnosed high-risk localized or locally advanced prostate cancer undergoing radical prostatectomy with pelvic lymph-node dissection

What did it show? At median follow-up of 61.7 months, perioperative ADT plus apalutamide increased 5-year metastasis-free survival from 73.5% to 78.2%, an absolute improvement of 4.7 percentage points (HR 0.80, 95% CI 0.67–0.96; P=.02). Pathological complete response or minimal residual disease was 8.9% vs 1.0% (OR 10.17, 95% CI 5.27–19.64).

Critical clinical reviewPROTEUS provides randomized phase III evidence that six cycles of perioperative apalutamide added to ADT before and after prostatectomy improves metastasis-free survival in high-risk localized or locally advanced prostate cancer. The absolute 5-year metastasis-free survival gain was 4.7 percentage points, while grade 3–4 adverse events increased from 31.0% to 39.6%. The key clinical question is whether this modest absolute metastasis-free survival gain justifies substantially greater treatment intensity and toxicity for all patients meeting the high-risk definition.

How to read the evidence

Every entry should make the clinical consequence easier to see.

01 · What the trial reported

HR, RR, OR or another relative measure, exactly as reported.

02 · What changes for patients

Absolute difference at a defined time point or clinically meaningful unit.

03 · Put it in 100 patients

What does this mean if we treat 100 people? NNT/NNH when appropriate.

Statistical guardrail: an HR is not itself an absolute risk reduction. Survival differences and NNT must be tied to a time horizon and baseline risk; where appropriate, the database can later support RMST and other absolute measures.
